PollyPolymer Secures Over 500 Million RMB in Consecutive Funding Rounds to Scale 3D Printing for Embodied AI

Nanjixiong 南极熊3D打印
Source published 2026-11-24 00:00 SGT
Media report
AI

Chinese 3D printing enterprise PollyPolymer has completed its B2 and B+ rounds of equity financing, raising a combined total of over 500 million RMB. The company plans to use the fresh capital to advance high-performance material R&D, expand equipment production capacity, develop industrial applications for embodied AI, and build a global delivery network.

Expand summary

Established in 2017, PollyPolymer specializes in high-molecular elastomer materials and ultra-high-speed 3D printing mass manufacturing. According to the company, it has accumulated over 16,000 polymer material formulations and developed a proprietary HALS ultra-high-speed 3D printing technology platform that increases molding efficiency by 20 to 100 times compared to traditional methods.

In terms of commercial expansion, PollyPolymer has applied its technology from shoe manufacturing to the robotics sector. The company reports that it has delivered over 10 million terminal parts globally and launched dedicated industry solutions such as RoboFab for robotics and PollyAlign for foot customization. In the robotics field, it currently provides elastic components like bionic muscles, silent foot pads, and anti-interference shells to dozens of robotics firms including UBTECH, with cumulative deliveries reaching tens of thousands of sets.

Bambu Lab Releases P3C Desktop 3D Printer with Seven Tool Slots Priced Around 7,000 RMB

Nanjixiong 南极熊3D打印
Source published 2026-11-24 00:00 SGT
Media report

Bambu Lab has released its third-generation desktop 3D printer, the P3C, bringing the Swaptek seven-tool-head architecture from its flagship H3C down to a consumer price point. With prices starting at around 7,000 RMB after subsidies, the machine supports multi-material and multi-color printing, aiming to shift industry competition toward material freedom and automated tool changes.

Expand summary

According to reports from Nanji Xiong, Bambu Lab launched the P3C desktop 3D printer on November 24, 2026, integrating the Swaptek seven-tool-head architecture previously found only in the H3C flagship model into a more affordable device. The P3C standard version starts at 6,374 RMB after subsidies, while the seven-tool flash-cut version is priced at 7,207 RMB, and a multi-diameter filament version is available at 7,292 RMB.

Technically, the P3C features five pre-installed 0.4mm PrintTools and two open slots for custom additions. Each tool slot has independent feeding and power interfaces, allowing for material switches in about 6 seconds without the need for traditional retraction and re-feeding. Paired with the AMS 3P feeding system and an optional dual-slot 75-degree-Celsius drying module, the machine can handle up to 22 colors or materials simultaneously, including soft filaments like TPU via the FlexFeed extruder.

To showcase its practical application, Bambu Lab collaborated with creators to release a modular glasses model on MakerWorld, demonstrating how soft and hard materials can be printed in a single run. This unexpected launch puts immediate pressure on competing manufacturers in the desktop 3D printing market.

Why Simulation Agents Are Like Junior Engineers

Siemens Blog
Source published 2026-11-24 21:07 SGT
Official release
AI

According to Siemens Blog: AI simulation agents are moving past the novelty phase into practical engineering work. Instead of replacing human engineers, they act like junior staff who need clear acceptance criteria to ensure their results are valid.

Expand summary

According to Siemens Blog:

As AI simulation copilots become common, the central engineering challenge is no longer how to run a simulation, but how to validate the results. According to the author, simulation agents should be treated like junior engineers: they handle the tedious tool syntax and execution, while human engineers provide the domain knowledge, physics, and rigorous acceptance criteria.

To ensure reliability, the author proposes a four-layer validation stack for agents: numerical convergence, first-principles physics, plausibility checks, and real-world data comparison. While agents can automate the first three gates, comparing results against messy real-world data remains the ultimate challenge for both humans and AI.

Directly Rewriting Robot Brains to Fix Mistakes Without Retraining

Siemens Blog
Source published 2026-11-23 22:32 SGT
Official release
Robotics × AI

According to Siemens Blog: Siemens Research applied weight-space programming to robot foundation models, allowing an AI agent to fix recurring errors by directly editing specific neurons. Tested in simulation on tasks like hammering and grasping, this method improved overall success rates without collecting new data or retraining the network, though it occasionally introduced minor regressions.

Expand summary

According to Siemens Blog:

How Direct Weight Editing Works

When robots fail simple tasks, fixing them traditionally requires gathering new training data and spending hours on expensive retraining. Drawing on MIT's foundational work, Siemens researchers tested an alternative approach using Claude Opus 5.5 to inspect a robot foundation model's internal activations and directly rewrite targeted weights in near-silent neurons. This setup allowed the system to introduce corrective behaviors, such as nudging a missed hammer strike or lowering a hovering gripper, entirely without gradient updates or supervised fine-tuning.

Performance and Trade-offs

Across multiple simulation tasks, the targeted edits improved performance on unseen scenes. For instance, overall success on the hammer task rose from 56.5% to 70.5%, and a basket-grasping task improved from 67.7% to 84.4%, even on a model already fine-tuned for the job. However, the approach did not solve every failure, and successful edits sometimes caused regressions by breaking a small number of previously successful runs. Control tests using random or reversed edits confirmed that the performance gains stemmed specifically from the intended directional corrections.

Industry Outlook

Siemens Research emphasizes that this technique remains at an early, exploratory stage in simulation rather than a commercial product. If refined, direct weight manipulation could eventually offer industry a cost-effective complement to traditional retraining by bypassing data collection, while also improving overall model interpretability.
