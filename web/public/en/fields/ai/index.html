Source published 2026-11-17 07:22 SGT
Open source

Expand summary

Release Overview

vLLM v0.34.0 marks a significant milestone driven by 1,061 commits from 412 contributors. This release completely phases out Model Runner V1, making Model Runner V2 the exclusive GPU runner for maximum efficiency. It also introduces major speedups tailored for Blackwell and MI355X hardware.

Core Performance Improvements

Performance is a major focus in this version. Users can now experience up to 1.95× decode acceleration on GB200 systems, alongside a 1.6× throughput boost for models like GLM-5.3-Flash. Additionally, long-prompt Time-to-First-Token (TTFT) has been slashed, running 1.24 to 1.40 times faster.

The update adds seamless support for 10 new state-of-the-art models, covering text, speech, and vision modalities. Highlights include Mistral Large 4 (featuring FP8 and NVFP4 support), MiniMax-M3.1-Flash, NVIDIA Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe, and Liquid AI's LFM3 series.

Breaking Changes & Upgrades

Several important changes require attention before upgrading. The default block size is now 64 instead of 16, and huggingface_hub >= 2.0.0 is strictly required. Furthermore, the extensible KV cache now automatically sizes itself based on actually free memory after warmup, maximizing your hardware's potential.

Expand summary

Background

The U.S. Commerce Department is rolling out an interim rule designed to close loopholes in existing export restrictions. The framework also rescinds the previous administration's AI Diffusion Rule.

Industry Impact

This regulatory shift forces global cloud and data center operators to strictly monitor customer ownership structures and network access. It comes in the wake of heightened scrutiny over Chinese firms scrambling for overseas compute capacity and aims to block actors from leveraging U.S. advanced semiconductors for intelligence-related or military activities.

arXiv Computation and Language
Source published 2026-11-16 13:00 SGT
Preprint

Expand summary

The authors report in this preprint: Current evaluations of Large Language Models (LLMs) primarily report dataset-level performance scores, which conceal internal processing mechanisms and failure modes. To address this, the authors propose a task-centric framework that combines attention- and activation-based signals with representation-based measures. This approach characterizes benchmarks along a continuum from retrieval-dominant to reasoning-dominant. The authors evaluate 16 LLMs across 24 diverse benchmarks and perform internal-state analyses on representative models and tasks. The results show systematic differences in processing patterns between knowledge-centric and reasoning-centric tasks. The code has been made open source, providing a tool to support targeted benchmark selection and informed model choice.

Completing AI: Physical intelligence and the role of memory

SK hynix Newsroom
Source published 2026-11-17 07:58 SGT
Official release
Energy × Robotics

According to SK hynix Newsroom: As AI moves from datacenters into physical devices like robots and smart glasses, computing power alone is no longer enough. According to SK hynix and UIUC Professor Nam-sung Kim, data movement and storage have become the primary bottlenecks due to strict limits on battery, heat, and latency. Future AI performance depends heavily on advanced memory technologies like custom HBM, PIM, and CXL to process data closer to where it is generated.

Expand summary

According to SK hynix Newsroom:

The Shift to Physical AI

As AI expands into real-world devices such as autonomous vehicles and industrial drones, offloading all decisions to a datacenter is impractical due to latency and connectivity risks. These physical AI systems must operate under strict constraints including limited battery capacity, tight thermal budgets, and restricted physical space. Consequently, system-level integration must begin with memory capacity and data transfer paths rather than raw compute alone.

Algorithmic Changes Driving Memory Design

New software techniques are altering how hardware handles information. Methods like quantization and Engram reduce computational loads by using conditional memory lookup tables instead of repetitive neural network calculations. However, this shift increases the demand for memory capacity and bandwidth, making efficient data delivery crucial for real-time inference.

A Unified Memory Hierarchy

To address these physical constraints, SK hynix outlines a vision combining custom HBM, 3D-stacked DRAM, Processing-in-Memory (PIM), CXL, and High Bandwidth Flash (HBF). Rather than relying on isolated components, the semiconductor industry is moving toward a full-stack approach where memory architecture is co-designed to match specific workloads and form factors.

Reflect-Reasoning: Training Large Language Models to Reason About Alignment Principles

arXiv Machine Learning
Source published 2026-11-16 13:00 SGT
Preprint

The authors report in this preprint: Researchers introduce Reflect-Reasoning, a training method that teaches large language models how to reason about alignment principles rather than just memorizing fixed rules, improving conformance across out-of-distribution domains.

Expand summary

The authors report in this preprint: Researchers from an arXiv preprint propose Reflect-Reasoning, a training approach designed to address the limitation where most alignment techniques rely on fixed human preferences or static constitutional principles. While deployers often use inference-time system prompting or post-generation reflection, these methods remain bounded by the model's inherent reasoning capabilities. Reflect-Reasoning instead trains models to acquire a generalizable reasoning skill for handling alignment norms. Tested across five models, the authors report that the method outperforms standard system prompting and explicit prompting strategies, generalizes to out-of-distribution medical and financial principles, reduces rare but severe principle violations, and maintains resilience against jailbreaking attempts using adversarial constitutions without eroding prior safety engineering. As a preprint, the study requires independent replication and further code/data release verification.

Impact

This construction provides a candidate nonperturbative definition for the Standard Model at finite lattice spacing, moving past a long-standing roadblock. However, it remains primarily a principle rather than a practical tool, and future work is needed to test conditions like reflection positivity and refine it for realistic calculations.
