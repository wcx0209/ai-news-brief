Expand summary

Background

The U.S. Commerce Department is rolling out an interim rule designed to close loopholes in existing export restrictions. Previously, rules required licenses only for shipping chips directly to companies in embargoed countries like China, Russia, Iran, and North Korea. However, companies have increasingly turned to remote-access loopholes, using overseas data centers to train advanced models.

Additionally, the rule introduces new licensing requirements for semiconductor transfers to Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand, and Brazil due to a higher risk of diversion, though chips destined for U.S.-based companies in these nations are exempt. The framework also rescinds the previous administration's AI Diffusion Rule.

Industry Impact

This regulatory shift forces global cloud and data center operators to strictly monitor customer ownership structures and network access. It comes in the wake of heightened scrutiny over Chinese firms scrambling for overseas compute capacity and aims to block actors from leveraging U.S. advanced semiconductors for intelligence-related or military activities.

Tracing Knowledge and Reasoning in LLMs

arXiv Computation and Language
Source published 2026-11-16 13:00 SGT

Expand summary

The authors report in this preprint: Current evaluations of Large Language Models (LLMs) primarily report dataset-level performance scores, which conceal internal processing mechanisms and failure modes. To address this, the authors propose a task-centric framework that combines attention- and activation-based signals with representation-based measures. This approach characterizes benchmarks along a continuum from retrieval-dominant to reasoning-dominant. The authors evaluate 16 LLMs across 24 diverse benchmarks and perform internal-state analyses on representative models and tasks. The results show systematic differences in processing patterns between knowledge-centric and reasoning-centric tasks. The code has been made open source, providing a tool to support targeted benchmark selection and informed model choice.

Reflect-Reasoning: Training Large Language Models to Reason About Alignment Principles

arXiv Machine Learning
Source published 2026-11-16 13:00 SGT
Preprint

The authors report in this preprint: Researchers introduce Reflect-Reasoning, a training method that teaches large language models how to reason about alignment principles rather than just memorizing fixed rules, improving conformance across out-of-distribution domains.

Expand summary

The authors report in this preprint: Researchers from an arXiv preprint propose Reflect-Reasoning, a training approach designed to address the limitation where most alignment techniques rely on fixed human preferences or static constitutional principles. While deployers often use inference-time system prompting or post-generation reflection, these methods remain bounded by the model's inherent reasoning capabilities. Reflect-Reasoning instead trains models to acquire a generalizable reasoning skill for handling alignment norms. Tested across five models, the authors report that the method outperforms standard system prompting and explicit prompting strategies, generalizes to out-of-distribution medical and financial principles, reduces rare but severe principle violations, and maintains resilience against jailbreaking attempts using adversarial constitutions without eroding prior safety engineering. As a preprint, the study requires independent replication and further code/data release verification.

A step toward defining the Standard Model on a lattice

OpenAI News
Source published 2026-11-16 06:00 SGT
Official release

According to OpenAI News: A physicist and GPT-6 Astra proposed a candidate nonperturbative definition of the Standard Model on a spacetime lattice, addressing a fifty-year-old foundational problem. The collaboration used refinement across finer grids to solve a longstanding phase correction challenge, though expert dialogue and further work remain necessary to make the method practical for simulations.

Expand summary

According to OpenAI News:

Background

For over fifty years, the Standard Model of particle physics has successfully described elementary particles and three fundamental forces, but it has lacked a nonperturbative definition. While researchers use perturbation theory or lattice field theory for the strong force, extending the lattice approach to the full Standard Model has been blocked by the challenges of chiral theories, notably the fermion doubling problem and the complex phase alignment.

Details

Writing in an October preprint, the author and GPT-6 Astra developed a candidate lattice definition. They mapped force fields across a succession of finer grids to calculate the necessary phase corrections around loops. Astra helped turn this refinement idea into a working construction, showing that comparisons on finer grids converge and taking the lead in developing the detailed proof, which was reviewed with human lattice experts.

Impact

This construction provides a candidate nonperturbative definition for the Standard Model at finite lattice spacing, moving past a long-standing roadblock. However, it remains primarily a principle rather than a practical tool, and future work is needed to test conditions like reflection positivity and refine it for realistic calculations.

Goal Engineering: The Future of Designing AI Agents

LangChain Blog
Source published 2026-11-15 00:00 SGT
Official release

According to LangChain Blog: As AI agents evolve to tackle complex, open-ended goals, designing their scope becomes critical. Companies need to focus on goal engineering rather than just prompt engineering, ensuring that agents operate within a scope that makes it easy for humans to provide feedback. A case study of Actively AI shows that organizing agents around specific types of jobs rather than individual accounts allows for faster learning and better context sharing.

Expand summary

According to LangChain Blog:

Background and Challenges

As AI agents transition from simple text generation and tool calling to executing full, open-ended tasks, the way we design their scope matters immensely. Pointing agents in the wrong direction wastes valuable tokens. A major bottleneck in agent performance is a lack of the right context, especially tacit knowledge that lives only inside human heads and cannot be found in written documents.

The Role of Feedback and UX

When agents tackle subjective tasks alongside humans, they need constant feedback to learn brand tastes, strategic preferences, and nuanced behaviors. Building an intuitive and delightful UX for reviewing agent work is essential. Since the bottleneck for agent performance is learning from feedback, optimizing the ease and quality of feedback collection unlocks long-horizon capabilities.

Case Study: Actively AI

Actively AI initially designed sales agents on a per-account basis, giving each agent the goal of moving a single prospective customer forward. However, they found that salespeople suffered from constant context switching and struggled to share learnings across different accounts. In their second iteration, Actively shifted to "motions", where agents handle specific jobs to be done, such as outbounding or deal progression, across multiple accounts. This aligned the workflow with how humans naturally review and provide feedback, allowing agents to learn much more quickly while background agents gather objective account data.
