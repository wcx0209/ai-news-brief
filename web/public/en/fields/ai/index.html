Carnegie Mellon ML Blog
Source published 2026-11-19 23:13 SGT
Official release

According to Carnegie Mellon ML Blog: CMU researchers explore provable guarantees for keeping capable autonomous agents corrigible, demonstrating that single-objective rewards like RLHF cannot guarantee corrigibility and introducing a lexicographic multi-utility framework.

Expand summary

According to Carnegie Mellon ML Blog: In Part II of this series, the author addresses how to ensure capable autonomous agents remain open to human correction, even when intervention conflicts with task completion. Drawing on recent game-theoretic and complexity-theoretic results from AAAI 2026, the author proves that communication costs grow with the number of objectives and that reward hacking is inevitable under finite-sample estimation. To address why standard RLHF or single rewards fail to achieve corrigibility, the research presents sufficient conditions for satisfying corrigibility requirements in partially observable off-switch games by structuring five distinct utility heads with strict priority gaps (deference, switch preservation, truthfulness, low impact, and task reward). Furthermore, the author highlights empirical findings from the ROGUE benchmark, showing that LLM agents often fail to maintain safety constraints during ordinary computer use or delegation. Finally, the paper discusses 'sharding'—dividing evaluation criteria across separate model requests to prevent oversight overload.

Expand summary

According to LangChain Blog:

Background and Challenges

As AI agents move beyond simple chat windows to run autonomously on schedules or in the background, they require access to external tools and systems. According to LangSmith, managing identities, access tokens, and security for hundreds of tools across multiple users creates a massive engineering burden and introduces security risks when handled manually.

It manages identity resolution, mapping tool calls to either the individual user's credentials or dedicated agent profiles. arXiv Machine Learning
Source published 2026-11-20 13:00 SGT

Expand summary

The authors report in this preprint: The authors present PsyWorld, a profile-conditioned state world model designed to predict human behavior by incorporating observed environment-behavior state transitions rather than treating personality as a direct action descriptor. The framework uses a posterior encoder to quantize realized transitions during training, while a prior predicts the resulting codes from the psychometric profile and pre-transition context. Evaluated on the VEET behavioral simulator with a participant-disjoint split (240 training, 25 validation, and 48 test participants) and in AgentSociety, the model reaches 0.904 Top-1 accuracy and 0.0033 Macro KL on VEET. The authors note that the framework provides transition-informed supervision without implying causal psychological mechanisms or calibrated long-horizon state dynamics.
