OpenAI Urges Industry to Use Perplexity as a Shared Metric for AI Progress and Safety

OpenAI News
Source published 2026-11-22 08:00 SGT
Official release

According to OpenAI News: OpenAI is advocating for perplexity as a core metric to track artificial intelligence progress and help set shared safety standards across the industry. The company reveals its internal pretraining measurements, arguing that monitoring a model's ability to compress data is the best predictor of emerging capabilities like reasoning and mathematics.

Expand summary

According to OpenAI News:

OpenAI argues that modern AI is fundamentally built by training a data compressor or predictor on a massive corpus, rather than stitching together disparate techniques. Tracking progress on this compression metric, known as perplexity or cross-entropy loss, allows researchers to observe how capabilities emerge predictably as loss drops. For instance, GPT-2 achieved coherent paragraphs, GPT-3 enabled in-context learning, and newer models with lower loss unlock advanced math and cybersecurity skills.

The company shares its internal pretraining data to encourage transparency and establish shared safety standards among frontier developers, despite the competitive risk. Furthermore, OpenAI warns that as AI begins to accelerate its own algorithmic research, fundamental pretraining advancements could speed up dramatically by 2027. Consequently, the company proposes establishing common development limits based on perplexity until safety monitoring techniques catch up.

Dawa Technology's AI Video Strategy: Focus on Control Rather Than Rendering

GeekPark 极客公园
Source published 2026-11-22 10:03 SGT
Media report

Dawa Technology reports a cumulative financing of about 500 million RMB and adopts a control-over-rendering approach for AI video generation. By separating the control layer from the rendering layer and utilizing structured process data from real film sets, the company aims to reduce trial-and-error costs and enable real-time world models on consumer-grade hardware.

Expand summary

Dawa Technology, headquartered in Yongchuan, Chongqing, originated from a tech-oriented film studio operation and transitioned into AI models. The company recently completed its Pre-A and A round financings, totaling around 500 million RMB, and reports an annual ARR of about 100 million USD.

Addressing the high computation and trial-and-error costs of mainstream video generation models, Dawa proposes a 'control layer versus rendering layer' architecture. According to CTO Zhu Ziwei, the rendering layer acts like a camera to output pixels, while the control layer acts like a director, understanding physical spaces, actors, lighting, and camera movements. This setup is powered by approximately 180TB of structured process data collected from real film shoots, recording the entire production timeline rather than just final pixels.

To address computational limitations, CPO Li Zongxing notes that the company developed a sparse diffusion autoregressive architecture that skips unchanged areas in a scene, allowing real-time execution at 720P on consumer-grade single GPUs. Dawa's current offerings include FilmOS for professional offline generation and Scenes AI for real-time interactive world modeling, with future expansions targeted toward live-streaming and interactive gaming.

Banks are cracking down on AI agents and warning users about bot risks

WIRED AI
Source published 2026-11-22 19:00 SGT
Media report
Robotics

Major US financial institutions are banning third-party bots from accessing user accounts. They warn that if an AI agent makes a costly mistake, the financial burden will fall entirely on the customer.

Expand summary

As autonomous AI tools become more common for managing personal tasks, major US banks are drawing a hard line against third-party bots attempting to log into customer accounts. Financial institutions are actively blocking these automated tools due to security and operational concerns. Furthermore, banks have issued warnings stating that if an AI agent makes a costly error during a transaction or account management process, the customer will be held strictly responsible for the financial fallout.
