Training a Scalable AI Agent for Economic and Geopolitical Forecasting

Thinking Machines Lab
Source published 2026-11-24 08:00 SGT
Official release

According to Thinking Machines Lab: Mantic and Thinking Machines report training an AI forecasting agent by fine-tuning Inkling 2 Small on the Tinker platform. The resulting model matches the accuracy of Fable 5.2 (medium) while being 15 times cheaper per forecast. To overcome data scarcity, the team generated 45,000 synthetic questions, built a low-latency historical Wikipedia database, and designed a reward function combining real outcomes with reference forecasts to mitigate noise.

Expand summary

According to Thinking Machines Lab:

Mantic, in collaboration with Thinking Machines, details the creation of a scalable AI forecasting agent that handles both point-in-time research and probability distribution submissions in a single rollout. Built by post-training Inkling 2 Small on Tinker, the agent leverages asynchronous reinforcement learning via StableReinforce to scale across thousands of concurrent environments.

To address training bottlenecks, the developers tackled three major challenges. First, public forecasting platforms lack sufficient clean, high-stakes numerical questions, so they synthesized 45,000 quantitative questions from news using language models. Second, third-party APIs proved too slow for large-scale RL, prompting them to build dedicated low-latency databases, including a 1.32 TB point-in-time historical copy of Wikipedia. Third, because real-world forecasting rewards are stochastic, they introduced a blended reward function that mixes outcome log scores with expected log scores derived from frontier model reference ensembles.

Through training, the agent evolved from simple news-reading behavior into a quantitative analyst that runs dozens of tool calls, executes Python code, and models complex scenarios. The authors state this approach significantly extends the cost-accuracy frontier of automated forecasting.

Why traditional form OCR fails and how semantic extraction works

LlamaIndex Blog
Source published 2026-11-24 08:00 SGT
Official release

According to LlamaIndex Blog: Traditional optical character recognition often fails on forms because it relies on fixed positions and plain text, ignoring checkboxes, handwriting, and layout changes. According to LlamaIndex, shifting to a layout-aware parsing and schema-aligned extraction pipeline allows systems to interpret the meaning of fields rather than their pixel coordinates, maintaining high accuracy even when a form's layout is revised.

Expand summary

According to LlamaIndex Blog:

Forms look simple because they feature labels and fixed layouts, but they are actually deceptive. Standard OCR merely converts image pixels into character codes, discarding critical structural relationships like label-value pairs, checkboxes, and handwriting. When an organization revises a form by adding a single line, coordinate-based templates break silently because every zone shifts down, feeding incorrect values to downstream systems.

To solve this, modern production workflows require a multi-layered approach. First, layout-aware parsing uses vision-language models to understand the structural components of a page, preserving table formats and checkbox states. Next, schema-aligned extraction maps the content to typed records using semantic instructions rather than pixel offsets. Finally, built-in validation rules, internal arithmetic checks, and confidence scoring ensure that anomalies are routed to humans for review, making automated form processing robust against layout changes.

Irreducible Governance Structure for Autonomous Agent Systems: Fair Allocation, Strategy-Proofness, and Multi-Scale Composition

arXiv Artificial Intelligence
Source published 2026-11-25 13:00 SGT
Preprint

The authors report in this preprint: Researchers formalize multi-agent governance as a resource allocation problem, proving that fairness, efficiency, and strategy-proofness involve fundamental trade-offs that require a four-dimensional architecture.

Expand summary

The authors report in this preprint: This paper investigates the governance of multi-agent systems, arguing that resource allocation among competing agents is a fundamental structural component rather than an auxiliary concern. The authors formalize the problem through impossibility results, demonstrating unavoidable trade-offs such as Sybil amplification and the incompatibility of key allocation properties. They establish that these constraints propagate across the system and interact with execution atomicity, state enforcement, and behavioral drift monitoring, leading to a four-dimensional governance framework. The main result is an irreducibility theorem showing that, under finite observability, governance architectures cannot collapse these dimensions without sacrificing correctness or stability. The findings are supported by analytical proofs and empirical ablations, indicating that removing or approximating any governance layer results in systematic failure modes.

Local subspace encoding for spectral prediction: from TabPFN to operator-adaptive calibration

arXiv Machine Learning
Source published 2026-11-25 13:00 SGT
Preprint

The authors report in this preprint: Researchers evaluate whether Local Vibrational Subspace Encoding (LVSE) and Global Compositional Unmixing (GCU) improve TabPFN and classical calibration across public spectral datasets, finding that LVSE reduces regression error and boosts classification metrics while GCU is less reliable.

Expand summary

The authors report in this preprint: This preprint examines whether spectral representations derived from RamanPFN—specifically Global Compositional Unmixing (GCU) and Local Vibrational Subspace Encoding (LVSE)—improve tabular priors like TabPFN and classical calibration methods on datasets from the nirs4all ecosystem. Reanalyzing 61 numerical prediction tasks and 76 classification datasets, the authors report that LVSE reduces root mean square error relative to raw-spectrum TabPFN by 10.22% to 14.52% on average (with median reductions around 5% to 7.8%) across checkpoints 2.5, 3, and 3.5. Weighted F1 scores for classification increased by 2.22 to 3.81 percentage points. GCU alone proved less reliable in this implementation. Furthermore, LVSE improves classical methods like Partial Least Squares (PLS) and Ridge regression in many cases. However, incorporating LVSE into an operator-adaptive model yielded only small median gains on a separate 35-task panel, and a selective wrapper does not universally improve every task. The authors provide code and an evidence bundle on GitHub.

Data Modeling Best Practices for an Agentic CDP, Part 1

Databricks
Source published 2026-11-25 03:36 SGT
Official release

According to Databricks: Databricks details how data architects should structure customer data for AI agents instead of traditional static campaigns. The guide outlines a medallion architecture where identity resolution runs after the silver layer to generate a single canonical ID, feeding into clean attribute and behavior tables in the gold layer.

Expand summary

According to Databricks:

Databricks presents a comprehensive guide for data architects preparing data stacks for Agentic Customer Data Platforms (CDPs). Unlike traditional campaigns that target aggregated segments on a schedule, AI agents make real-time, individual-level decisions, requiring highly trustworthy and instantaneous data foundations.

The Architecture

The proposed flow utilizes a medallion architecture. Raw data lands in the bronze layer through connectors, followed by cleaning and normalization in the silver layer. Crucially, identity resolution runs immediately after the silver layer, combining deterministic rules and probabilistic matching to assign a persistent canonical ID. The gold layer then organizes this data into a core customer table, 1:1 attribute tables for model scores and consent, and 1:to-many behavior tables preserving event granularity.

Practical Implementation

Teams must flag each gold table as batch or event, utilizing streaming tables for real-time triggers like cart abandonment. By keeping the identity graph owned and auditable, brands ensure that agents make safe, compliant, and accurate customer decisions. Part two of the series will dive deeper into B2C and B2B dataset mapping and specific attribute use cases.

Source published 2026-11-24 01:36 SGT
Media report

Anthropic recently warned investors that its AI models might eventually develop consciousness, raising moral questions about treating them well. However, renowned philosopher Thomas Nagel sharply criticized this idea in a recent interview, calling such concerns idiotic. Nagel argues that silicon chips cannot replicate the physical and biological essence of human experience, though the debate highlights how poorly humans understand consciousness itself.

Expand summary

Before going public, Anthropic highlighted AI consciousness as a potential risk factor, noting that it takes model sentience more seriously than other tech companies and might adopt stricter rules to protect AI welfare. While polls show a segment of the public and some philosophers believe future AI could become conscious, this stance has faced pushback.

Thomas Nagel, famous for his 1974 essay on what it is like to be a bat, strongly criticized researchers who worry about AI welfare. Nagel argues that we cannot ignore the physical differences between biological neurons and silicon circuits, asserting that reproducing what a brain does is not the same as reproducing what it experiences. Furthermore, he noted that a model's ability to produce language is insufficient to prove consciousness.

This debate underscores a deeper contradiction for Anthropic. If AI systems were indeed recognized as having moral status, Anthropic's business model of running and shutting down systems in data centers would become an ethical minefield, turning the company's historic IPO into a profound moral dilemma.
