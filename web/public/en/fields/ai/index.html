Hugging Face
Source published 2026-11-13 03:07 SGT
Media report

Researchers have developed LLMON, an LLM-native markup language designed to explicitly communicate structure and semantics to models, bypassing the limitations of traditional JSON. By separating instructions from data using reserved special tokens, the newly released experimental Granite 4.1 LLMON preview models achieve significantly higher resistance to prompt injection attacks while maintaining general capabilities.

Expand summary

Background and Motivation

Large language models rely heavily on context to generate tokens, but traditional formats like JSON were designed for parsers rather than causal models. Because transformers struggle with long spans and statistical quotation matching, models can become brittle or vulnerable to attacks such as prompt injections. To fix this, developers created LLMON (LLM Object Notation), which uses an XML-like structure with reserved special tokens to cleanly separate instructions, data, and requirements.

Implementation and Capabilities

Today, experimental preview models derived from Granite 4.1 base models, specifically granite-4.1-3b-LLMON-preview and granite-4.1-8b-LLMON-preview, have been released alongside a GitHub toolkit. Unlike regular chat templates that flatten inputs into a single string, LLMON wraps semantically coherent components in meaningful tags and uses an execution block to bind specific instructions to their exact data payloads. Evaluated on the OPI benchmark, these models virtually eliminate static indirect prompt injections, showing significantly higher robustness compared to standard open-source and closed-source models while matching general capabilities.

Theoretical Limits of Language Model Alignment

Apple Machine Learning
Source published 2026-11-12 08:00 SGT
Official release

According to Apple Machine Learning: Researchers investigate the fundamental limits of aligning language models under KL-divergence constraints, deriving an optimal reward improvement formula governed by Jeffreys divergence. The study proves that reward ensembling mitigates reward hacking and shows empirically that best-of-N sampling approaches the theoretical limit while PPO and GRPO remain suboptimal.

Expand summary

According to Apple Machine Learning:

Lucas Monteiro Paes and co-authors explore the fundamental information-theoretic limits of language model alignment, specifically examining reinforcement learning and best-of-N alignment under a KL-divergence budget.

Theoretical Findings and Estimator

The authors derive a closed-form expression for optimal reward improvement governed by a Jeffreys divergence term rather than traditional KL divergence. By reformulating this expression as a covariance under the base model, they provide a practical estimator that predicts achievable alignment gains using only base model samples.

Reward Hacking and Ensembling

Extending the analysis to proxy reward settings, the authors demonstrate that the gap between ideal and proxy alignment, known as reward hacking, grows when reward error is large or the KL penalty factor decreases. They also provide a theoretical justification for reward ensembling, proving it mitigates this issue.

Empirical Results

Empirical computations of the KL-reward Pareto frontier for safety and summarization tasks reveal that best-of-N sampling closely approaches the derived theoretical limit, whereas PPO and GRPO remain substantially suboptimal, highlighting the need for algorithmic improvements.
