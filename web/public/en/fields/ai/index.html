Google Research releases Antigravity teamwork and Gemini 4 for scientific problem solving

Google Research
Source published 2026-11-14 04:47 SGT
Official release

According to Google, these tools pair parallel generation with dedicated verifiers to bridge the gap in model predictability and partition context to maintain multi-day task accuracy. Google is making these agentic tools available externally to researchers and developers.

Expand summary

According to Google Research:

Background and Design

Google Research details the design and applications of Antigravity's teamwork multiagent systems, built on top of various Gemini models including Gemini 4 Argon, Carbon, and Hydrogen. Frontier research requires navigating vast literature, building computational pipelines, and verifying every step. To address the unreliability of single model generations and the loss of context in multi-day tasks, teamwork uses parallel exploration paired with dedicated falsifiers like Lean 4 proof checkers and execution simulators, while partitioning context across specialized agents.

Scientific and Engineering Results

According to Google, teams across Google and academic institutions used these multi-agent workflows to solve long-standing open problems. In mathematics and theoretical computer science, researchers proved the 2013 Courtade–Kumar conjecture, resolved a 2014 open question in adaptive data analysis, and formalized complex proofs in Lean 4, including the Weil conjectures and parts of the Navier–Stokes equations. In systems software and hardware engineering, the tools were used to build the FarArray low-latency kernel-bypass system, rewrite large codebases like the Zircon microkernel and libgav1 into safe Rust, and optimize TPU kernels, yielding significant performance gains over manual implementations.

Availability

Google states that these features, along with the /boost command and Deep Review document evaluation tools, are currently available in Antigravity. Additionally, Google has opened applications providing 10,000 academic researchers with six months of free access to their highest usage tier.

Goal Engineering: The Future of Designing AI Agents

LangChain Blog
Source published 2026-11-15 00:00 SGT
Official release

According to LangChain Blog: As AI agents evolve to tackle complex, open-ended goals, designing their scope becomes critical. Companies need to focus on goal engineering rather than just prompt engineering, ensuring that agents operate within a scope that makes it easy for humans to provide feedback. A case study of Actively AI shows that organizing agents around specific types of jobs rather than individual accounts allows for faster learning and better context sharing.

Expand summary

According to LangChain Blog:

Background and Challenges

As AI agents transition from simple text generation and tool calling to executing full, open-ended tasks, the way we design their scope matters immensely. Pointing agents in the wrong direction wastes valuable tokens. A major bottleneck in agent performance is a lack of the right context, especially tacit knowledge that lives only inside human heads and cannot be found in written documents.

The Role of Feedback and UX

When agents tackle subjective tasks alongside humans, they need constant feedback to learn brand tastes, strategic preferences, and nuanced behaviors. Building an intuitive and delightful UX for reviewing agent work is essential. Since the bottleneck for agent performance is learning from feedback, optimizing the ease and quality of feedback collection unlocks long-horizon capabilities.

Case Study: Actively AI

Actively AI initially designed sales agents on a per-account basis, giving each agent the goal of moving a single prospective customer forward. However, they found that salespeople suffered from constant context switching and struggled to share learnings across different accounts. In their second iteration, Actively shifted to "motions", where agents handle specific jobs to be done, such as outbounding or deal progression, across multiple accounts. This aligned the workflow with how humans naturally review and provide feedback, allowing agents to learn much more quickly while background agents gather objective account data.

After three and a half years of growth, the llama.cpp project has become a foundational tool for running multi-modal large language models locally on everything from consumer computers to enterprise servers.
