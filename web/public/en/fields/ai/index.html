Anthropic researchers shrink Riemann hypothesis proof by 99.5 percent using AI

QbitAI 量子位
Source published 2026-11-26 08:40 SGT
Media report

Anthropic researcher Luke Sernau and colleagues, working with Claude, have reduced a massive 17.4-million-line formal proof of the Riemann hypothesis down to about 76,000 lines. The original proof, created by mathematician Levent Alpöge and Claude, was so massive that it took over 21 hours to compile on high-end AWS hardware. In contrast, the newly streamlined version builds in just one to two hours on a standard 12-core, 24GB machine and has passed multiple independent verifications.

Expand summary

Background

Earlier, mathematician Levent Alpöge collaborated with Anthropic's Claude to release a 17.4-million-line formal proof of the Riemann hypothesis and generalized Riemann hypothesis in Lean. While it was a major milestone, the massive text contained over 175,000 files with almost no comments, making it nearly impossible for human mathematicians to read and verify directly.

Refactoring Details

Anthropic researcher Luke Sernau and his colleagues stepped in to completely rewrite and restructure the proof with Claude. They released two versions: a readable version with around 300,000 lines of code accompanied by documentation, and a compact version trimmed down to roughly 7,600 lines of pure code across 792 files. Sernau explained that the reduction was achieved by unrolling definitions to find global simplifications across module boundaries, rewriting certain sub-arguments, and then repackaging the code.

Impact

This dramatic shrinkage brings human-readable mathematical understanding much closer, translating the proof to a scale roughly equivalent to a 1,300-page paper. The entire streamlined code relies on standard Lean axioms and has passed independent checks by multiple verification tools. Additionally, the project has been open-sourced under the Apache 2.0 license, allowing the broader mathematical and AI community to freely use and study it.

Expand summary

Beyond film drafting, they also tested the model as a world model on real-world robotics and autonomous driving footage.

Verifiable Social Reasoning for LLM Assistants

Google Research
Source published 2026-11-26 01:10 SGT
Official release

According to Google Research: Google researchers introduce an evaluation framework using multi-agent simulations to test how well AI assistants handle social reasoning through subjective user narratives. Testing 12 LLMs reveals that relying on user-mediated reality complicates reasoning, as models are easily swayed by user biases and framing.

Expand summary

According to Google Research:

The User-Mediated Reality Challenge

Unlike objective tasks like math or coding, when AI assistants give personal advice, they don't observe social interactions firsthand. Instead, they rely solely on the subjective lens of the user, creating an information asymmetry where omitted context or personal bias can lead the model to validate one-sided assumptions rather than offer objective assessments.

Multi-Agent Simulation Approach

To solve the lack of verifiable ground truth in everyday social interactions, the researchers developed a five-step evaluation framework using the open-source Concordia library. The framework starts with a controllable entity possessing a hidden motive, has simulated characters interact naturally, and then has the user agent consult the evaluated assistant. This setup separates simulation from debrief, generating over 20,000 examples for testing.

Key Findings and Open Sourcing

Testing 12 LLMs uncovered several issues: an assistant-observer gap where models perform worse through subjective user lenses, high sensitivity to user framing, and the failure of multi-turn conversations to always improve accuracy due to anchoring effects. Google has open-sourced the framework and dataset to encourage further research.

Context Scaling: A New Frontier of AI Scaling

arXiv Artificial Intelligence
Source published 2026-11-26 13:00 SGT
Preprint

The authors report in this preprint: Researchers formalize 'context scaling' as a distinct pillar of AI scaling alongside training and decision-time compute, focusing on how systems acquire, represent, and deliver deployment state.

Expand summary

The authors report in this preprint: This position paper and narrative survey, published in AI Plus by Xipeng Qiu and colleagues, argues that many failures in deployed AI stem from a mismatch between the state a task requires and the context available when the system acts. The authors introduce 'context scaling' as a third scaling dimension alongside training budget (B_theta) and decision-time budget (B_d), governed by a context-infrastructure budget (B_c). By treating the model-infrastructure pair as the primary unit of analysis, the framework formalizes a five-operation context lifecycle, categorizes deployment state failures (availability, fidelity, selection, and utilization), and proposes utility-cost curves and controlled budget interventions for evaluation. As a conceptual survey, it does not introduce new model weights or empirical benchmarks, but offers a structured research agenda for persistent, adaptive AI systems.

Attributing Agentic Failure in Reasoning Vision-Language Models with Interactive 2D Environments

arXiv Machine Learning
Source published 2026-11-26 13:00 SGT
Preprint

The authors report in this preprint: Researchers introduce MultiNet v2.0-Gridworld, an interactive maze environment designed to disentangle the causes of failure in reasoning vision-language models, revealing severe limitations in handling state and multi-step mechanisms.

Expand summary

The authors report in this preprint: Current agentic benchmarks typically report a single success rate that confounds distinct failure causes, such as environmental misinterpretation, misunderstanding mechanism mechanics, incorrect ordering, or a lack of error recovery. To address this, the authors present an early preview of MultiNet v2.0-Gridworld, an interactive 2D maze environment built for granular attribution rather than simple leaderboard ranking. The environment allows four mechanism types—key-door pairs, switch-gate pairs, distractors/decoys, and ordered combinations—to be independently modulated while keeping the renderer, action space, and scoring function fixed. By performing a breadth-first search over the joint state space of position, facing direction, inventory, and mechanism state, every instance receives an exact optimal action count to define difficulty and grade unfinished episodes. Evaluating three vision-language models on a fixed protocol of 50 mazes (540 episodes total across variants), the authors report that the models solved only 6 out of 150 evaluated episodes, 45 out of 50 mazes were completely unsolved by any model, and no episode containing a switch-gate mechanism was successfully completed. This preprint provides a clear diagnostic framework but highlights significant current performance bottlenecks in agentic vision-language models.

Expand summary

According to Chinese media reports and social media images, Alibaba Group Holding co-founder Jack Ma recently made a rare visit to the offices of the company's chip design unit T-Head in Shanghai. Dressed casually in a grey jumper, Ma posed for photos with employees just as the wholly owned subsidiary moves forward with a planned Hong Kong listing. While the exact size and timeline of the IPO remain unfinalised, Alibaba has confirmed that T-Head will stay a subsidiary post-listing.

Founded in 2018, T-Head forms a central pillar of Alibaba's full-stack artificial intelligence strategy, which encompasses chips, computing infrastructure, AI models, and applications. Amid tightening US export controls, Alibaba chief executive Eddie Wu Yongming stated that the firm is accelerating its pivot to domestic processors and already training its next-generation AI models on T-Head-designed chips. This push aligns with broader national objectives, as Beijing prioritizes technological self-sufficiency in its current five-year plan, with regional authorities like Guangdong encouraging computing centres to favor domestic hardware.
