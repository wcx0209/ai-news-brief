Story angle

Quantum simulators reveal hidden non-equilibrium phases and phase transitions in Nature study

AWS Quantum Technologies
Source published 2026-11-18 02:13 SGT
Official release

According to AWS Quantum Technologies: Researchers from Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory and QuEra used Amazon Braket to study interacting quantum particles pushed far from equilibrium. According to _Nature_, the team discovered long-lived prethermal regimes, resonant melting mechanisms, and a first-order dynamical phase transition that differs from the system's equilibrium behavior.

Expand summary

According to AWS Quantum Technologies:

In a recent _Nature_ study, a collaboration between NERSC, QuEra Computing, Harvard, and Boston University used QuEra's Aquila quantum processor via Amazon Braket alongside NERSC's Perlmutter supercomputer to explore quantum particles pushed far from equilibrium. When the system is rapidly disturbed, it typically re-equilibrates to thermal physics. However, the team observed stable dynamical behaviors that escape ordinary thermalization.

By adjusting atom spacing, geometry, and control fields across arrays of up to 180 atoms, the researchers mapped out prethermal regimes in one and two dimensions. They found that strong interactions suppress simultaneous excitations, protecting the system in a long-lived prethermal state before it eventually thermalizes. Furthermore, specific energy resonances cause these states to 'melt' through identifiable pathways. In two-dimensional lattices, as system sizes scaled up, a sharp boundary emerged that satisfied a generalized Clausius-Clapeyron relation, providing quantitative evidence of a first-order dynamical phase transition occurring outside of equilibrium.

IBM Shows Progress in Bringing Quantum and HPC Together at SC26

IBM Quantum
Source published 2026-11-17 00:00 SGT
Official release

According to IBM Quantum: IBM and its partners demonstrated how quantum computers are integrating into traditional supercomputing workflows at the SC26 conference. Highlights include pairing quantum resource management with Slurm to simplify job scheduling, achieving record-breaking molecular simulations using hybrid quantum-classical methods, and launching new open-source tools to accelerate quantum error correction research on GPUs.

Expand summary

According to IBM Quantum:

Quantum-HPC Integration

IBM, Cleveland Clinic, and RIKEN are finalists for the 2026 ACM Gordon Bell Prize after simulating protein-ligand complexes of up to 12,635 atoms using a combination of quantum processors and classical supercomputers. By breaking complex molecules into clusters, the team solved difficult parts using sample-based quantum diagonalization (SQD) alongside classical HPC methods.

Platforms for Error Correction

To tackle error correction, IBM partnered with ORNL and AMD to launch a co-design platform that places classical hardware directly next to physical qubits in Poughkeepsie, New York. Additionally, IBM and AMD released Pelagos, an open-source framework written in Triton that accelerates quantum error correction decoder tests on GPUs up to 97 times faster than standard CPU execution.

Workflow Management

Supercomputing centers rely on schedulers like Slurm to manage tasks. IBM and NVIDIA are integrating the open-source Quantum Resource Management Interface (QRMI) with Slurm's features, allowing researchers to request quantum processing units in job scripts just like they would request traditional GPUs or CPUs.

Altera, OQC and NVIDIA Link Quantum Processor to GPU Computing

The Quantum Insider
Source published 2026-11-16 22:05 SGT
Media report
AI

Altera, Oxford Quantum Circuits (OQC) and NVIDIA developed a reference design that connects quantum processors directly to GPUs using FPGA technology. This setup aims to solve the space and hardware challenges in quantum computing by using GPUs for heavy tasks like calibration and machine learning. The companies plan to release the design in January 2027.

Expand summary

Background

As quantum computers grow in scale, the number of qubits is increasing much faster than the physical space available for the traditional control equipment around them. This creates a bottleneck where control hardware needs to become significantly denser.

Details

According to Altera, the new end-to-end reference design is built on an Agilex 9 Direct RF FPGA. The FPGA handles real-time system control, qubit initialization, and resonator readout, communicating directly with an OQC quantum processing unit through specialized RF electronics. Meanwhile, NVIDIA NVQLink connects the FPGA to NVIDIA GPUs, which take over heavy computational tasks such as system calibration and machine learning-based readout classification using CUDA.

Impact

This collaboration provides developers with a validated software stack and a built-in emulation path to test algorithms before physical hardware is ready. The system is scheduled for a live demonstration at Supercomputing 2026 in Chicago, with the commercial release expected in January 2027.

GenQ Emerges From Stealth to Commercialize Quantum Measurement Technology

The Quantum Insider
Source published 2026-11-17 04:48 SGT
Media report

Quantum measurement startup GenQ has officially emerged from stealth, announcing a seed funding round co-led by Wonder Ventures and Offline Ventures. Founded by former SpaceX engineer Dr. Haley Weinstein, the company develops compact, room-temperature products designed to integrate with existing classical infrastructure, such as optical network monitoring, aiming to deliver practical quantum applications today rather than years in the future.

Expand summary

Background

For decades, practical and scalable quantum technology has largely been viewed as a distant, aspirational goal requiring years of speculative research and development. Generation Quantum (GenQ) was founded to challenge this narrative by focusing on near-term commercial viability.

Details

Founded by Dr. Haley Weinstein, who drew on her experience working on intersatellite laser communications at SpaceX, GenQ develops compact, room-temperature quantum measurement products. The company's recent seed funding round was co-led by Wonder Ventures and Offline Ventures, with additional backing from pre-seed investor Ambition Capital. GenQ's technical team includes talent with backgrounds from the NSA, Boeing, and the University of Southern California (USC).

Impact

Investors and academic partners report that GenQ's technology has already outperformed traditional monitoring systems in specific tests, operating at the fundamental limits of quantum sensitivity. According to its backers, the company aims to serve both commercial and government markets by augmenting existing classical infrastructure without requiring entirely new foundational networks.
