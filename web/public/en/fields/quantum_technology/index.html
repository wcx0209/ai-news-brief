Germany Invests €35 Million to Build a 200-Qubit Silicon Quantum Computer

Quantum Computing Report
Source published 2026-11-25 07:05 SGT
Media report

Forschungszentrum Jülich and ARQUE Systems have secured €35 million to launch the Q-STAR.NRW initiative, aiming to build a 200-qubit semiconductor-based quantum computer by the end of 2029. Funded largely by the German federal government and North Rhine-Westphalia, the project will use a patented electron-shuttling architecture in silicon to reduce wiring complexity and make large-scale quantum processors easier to build.

Expand summary

Project Background and Funding

According to Forschungszentrum Jülich, the Jülich–Aachen spin-off ARQUE Systems has launched the Q-STAR.NRW initiative backed by a €35 million investment running through the end of 2029. The funding is provided 90% by German federal funds under the Coal Regions Investment Act and 10% by the State of North Rhine-Westphalia via Forschungszentrum Jülich. The resulting system will be deployed into the Jülich UNified Infrastructure for Quantum Computing at the Jülich Supercomputing Centre.

Technology and Architecture

Building on ARQUE Systems’ earlier five-qubit prototype, the new system encodes quantum information in the spins of individual electrons trapped in silicon and silicon-germanium structures. Instead of using traditional fixed couplings and dedicated control lines for every single qubit, the system utilizes a patented shuttling architecture that moves electrons to shared interaction zones. This design aims to significantly reduce wiring overhead and lay the groundwork for scaling beyond ten million qubits.

Software and Applications

In addition to hardware construction, the project partners will develop hybrid quantum-HPC software stacks and user support services to integrate the 200-qubit processor with classical supercomputers. This infrastructure will grant academic researchers and small and medium-sized enterprises access to test practical applications in materials science, chemistry, pharmaceuticals, and logistics.

IBM Celebrates One Year of the Genesis Mission to Advance Quantum Computing

IBM Quantum
Source published 2026-11-24 13:00 SGT
Official release

According to IBM Quantum: One year after the launch of the U.S. Genesis Mission, IBM and its partners are pushing forward with quantum-centric supercomputing by combining quantum hardware, AI, and classical systems. Recent efforts focus on error correction, fault-tolerant computing, and scientific applications in materials and energy research.

Expand summary

According to IBM Quantum: Marking the first anniversary of the Department of Energy's Genesis Mission, IBM outlines its ongoing contributions to integrating quantum computing, artificial intelligence, and high-performance computing. Over the past month, IBM has collaborated with institutions like Oak Ridge National Laboratory, AMD, NVIDIA, and the University of Chicago on several initiatives. These include co-design platforms for quantum error correction, integrating quantum resource management with NVIDIA's Slurm workload manager, and deploying new quantum processors for research in drug discovery, materials, and high-energy physics. IBM continues to work toward its goal of deploying the fault-tolerant quantum computer, Quantum Starling, by 2029, combining hybrid quantum-classical algorithms and AI to tackle complex challenges in fields such as nuclear fusion research.

QuEra joins 12 million dollar US project to use AI for quantum error correction

The Quantum Insider
Source published 2026-11-24 02:39 SGT
Media report
AI

QuEra Computing has joined a Harvard-led, 12 million dollar U.S. Department of Energy project to develop AI-designed error correction for quantum computers. The ASQC project brings together national labs, universities, NVIDIA, and Anthropic to tailor error-correcting codes to specific scientific workloads. Researchers will test these systems on QuEra's neutral-atom quantum computers, with the ultimate goal of reducing the overhead that slows down fault-tolerant quantum computing.

Expand summary

According to QuEra Computing, the company has partnered in the Application-Aware Error Correction Codesign for Scientific Quantum Computing project, known as ASQC. This initiative is part of the Department of Energy's Genesis Mission and aims to leverage AI models to identify the errors that most heavily impact scientific workloads like chemistry, materials science, and nuclear science. By designing tailored error-correcting codes and decoders, the project seeks to lower the heavy resource overhead that currently separates noisy hardware from fault-tolerant quantum computers.

The project team includes prominent researchers from Harvard, MIT, Cornell, and national laboratories alongside industry partners like NVIDIA and Anthropic. According to QuEra, the effort builds on prior work, including a preprint describing a neural-network decoder that reduced error rates, and previous tests where large language models like Claude helped control hardware testbeds. QuEra CEO Andy Ory states that application-co-designed error correction could accelerate the company's roadmap toward its planned Libra fault-tolerant system on AWS in early 2028, though commercial deployment timelines remain subject to ongoing hardware and software development.
