Stanford Physicist Uses AI to Tackle Quantum Gravity

The Quantum Insider
Source published 2026-11-26 20:32 SGT
Media report
AI

A Stanford physicist has proposed a quantum theory of gravity using the paths of light, which successfully yielded a finite calculation and reproduced a known black hole entropy correction. The researcher stated that the work involved months of collaboration with OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra, utilizing about 400 million tokens.

Expand summary

Background and Approach

Reconciling general relativity with quantum mechanics has challenged physicists for decades. Stanford researcher Venkatesa Chandrasekaran proposed using light paths to establish the boundaries of finite spacetime regions, allowing calculations to avoid infinite divergence.

Calculations and AI Collaboration

By keeping the gravitational constant at its physical value, the approach produced finite answers in a graviton-pair calculation. The author acknowledged that several proofs and calculations were developed through months of back-and-forth work with OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra, consuming roughly 400 million tokens.

Status and Limitations

The study also recovered the standard relationship between black hole entropy and surface area, alongside a quantum correction. However, the paper is currently a preprint on arXiv and has not yet undergone official peer review.

Diraq Secures $38 Million U.S. Funding for Silicon Quantum Chips

Quantum Computing Report
Source published 2026-11-27 19:09 SGT
Media report

Silicon spin-qubit developer Diraq has finalized a $38 million funding agreement with the U.S. Department of Commerce under the CHIPS and Science Act. The money will help accelerate U.S.-based fabrication and testing infrastructure for its CMOS-compatible silicon quantum processors, aiming to overcome key scaling bottlenecks.

Expand summary

Diraq has officially converted its earlier Letter of Intent into a definitive funding award of up to $38 million from the U.S. Department of Commerce’s CHIPS Research and Development Office. As the only silicon spin-qubit company chosen in this round of CHIPS R&D funding, Diraq plans to pair its Australian scientific base with expanded facilities across several U.S. locations including Palo Alto and Chicago. The company aims to use the infrastructure support to address challenges in scalable Quantum Logic Units and global qubit control.

Looking ahead, Diraq's roadmap outlines ambitious technical milestones. The company targets a compact data center footprint supporting 150,000 physical qubits by 2029, and plans to scale beyond two million physical qubits by 2031. This builds on its recent progression in DARPA’s Quantum Benchmarking Initiative and a planned commercial data-center deployment with Equinix.

Riverlane and Nature Research Intelligence Release 2026 Quantum Error Correction Report

Quantum Computing Report
Source published 2026-11-26 13:14 SGT
Media report

According to Riverlane and Nature Research Intelligence, the quantum computing industry is shifting from basic physics experiments to full-stack engineering of fault-tolerant machines. Based on data from 2021 to 2026, research output, patent filings, and public funding have all grown significantly, driven by a transition toward advanced error codes like qLDPC.

Expand summary

Riverlane and Nature Research Intelligence published The Quantum Error Correction Report 2026, combining publication records, patent data, and expert interviews. The report states that the industry is pivoting toward full-stack engineering. Between 2021 and August 2026, QEC research output grew at a compound annual growth rate of 36.4%, with the U.S. and China leading in publication volume. Patent families also grew significantly, led by tech companies like Microsoft, Google, and Riverlane. Meanwhile, public funding has reached approximately $2.5 billion, supporting government-backed validation programs in the U.S., UK, and Europe.

Technically, while surface codes remain common, the report notes a sharp increase in high-rate quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) code adoption. Milestones include Google Quantum AI's magic-state cultivation and Quantinuum's iceberg code demonstrations. Looking ahead, experts warn that real-time decoding and cross-layer system integration remain the primary bottlenecks for scaling fault-tolerant computers.

Equal1 Advances in EIC STEP Scale Up Program for Up to €30 Million Investment

The Quantum Insider
Source published 2026-11-27 23:13 SGT
Media report

Irish quantum computing company Equal1 has advanced to the due-diligence phase for a potential equity investment of up to €30 million from the European Innovation Council Fund. The funding will be used to scale the production and deployment of its on-premises quantum computers, which use standard silicon manufacturing processes.

Expand summary

Equal1, a spinout from University College Dublin, announced it has been selected to advance in the EIC STEP Scale Up program, opening the path for a potential equity investment of up to €30 million. According to the company, the prospective funding will accelerate its commercialization roadmap and scale the deployment of its on-premise quantum computing solutions.

Equal1's technical approach focuses on integrating quantum silicon spin qubits, control electronics, and error correction onto a single chip using standard CMOS semiconductor manufacturing processes. CEO Jason Lynch stated that this method aims to eliminate the massive infrastructure barriers associated with legacy quantum systems. The EIC STEP Scale Up program provides equity investments of €10 to 30 million to help European deep tech companies scale strategic technologies.

IQM to Deploy 54-Qubit Quantum Computer in South Korean Data Centre

The Quantum Insider
Source published 2026-11-26 02:08 SGT
Media report

IQM Quantum Computers has secured an order from South Korean firm QAI to install an IQM Radiance R3 54-qubit quantum computer in Seoul by the end of 2027. This marks IQM's first private data centre deployment in Asia, aiming to provide Quantum-as-a-Service alongside GPU infrastructure for local enterprises and researchers.

Expand summary

Partnership Details

IQM Quantum Computers has announced an agreement with South Korean company QAI to supply an IQM Radiance R3 54-qubit system. According to the announcement, the quantum computer is scheduled for delivery and on-premises installation at QAI's data centre in Seoul by the end of 2027. This system represents the first time an IQM quantum computer will be integrated into a commercial, private data centre in the Asia-Pacific region.

Infrastructure and Applications

QAI plans to integrate the quantum computer with its existing GPU-based infrastructure, offering access via a Quantum-as-a-Service platform. According to QAI, the combined system will support compute-intensive fields including drug discovery, new materials development, financial portfolio optimization, and supply chain scheduling for corporate, academic, and public-sector users.

National Context

The deployment aligns with South Korea's broader national focus on quantum technology. Under government science and technology frameworks, Korea aims to foster quantum capabilities and widespread utilization. IQM previously installed an IQM Spark system at Chungbuk National University through government procurement and established a Seoul office in June 2025.

SAP and planqc Expand Quantum Computing Partnership

The Quantum Insider
Source published 2026-11-26 22:30 SGT
Media report

SAP and planqc have demonstrated a cloud interface linking enterprise software to a neutral-atom quantum platform. They successfully tested a logistics loading problem using a quantum simulator and plan to run tests on actual hardware in 2027.

Expand summary

SAP and planqc are expanding their quantum computing partnership, marked by the demonstration of a cloud interface that connects SAP enterprise software with planqc's neutral-atom quantum platform. During an event in Munich, the companies showcased a live logistics use case, where an SAP system sent a beverage truck-loading optimization problem to a quantum simulator and received the resulting loading plan back into its software environment. This hybrid architecture aims to combine classical computing with quantum resources. According to planqc, the collaboration seeks to embed quantum capabilities into standard business workflows. Both companies plan to begin testing actual business applications on planqc's neutral-atom quantum hardware by 2027, as SAP explores ways to address complex optimization challenges.
