Google Quantum AI proves quantum contextuality works at scale on Willow processor

Google Research
Source published 2026-11-20 04:03 SGT
Official release
AI

According to Google Research: Google Quantum AI researchers report demonstrating quantum contextuality on their Willow superconducting quantum processors, outperforming classical strategies across tasks involving up to 105 qubits. Published in Science, the work provides an experimental test showing that quantum systems do not assign fixed, pre-existing values to unmeasured properties, serving as both a core computational resource and a strict hardware benchmark.

Expand summary

According to Google Research:

Beating the classical limit

Researchers ran the magic square game on the Willow quantum processor, using entangled Bell pairs instead of a pre-written table. While the best classical strategy wins at most 88.89% of the time, the qubits won 98.30% of the time, proving that answers take shape only upon measurement according to their context.

Measuring contextuality at scale

Using a Bell-Kochen-Specker inequality, the team measured a contextuality score of 5.590, comfortably past the classical bound of 4 and close to the quantum limit of 6. They also scaled the experiment using the GHZ parity game and solved hidden linear function problems on grids of up to 105 qubits.

A new hardware ruler

These tasks demand strict agreement among multiple qubits, exposing subtle coherent errors that conventional random-circuit benchmarks miss. This provides an objective performance ceiling grounded in physics rather than shifting classical standards.

Chinese Team Wins 2026 Gordon Bell Prize for Exascale Nitrogen Fixation Simulation

The Quantum Insider
Source published 2026-11-21 00:13 SGT
Media report

A 10-member team from Chinese institutions has won the 2026 ACM Gordon Bell Prize for developing the first classical solution at exascale for a key nitrogen fixation simulation. The researchers modeled the iron-molybdenum cofactor on two Chinese supercomputers, achieving a sustained 1.456 exaflops in double precision.

Expand summary

According to the Association for Computing Machinery (ACM), the 10-member team received the prestigious Gordon Bell Prize during the SC26 conference in Chicago for their project titled 'Closing the FeMo-Cofactor Quantum-Advantage Gap with Exascale Supercomputers.' The Gordon Bell Prize recognizes outstanding achievements in applying high-performance computing to complex scientific and engineering problems.

The team focused on the FeMo Cofactor CAS problem, which involves simulating the complex quantum-level interactions of the iron-molybdenum cofactor that helps enzymes convert nitrogen into ammonia—a crucial process for agriculture and biology. Prior to this, classical computers struggled to simulate these intricate electronic behaviors at scale.

To tackle this, the researchers tuned their simulation code for two advanced Chinese supercomputers, LineShine and China's New-generation Intelligent Super-computer (CNIS). According to their paper's abstract, the team achieved a sustained performance of 1.456 exaflops in double precision across 20,480 nodes on LineShine, utilizing 58.9% of the machine's peak capacity, alongside 5.626 exaflops in mixed precision.

Google's Quantum Chip Beats Classical Limits in Tests of Teamwork

The Quantum Insider
Source published 2026-11-20 04:31 SGT
Media report

Google Quantum AI researchers used their Willow processor to show that quantum contextuality can outperform classical strategies in cooperative games. The chip won a four-qubit game 98.3% of the time, beating the classical limit, and successfully scaled past classical limits with up to 71 qubits, offering a new method to detect hardware errors.

Expand summary

Cooperative Games and Quantum Contextuality

Researchers at Google Quantum AI utilized the Willow processor to investigate quantum contextuality, a property where measurement outcomes depend on accompanying measurements. In a magic square game preventing player communication, four qubits achieved a 98.3% winning rate, surpassing the 88.9% classical limit.

Scaling Up to 71 Qubits

The team scaled the experiments using a Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger state. The winning rate stayed above the classical ceiling through 71 players, although the performance margin shrank as more qubits were introduced due to experimental noise.

Mathematical Problem and Error Detection

In a final test involving a two-dimensional hidden linear function, a 105-qubit experiment returned valid answers more than half the time. The researchers note that these tests can help spot subtle hardware errors that conventional checks might miss.

Sorbonne and Qubit Pharmaceuticals introduce VULCAN framework for efficient quantum chemistry

Quantum Computing Report
Source published 2026-11-21 16:14 SGT
Media report

Researchers from Sorbonne Université and Qubit Pharmaceuticals have introduced VULCAN, a hybrid quantum-classical framework designed to make quantum chemistry calculations much more efficient. By combining smart circuit compression and GPU acceleration, the method reaches chemical accuracy using a fraction of the quantum resources previously required.

Expand summary

According to a preprint on arXiv, researchers from Sorbonne Université and Qubit Pharmaceuticals developed VULCAN (Variational Upbuilding, Layered Compression, Adaptive N-body perturbation) to solve the major measurement bottleneck in traditional adaptive quantum algorithms. Instead of checking thousands of candidate operators one by one at every step, VULCAN derives relevant excitations quickly and appends full layers simultaneously.

In GPU-accelerated tests up to 32 qubits, the authors report that VULCAN achieved chemical accuracy using only 5% to 20% of the measurements required by standard batched methods across various molecules. The team also conducted an initial hardware validation on a Quantinuum trapped-ion processor for molecular hydrogen, successfully measuring a ground-state energy close to the exact reference.
