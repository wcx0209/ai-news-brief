Merck and Kelun's new cancer drug succeeds in stomach cancer trial

BioSpace Drug Development
Source published 2026-11-20 22:33 SGT
Media report

Merck announced that its jointly developed drug with Kelun-Biotech, sac-TMT, successfully improved overall survival in a late-stage stomach cancer trial, marking its second major Phase 3 milestone. Although it missed other secondary goals, experts note that overall survival remains the gold standard in oncology, paving the way for future regulatory submissions.

Expand summary

According to Merck, the Phase 3 clinical trial known as TroFuse-015 evaluated 473 patients with advanced gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma who had previously received multiple therapies. The drug, a TROP2-targeting antibody-drug conjugate originally discovered by China startup Kelun-Biotech, successfully met its primary endpoint by significantly improving overall survival compared to standard treatments chosen by physicians. However, the trial missed secondary endpoints including progression-free survival and objective response rate. Market analysts point out that missing these secondary goals is not uncommon in cancer immunotherapies as long as the primary survival metric succeeds.

This trial is part of a broad global clinical program featuring 18 late-stage studies across various tumor types. The therapy is also under priority review by the U.S. FDA for endometrial cancer and has secured approvals in China for multiple indications. Financial analysts view this drug as a critical asset for Merck to offset future revenue risks from its blockbuster drug Keytruda. Merck initially partnered with Kelun in 2022 through lucrative licensing deals worth billions to secure rights to these promising antibody-drug conjugates.

Clearing Cellular Traffic Jams Treats Multiple Diseases in Mice

Broad Institute
Source published 2026-11-20 00:00 SGT
Official releaseLaboratory result

According to Broad Institute: Researchers at the Broad Institute have discovered a shared biological mechanism behind multiple genetic diseases caused by misfolded proteins. By targeting a specific molecular complex that gets jammed in cells, they successfully cleared disease hallmarks in mouse models of both kidney disease and blindness, opening a potential path to treat millions of patients with similar conditions.

Expand summary

According to Broad Institute:

According to researchers at the Broad Institute publishing in _Neuron_, a group of over 50 inherited disorders known as proteinopathies share a common root cause: misfolded proteins accumulating inside cells and overwhelming the cellular quality-control machinery.

Building on their previous work on TMED9 in MUC1 kidney disease, the team found that TMED2, 7, 9, and 10 form a protein complex that traps these misfolded proteins and causes a cellular traffic jam. By deleting or targeting a key component of this complex called TMED7, scientists were able to release the trapped proteins to be recycled without disrupting normal cellular functions, successfully reversing disease hallmarks in mice with MUC1 kidney disease and slowing vision loss in a model of retinitis pigmentosa.

Revolution Medicines reports positive early survival data for pancreatic cancer drug Rasonque

BioSpace Drug Development
Source published 2026-11-20 22:51 SGT
Media report

Revolution Medicines shared early data showing that its pancreatic cancer drug Rasonque, when combined with chemotherapy, achieved a 60.7% response rate in initial treatment. Financial analysts at Truist Securities believe these strong results could push future sales beyond current projections, strengthening the drug's position as a new standard treatment.

Expand summary

Clinical Results and Trial Details

According to Revolution Medicines, data from 61 patients in the ongoing Phase 1/2 RMC-GI-102 open-label trial presented at a recent medical meeting showed that Rasonque combined with two types of chemotherapy achieved an objective response rate of 60.7% in first-line pancreatic cancer patients. The trial reported a median progression-free survival of 12.8 months, outperforming analyst expectations, while overall survival reached 76.6% at the 12-month mark.

Market Impact and Competition

Following these findings, Truist Securities noted that the drug's profile sets a high efficacy bar and could boost Revolution Medicines beyond its currently estimated $14.5 billion in peak sales. Meanwhile, the company faces patent litigation and upcoming clinical readouts from competitors like Erasca, which is developing its own targeted therapies in the space.

Novartis CEO says AI will speed up drug development, but solving biology will take a decade

Fierce Biotech
Source published 2026-11-20 06:31 SGT
Media report
AI

Novartis CEO Vas Narasimhan states that AI is already cutting clinical trial timelines and paperwork. However, he warns that solving complex biological latency will be a long journey, pushing back against tech optimists who claim major diseases will disappear in a decade.

Expand summary

Speaking at an investor event, Novartis CEO Vas Narasimhan outlined the drugmaker's goal to use AI to improve clinical success rates from 8% to 12% while slashing four years off the standard 12-year R&D cycle. Narasimhan categorized drug development bottlenecks into information, operational, and biological latency. Novartis is already seeing major gains in the first two areas, with AI assisting 90% of development content and cutting statistical programming time dramatically.

However, Narasimhan and Novartis biomedical research president Fiona Marshall argued that biological latency remains the hardest hurdle. While AI excels at molecular design, translating predictions into human biology requires navigating complex systems that current models cannot fully simulate on computers alone. This perspective echoes pushback from other pharma leaders against tech sector claims that AI will eliminate major diseases in under a decade, emphasizing that steady, incremental progress and wet-lab validation are still essential.

CTAD 2026: Biogen, AriBio and others share new Alzheimer's drug trial results

BioSpace Drug Development
Source published 2026-11-19 00:02 SGT
Media report

At the 2026 Clinical Trials on Alzheimer’s Disease conference, drug developers shared new data on various experimental treatments. Biogen reported that its anti-tau therapy diranersen slowed cognitive decline by 38% at two years in a mid-stage study. Meanwhile, South Korea's AriBio announced that its oral drug candidate AR1001 achieved a 31% slowing of clinical decline in a specific monotherapy patient group during a Phase 3 trial.

Expand summary

Researchers and biopharma companies gathered in Boston for the 2026 Clinical Trials on Alzheimer’s Disease conference to discuss treatments moving beyond traditional amyloid approaches.

Biogen's anti-tau therapy Biogen presented two-year data from the Phase 2 CELIA study of diranersen, an antisense oligonucleotide developed with Ionis. According to BMO Capital Markets and Jefferies, the low-dose regimen slowed clinical decline by 38% on a key scale. Although the trial missed its primary endpoint regarding statistically significant dose responses, analysts and trial investigators noted that the growing separation between treated and placebo groups over time supports advancing the asset into Phase 3.

AriBio's Phase 3 success AriBio reported that its oral PDE5 inhibitor, AR1001, met its primary efficacy objective in a prespecified monotherapy population during a Phase 3 trial. Among 604 patients taking the drug alone, cognitive decline slowed by 31% compared to placebo. While the overall trial population of 1,535 patients did not show a statistically significant effect, the company plans to discuss the monotherapy results with the FDA.

Other pipeline updates German biotech Priavoid reported that its oral amyloid-targeting drug PRI-002 missed its primary efficacy endpoint in a Phase 3 trial, though a small subgroup showed slower decline over 72 weeks. Additionally, Cognito Therapeutics plans to consult the FDA regarding its at-home light and sound stimulation platform, Spectris AD, which missed its primary endpoint in a Phase 3 study but showed a nominal signal in a mild Alzheimer's subgroup.

BMS Alzheimer’s drug shows promise but safety questions remain

BioSpace Drug Development
Source published 2026-11-19 00:15 SGT
Media report

Bristol Myers Squibb revealed early clinical data showing its drug Cobenfy reduced hallucinations and delusions in Alzheimer’s patients. However, analysts pointed out that safety events and high rates of side effects still leave important questions unanswered ahead of more definitive 2027 results.

Expand summary

Bristol Myers Squibb presented early data from its Phase 3 ADEPT program at the CTAD conference, testing the drug KarXT (marketed as Cobenfy) for psychosis associated with Alzheimer’s disease. Data from two studies, ADEPT-1 and ADEPT-3, showed improvements in symptoms like hallucinations and delusions.

However, treatment-emergent adverse events affected over half of the patients, reaching 56.6% in one study and 65% in the other, raising tolerability concerns. Analysts from Truist Securities noted that the current data lacks a placebo control and does not address the main trial endpoint of relapse prevention. The definitive randomized results are expected in 2027.
