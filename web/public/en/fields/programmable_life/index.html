AlphaGenome 2 predicts how DNA changes affect specific human cells

Google DeepMind
Source published 2026-11-11 23:00 SGT
Official release

According to Google DeepMind: Google has announced AlphaGenome 2, an AI model that predicts how DNA sequence variations impact specific human cell types and disease states. Trained on over 100 million individual cell profiles, the model identifies twice as many causal variants as its predecessor, helping scientists uncover the cellular roots of various diseases.

Expand summary
According to Google DeepMind: AlphaGenome 2 is a next-generation DNA sequence model designed to bridge the gap between single-cell biology and human genetics. Unlike the original version which analyzed broad tissues in parallel, AlphaGenome 2 uses a conditional architecture to evaluate how a million-letter DNA sequence behaves in one specific cell type or state at a time. Trained on over 107 million individual cell profiles from humans and mice, the model can predict gene expression, chromatin accessibility, and DNA methylation across specialized cells. It can also generalize to unseen cell states, such as those triggered by diseases or vaccinations, without requiring retraining. In benchmark tests, AlphaGenome 2 successfully identified nearly twice as many causal variants and directional gene effects compared to previous tools. It has already demonstrated the ability to pinpoint complex disease drivers, such as identifying a regulatory variant that overactivates the ETS2 gene in gut immune cells to cause inflammatory bowel disease. The tool is now available for non-commercial research via an API, with commercial access offered in private preview, and future integration planned for Google's agentic development platform.

PepGen shares almost double as DM1 oligonucleotide achieves 'best in class' splicing

BioSpace Drug Development
Source published 2026-11-11 23:23 SGT
Media report

PepGen's experimental drug for myotonic dystrophy type 1 achieved a record splicing correction of 46.5% in a mid-stage clinical trial, outperforming competitors and causing its stock price to nearly double. The positive safety profile and potential for higher doses offer new hope for treating this rare genetic disease.

Expand summary
PepGen announced that its experimental oligonucleotide PGN-EDODM1 achieved a mean splicing correction of 46.5% at week 13 in a Phase 2 trial for myotonic dystrophy type 1 (DM1). This result significantly outperforms competitors like Dyne Therapeutics, Novartis, and Ionis, which have typically achieved less than 30% correction. Analysts praised the outcome as a best-case scenario, and the news sent PepGen's stock soaring 92% to $4.61. Beyond genetic metrics, investigators noted meaningful clinical improvements in patients' muscle control, mobility, and grip strength. The data came from the lowest dose group of 10 mg/kg, and a higher 12.5 mg/kg dose arm is currently being evaluated, with results expected in the first half of 2027. Despite a lingering partial clinical hold from the FDA regarding animal safety data, PepGen successfully advanced trials internationally and launched a $125 million public offering to fund further development.

FDA's new rare disease framework faces its first big test with Capricor's DMD drug

BioSpace Drug Development
Source published 2026-11-11 03:06 SGT
Media report

The FDA's newly launched rare disease framework is facing its first major test as the agency decides whether to approve Capricor's cell therapy, deramiocel, for Duchenne muscular dystrophy. With the decision looming, the case highlights the tension between strict statistical rules and the harsh realities of treating progressive, rare conditions where slowing physical decline means preserving patient independence.

Expand summary
Capricor Therapeutics is seeking FDA approval for deramiocel, a cell therapy designed to preserve upper limb function in non-ambulatory Duchenne muscular dystrophy patients. In its Phase 3 trial, the drug slowed upper limb decline by 54% compared to placebo, though the statistical analysis plan was revised, sparking debate and scrutiny from FDA reviewers during an advisory committee meeting. The debate underscores the core dilemma of the FDA's new Rare Disease Framework Initiative. In small patient populations with progressive diseases, running large trials is nearly impossible, and missing data is common. Experts argue that the FDA needs to explicitly define how it handles analysis plan changes, evaluates the totality of evidence, and values slowing functional loss as a meaningful clinical outcome. The upcoming decision will reveal whether the FDA's new framework leads to a genuinely flexible approach or simply changes public relations rhetoric. For patients, delaying approval for another multi-year trial means losing irreversible physical independence.

Faeth’s oral cancer combo clears phase 2, challenging big drug injection therapies

Fierce Biotech
Source published 2026-11-11 22:47 SGT
Media report

Faeth Therapeutics reported that its oral cancer therapy combination, called Piktor, hit key efficacy goals in a phase 2 trial for endometrial cancer. The drug achieved a 33% confirmed overall response rate, outperforming expectations and offering a pill-based alternative to injectable treatments like ADCs and chemotherapies.

Expand summary
Faeth Therapeutics tested its combination therapy, named Piktor—consisting of serabelisib and sapanisertib—alongside intravenous paclitaxel in 42 patients with advanced or recurrent endometrial cancer. The trial yielded a 33% confirmed overall response rate and a preliminary median progression-free survival of 7.4 months. These results surpass the 30% response rate benchmark set by market analysts, rivaling standard injectable options like immunotherapies and antibody-drug conjugates. While major pharmaceutical companies heavily focus on injectable antibody-drug conjugates that often rely on topoisomerase I inhibitors, patient resistance remains a challenge. Faeth's oral approach targets the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway at multiple points, offering a distinct mechanism of action. The company now plans to discuss a randomized phase 3 trial with the FDA and expects to release data from an ongoing breast cancer study next year.

Max Jaderberg on Isomorphic Labs' Vision for AI-Driven Drug Discovery

No Priors
Source published 2026-11-12 19:00 SGT
Interview
AI

In a podcast conversation with Elad Gil, Isomorphic Labs president Max Jaderberg discusses the company's vision to shift drug discovery into a process of precision engineering through predictive AI models. Jaderberg highlights how AlphaFold's success served as a proof of concept for deep learning in biology, and predicts that compounding learnings at scale will improve clinical trial success rates and speed, potentially reducing animal testing and extending human healthspan.

Expand summary
Isomorphic Labs president Max Jaderberg spoke with Elad Gil about the company's approach to transforming drug discovery from empirical trial and error into a computational discipline. Jaderberg noted that traditional drug development is slow and costly, and argued that success with models like AlphaFold demonstrates how deep learning can generalize in biology. He explained that Isomorphic focuses on building disease-agnostic drug design engines capable of handling various target classes and modalities. According to Jaderberg, scaling AI models allows them to compound learnings and achieve generalizability, which could increase the probability of success in real-world clinical trials. He shared his perspective that accurate molecular engineering may eventually reduce the reliance on animal testing through in-silico models and could enable sophisticated multidrug cocktails targeting complex biological pathways. Jaderberg also discussed the long-term outlook for treating diseases and improving human healthspan as computational optimization advances.
