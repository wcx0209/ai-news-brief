Novo’s CagriSema drives more than 20% weight loss at lower doses

BioSpace Drug Development
Source published 2026-11-16 22:53 SGT
Media report

Novo Nordisk presented new data at ObesityWeek showing that lower doses of its next-gen obesity drug CagriSema achieved over 20% weight loss in a Phase 3 trial. This development helps rebuild confidence as the company awaits a crucial FDA decision by the end of the year, following earlier investor disappointments.

Expand summary

Clinical Trial Results

Novo Nordisk reported data from the Phase 3 REDEFINE 9 trial at the ObesityWeek 2026 conference. Patients receiving weekly doses of 1 mg and 1.7 mg of CagriSema lost 21% and 20.7% of their body weight, respectively, at week 68. These findings demonstrate that meaningful efficacy can be achieved below the previously studied highest dose.

Background and Market Context

CagriSema combines the amylin analog cagrilintide and the GLP-1 receptor agonist semaglutide. Earlier data from the REDEFINE 1 trial showed 22.7% weight loss at higher doses, which initially disappointed investors who had expected even higher figures. Despite previous setbacks, including failing to beat Eli Lilly’s Zepbound in a head-to-head test, Novo Nordisk continues to position the drug as a major next-generation therapy.

Regulatory Outlook and Tolerability

The latest findings form part of a broader data presentation as the drug awaits a U.S. FDA decision expected by the end of 2026. Analysts noted that tolerability remained consistent with previous studies, showing common side effects such as nausea and vomiting, while the company prepares to leverage its extensive clinical data to enter the market.

Biogen inks $411M neurodegeneration pact with protein degrader biotech TrimTech

Fierce Biotech
Source published 2026-11-16 23:00 SGT
Media report

Biogen has partnered with TrimTech in a $411 million deal to develop protein degraders for neurodegenerative diseases like Alzheimer's and Parkinson's. TrimTech will use its TRIM21-based TRIMTAC and TRIMGLUE platforms to clear disease-causing protein aggregates, marking a major validation for the young biotech.

Expand summary

Collaboration Details

Biogen has entered into a biobucks agreement with U.K.-based TrimTech worth up to $411 million, which includes $32 million in upfront and research milestone payments. Under the pact, Biogen secures an exclusive global license to small molecule protein degraders developed through TrimTech's proprietary platform.

Technology and Targets

TrimTech focuses on using the E3-ligase TRIM21 to clear disease-causing protein aggregates. Its approach features bispecific chimeric molecules called TRIMTAC and mutual binding facilitator TRIMGLUE, offering an alternative to standard PROTACs. The company targets neurodegenerative conditions such as Huntington’s, Alzheimer’s, and Parkinson’s.

Industry Context

Founded in 2023, TrimTech previously raised a $47 million seed round involving major players like Johnson & Johnson, Eli Lilly, and Pfizer. This new agreement reinforces the growing interest from major drugmakers in targeted protein degradation for challenging diseases.

Johnson & Johnson to buy Ollin Biosciences for $2.2 billion upfront and partners with Blackstone

BioSpace Drug Development
Source published 2026-11-16 23:21 SGT
Media report

Johnson & Johnson is acquiring Ollin Biosciences for $2.2 billion upfront to strengthen its ophthalmology pipeline. At the same time, the company is partnering with Blackstone Life Sciences, which will provide $750 million to fully fund the Phase 3 clinical trial of Ollin's leading retinal disease antibody.

Expand summary

Acquisition Details

Johnson & Johnson is paying $2.2 billion upfront to acquire Ollin Biosciences, alongside up to $1.125 billion in potential milestone payments. This move comes as J&J aims to rebuild its ophthalmology pipeline following recent setbacks, including handing back rights to a failed gene therapy and axing an experimental geographic atrophy treatment.

Clinical Asset

The central asset in the buyout is Ollin’s OLN324, a next-generation bispecific antibody targeting retinal diseases like wet age-related macular degeneration and diabetic macular edema. According to Ollin, mid-stage trial data showed that OLN324 demonstrated faster and greater improvements in retinal anatomy and vision gains compared to a major competing therapy.

Blackstone Funding Partnership

To support the late-stage development of OLN324, J&J has enlisted private equity firm Blackstone Life Sciences. Blackstone will pay J&J $750 million over several years to fully fund the upcoming Phase 3 trials, in exchange for regulatory and sales milestone payments plus single-digit royalties.

Helus' Psychedelic Depression Drug Succeeds in Phase 3 Trial But Faces Safety Scrutiny

STAT Biotech
Source published 2026-11-16 21:07 SGT
Media report

According to Helus Pharma, its psychedelic drug candidate HLP003 significantly reduced major depression symptoms in a Phase 3 clinical trial. However, the study also recorded a number of serious adverse events, casting doubt on the overall safety of the treatment.

Expand summary

Helus Pharma announced that its psychedelic drug candidate, HLP003, showed positive results in a Phase 3 clinical trial for major depression. In the study, participants received one dose on the first day and a second dose three weeks later alongside standard antidepressants. At six weeks, the treatment group achieved a 19.5-point reduction on the MADRS depression rating scale, compared to an 11.9-point reduction in the placebo group. At 12 weeks, the effect was largely sustained, with a 16.9-point reduction for the treatment group versus 9.9 points for the placebo group, with all differences being statistically significant.

Despite the promising efficacy data, the trial recorded a number of serious adverse events. These safety concerns have raised questions regarding the risk-benefit profile of utilizing psychedelics for psychiatric conditions, a therapeutic avenue currently being explored by multiple companies in the biotechnology sector.

Versant and Lilly back new startup Seawolf Therapeutics with $100 million for viral-free gene therapy

STAT Biotech
Source published 2026-11-16 18:00 SGT
Media report

Versant Ventures and Eli Lilly have invested $100 million into a new startup named Seawolf Therapeutics. The company aims to solve a major headache in gene therapy by delivering genes without viruses, avoiding the immune system limits and side effects tied to traditional virus-based methods.

Expand summary

A new biotech startup called Seawolf Therapeutics has launched with $100 million in funding from investors including Versant Ventures and Eli Lilly, alongside a partnership with Sanofi. According to the report by STAT, the company's core mission is to tackle one of the most stubborn hurdles in genetic medicine: delivering therapeutic genes without relying on viruses.

Traditional gene therapies often use viruses as delivery vehicles, which have advanced many clinical trials for rare diseases. However, these virus-based methods carry significant drawbacks, such as triggering antibodies that prevent patients from receiving more than a single dose, as well as severe side effects at high doses.
