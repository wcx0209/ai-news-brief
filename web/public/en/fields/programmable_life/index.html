Isomorphic Labs raises record $5B in Series C funding for AI drug discovery

BioSpace Drug Development
Source published 2026-11-17 23:52 SGT
Media report
AI

Alphabet-backed AI drug designer Isomorphic Labs has secured $5 billion in a Series C funding round, breaking the record for the largest private fundraise in biotechnology history. The fresh capital will be used to scale its AI simulation models and advance its internal oncology and immunology pipelines toward clinical trials.

Expand summary

According to BioSpace, AI-driven drug designer Isomorphic Labs has clinched $5 billion in Series C funding, more than doubling its $2.1 billion Series B raised in May and surpassing Altos Labs' previous $3 billion record set in 2022. The round was co-led by Greenoaks and Sequoia Capital, with continued backing from parent company Alphabet.

Founder and CEO Demis Hassabis stated that the new funds will accelerate the company's progress in building foundational biology models and driving therapeutic programs toward clinical trials. Although the company has not yet publicly disclosed any specific clinical assets, it reports having produced preclinical results for internal programs in oncology and immunology, alongside ongoing research partnerships with major pharmaceutical companies like Novartis, Eli Lilly, and Johnson & Johnson.

Vertex Awaits FDA Verdict on Key Kidney Disease Drug

BioSpace Drug Development
Source published 2026-11-17 23:40 SGT
Media report

Vertex Pharmaceuticals is waiting for an FDA decision this month on povetacicept, a potential multi-billion-dollar therapy for IgA nephropathy. Analysts say this approval could open a new growth avenue beyond its heavily reliant cystic fibrosis business.

Expand summary

Vertex Pharmaceuticals relies heavily on its cystic fibrosis portfolio, which generated the vast majority of its $3.44 billion revenue in the third quarter. To diversify its revenue base, CEO Reshma Kewalramani has pursued both internal innovation and external deals, notably acquiring Alpine Immune Sciences for $4.9 billion in 2024 to secure the fusion protein therapeutic povetacicept.

According to clinical data from the Phase 3 RAINIER study, povetacicept demonstrated a strong reduction in proteinuria compared to a placebo. While analysts are optimistic about its efficacy and convenient monthly dosing, some investors have raised safety concerns regarding a drop in IgG levels and associated infection risks. The FDA is expected to make its decision by November 30.

Sellas' experimental blood cancer immunotherapy succeeds in clinical trial

STAT Biotech
Source published 2026-11-17 20:00 SGT
Media report

Sellas Life Sciences reports that its experimental immunotherapy, galinpepimut-S, reduced the risk of death by 41% compared to standard treatment in a Phase 3 study for advanced acute myeloid leukemia. This unexpected positive result met the primary goal of the trial, reversing prior skepticism from industry analysts.

Expand summary

According to Sellas Life Sciences, its experimental blood cancer immunotherapy drug called galinpepimut-S achieved the main goal in a Phase 3 clinical trial. The treatment successfully reduced the risk of death by 41% compared with standard care for patients suffering from an advanced stage of acute myeloid leukemia, a type of blood cancer.

This outcome comes as a surprise to industry observers. Previously, biotech columnists and analysts had publicly predicted that the long-delayed study would end in failure and fail to prolong patient survival. The company's latest announcement marks a notable turn of events for the experimental therapy.

Merck Scraps Centerpiece of $2.7B ArQule Buy After Late-Stage Safety Concerns

BioSpace Drug Development
Source published 2026-11-18 22:58 SGT
Media report

Merck is ending development of the oral BTK inhibitor nemtabrutinib after a benefit-risk review by an external data monitoring committee, leading to the cancellation of multiple Phase 3 studies under its BELLWAVE program. The drug was the core asset of Merck's $2.7 billion acquisition of ArQule in late 2019, dealing a major blow to the pharma giant's efforts to enter the non-covalent BTK inhibitor race.

Expand summary

According to BioSpace, Merck is completely shutting down its BELLWAVE clinical development program for nemtabrutinib (also known as MK-1026 or ARQ 531) following a recommendation from an external data monitoring committee. The committee reviewed findings from the late-stage BELLWAVE-011 trial in untreated chronic lymphocytic leukemia and small lymphocytic lymphoma, concluding that the potential benefits might not outweigh the risks. Consequently, Merck is also terminating the BELLWAVE-008 and BELLWAVE-010 trials.

Originally acquired as the centerpiece of the $2.7 billion ArQule buy at the end of 2019, nemtabrutinib was meant to establish Merck in the non-covalent BTK inhibitor space, where competitors like Eli Lilly have already secured regulatory approvals with drugs like Jaypirca. This discontinuation comes as Merck prepares for upcoming patent expiries for Keytruda starting in 2028, which puts billions in revenue at risk, though analysts remain generally optimistic about its other pipeline candidates.

AriBio Reports AR1001 Slows Alzheimer's Decline in Phase 3 Trial

BioSpace Drug Development
Source published 2026-11-17 22:36 SGT
Media report

At the 2026 CTAD conference, AriBio reported that its oral drug candidate AR1001 slowed cognitive decline by 31% in early Alzheimer's patients when used as a monotherapy. However, the drug did not achieve statistically significant results in the overall trial population.

Expand summary

AriBio presented data from its Phase 3 POLARIS-AD trial at the Clinical Trials on Alzheimer’s Disease conference, evaluating the oral drug candidate AR1001. The trial randomized 1,535 early Alzheimer's patients to receive AR1001 or placebo daily for 52 weeks.

The study utilized dual primary endpoints. While the overall population showed a non-significant 7% slowing of decline, a prespecified subpopulation of 604 patients receiving AR1001 as monotherapy achieved a 31% slowing of clinical decline compared to placebo. AR1001 works by inhibiting PDE5, aiming to protect neurons and increase blood flow, and showed favorable trends on secondary cognitive measures without the brain swelling risks linked to some anti-amyloid therapies.
