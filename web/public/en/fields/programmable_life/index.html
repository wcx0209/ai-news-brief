AlphaGenome 2 predicts how DNA changes affect specific human cells

Google DeepMind
Source published 2026-11-11 23:00 SGT
Official release

According to Google DeepMind: Google has announced AlphaGenome 2, an AI model that predicts how DNA sequence variations impact specific human cell types and disease states. Trained on over 100 million individual cell profiles, the model identifies twice as many causal variants as its predecessor, helping scientists uncover the cellular roots of various diseases.

Expand summary

According to Google DeepMind:

AlphaGenome 2 is a next-generation DNA sequence model designed to bridge the gap between single-cell biology and human genetics. Unlike the original version which analyzed broad tissues in parallel, AlphaGenome 2 uses a conditional architecture to evaluate how a million-letter DNA sequence behaves in one specific cell type or state at a time. Trained on over 107 million individual cell profiles from humans and mice, the model can predict gene expression, chromatin accessibility, and DNA methylation across specialized cells. It can also generalize to unseen cell states, such as those triggered by diseases or vaccinations, without requiring retraining.

In benchmark tests, AlphaGenome 2 successfully identified nearly twice as many causal variants and directional gene effects compared to previous tools. It has already demonstrated the ability to pinpoint complex disease drivers, such as identifying a regulatory variant that overactivates the ETS2 gene in gut immune cells to cause inflammatory bowel disease. The tool is now available for non-commercial research via an API, with commercial access offered in private preview, and future integration planned for Google's agentic development platform.

Biopharma industry looks beyond GLP-1s for next-gen weight loss treatments

BioSpace Drug Development
Source published 2026-11-13 21:15 SGT
Media report

At the ObesityWeek 2026 conference, biopharma companies are shifting attention beyond standard GLP-1 drugs to explore new pathways like amylin modulation. Industry experts emphasize that the next generation of weight loss therapies will focus heavily on improving treatment persistence and the quality of weight reduction, particularly preserving muscle mass.

Expand summary

Amylin Pipeline Competition

As existing GLP-1 treatments like Wegovy and Zepbound face challenges regarding side effects and plateauing results, amylin analogs are emerging as a primary alternative. Companies such as Novo Nordisk, Eli Lilly, and a partnership between Roche and Zealand Pharma are showcasing data for their pipeline assets, balancing higher efficacy against tolerability trade-offs.

Muscle Preservation and Quality

Beyond raw weight loss numbers, companies are prioritizing body composition. Biohaven and Wave Life Sciences are presenting data on therapies designed to target myostatin and INHBE proteins, aiming to reduce fat while preserving lean muscle, addressing a major drawback of current incretin drugs.

Persistence and Long-Term Value

Industry leaders note that roughly half of patients drop out of treatment within six months. Presentations at the conference, including Rhythm Pharmaceuticals' long-term data, highlight the growing importance of treatment durability and persistence in determining the true commercial and clinical value of obesity therapeutics.

Delix raises $100M for depression drug without psychedelic trips

Fierce Biotech
Source published 2026-11-13 22:02 SGT
Media report

Delix Therapeutics has secured $100 million in Series C funding to advance zalsupindole, a non-hallucinatory psychedelic therapy designed to treat major depressive disorder. Backed by Johnson & Johnson, the company plans to start a Phase 2b clinical trial in 2027.

Expand summary

Background

Delix Therapeutics, a Boston-based biotech born from a University of California lab in 2019, focuses on developing neuroplastogens, a class of drugs aimed at regrowing lost brain connections without causing traditional psychedelic trips.

Details

The newly raised $100 million Series C round was led by J&J's venture arm, with participation from several other investors. The funds will primarily advance its lead oral candidate, zalsupindole, which is based on a naturally occurring compound found in plants and toad secretions. Delix reported positive Phase 1b safety data in healthy volunteers in late 2024 and aims to launch a Phase 2b trial for major depressive disorder in the first half of 2027.

Impact

Beyond depression, the capital will support the pipeline's expansion into other neurological and neurodegenerative conditions, such as schizophrenia and Alzheimer's disease. The deal arrives amid renewed industry momentum, highlighted by recent high-profile acquisitions and regulatory focus on psychedelic-based mental health treatments.

PepGen shares almost double as DM1 oligonucleotide achieves 'best in class' splicing

BioSpace Drug Development
Source published 2026-11-11 23:23 SGT
Media report

PepGen's experimental drug for myotonic dystrophy type 1 achieved a record splicing correction of 46.5% in a mid-stage clinical trial, outperforming competitors and causing its stock price to nearly double. The positive safety profile and potential for higher doses offer new hope for treating this rare genetic disease.

Expand summary

PepGen announced that its experimental oligonucleotide PGN-EDODM1 achieved a mean splicing correction of 46.5% at week 13 in a Phase 2 trial for myotonic dystrophy type 1 (DM1). This result significantly outperforms competitors like Dyne Therapeutics, Novartis, and Ionis, which have typically achieved less than 30% correction. Analysts praised the outcome as a best-case scenario, and the news sent PepGen's stock soaring 92% to $4.61.

Beyond genetic metrics, investigators noted meaningful clinical improvements in patients' muscle control, mobility, and grip strength. The data came from the lowest dose group of 10 mg/kg, and a higher 12.5 mg/kg dose arm is currently being evaluated, with results expected in the first half of 2027. Despite a lingering partial clinical hold from the FDA regarding animal safety data, PepGen successfully advanced trials internationally and launched a $125 million public offering to fund further development.

Merck’s MASH drug efinopegdutide succeeds in mid-stage trial

Fierce Biotech
Source published 2026-11-13 22:02 SGT
Media report

Merck reports that its experimental drug efinopegdutide successfully treated MASH (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis) in a mid-stage clinical trial. The drug outperformed a placebo by helping clear the liver disease in over half of the tested patients without worsening liver scarring.

Expand summary

Clinical Trial Results

Merck revealed data from its phase 2b trial involving 386 patients with MASH and stage F2 or F3 fibrosis. Participants received one of three doses of efinopegdutide or a placebo for 52 weeks. The trial met its primary endpoint, showing MASH resolution in 56% to 63% of patients across the different doses, compared to 13% for the placebo arm.

Drug Mechanism and Context

Efinopegdutide is a subcutaneously injected co-agonist targeting both GLP-1 and glucagon receptors. Merck included a research arm using Novo Nordisk's semaglutide, where 53% achieved MASH resolution. Merck originally licensed the drug from Hanmi Pharmaceutical in 2020 after it passed through Johnson & Johnson's pipeline.

Regeneron's antibody curbs POTS heart rate spikes in mid-stage trial

BioSpace Drug Development
Source published 2026-11-13 21:59 SGT
Media report

Regeneron announced that its experimental monoclonal antibody, baloncibart, met its primary goal in a Phase 2 trial for postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome, a chronic condition with no FDA-approved treatments. Patients receiving a single high dose experienced a mean drop in heart rate of 11.4 beats per minute compared to placebo, paving the way for a Phase 3 study planned for early 2027.

Expand summary

Clinical Trial Results

Regeneron reported data from its Phase 2 Pinnacle trial evaluating baloncibart, also known as REGN7544, in 82 patients with postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome (POTS). Patients receiving a 450-mg injection experienced a mean drop in heart rate of 11.4 beats per minute compared to placebo at day eight, while a 150-mg dose reduced heart rate by 9.5 beats per minute. The high-dose group also showed sustained heart rate reductions and reported improvements in physical functioning at day 29.

Mechanism and Development

POTS is a chronic autonomic nervous system disorder associated with lightheadedness and fatigue, with no currently approved treatments. Baloncibart is an NPR1-blocking monoclonal antibody designed to increase blood volume in circulation. Regeneron plans to launch a Phase 3 study in the first half of 2027, building on this mid-stage success.

Boehringer plans 2027 survodutide submission after positive heart safety trial

Fierce Biotech
Source published 2026-11-13 07:27 SGT
Media report

Boehringer Ingelheim reports that its obesity drug survodutide has successfully met its primary endpoint in a late-stage cardiovascular safety trial, showing no increased risk of major heart-related events. The company plans to submit for regulatory approval next year, positioning the therapy for patients looking to lose metabolic fat while preserving muscle, despite higher gastrointestinal side effect discontinuation rates compared to some rivals.

Expand summary

Boehringer Ingelheim presented results from the Synchronize-CVOT study at the American Heart Association Scientific Sessions, showing that survodutide achieved a hazard ratio of 0.89 in comparing major adverse cardiovascular events against placebo among 5,530 adult participants. The dual glucagon and GLP-1 receptor agonist also met secondary endpoints regarding cardiometabolic health markers.

While the drug demonstrated a meaningful weight loss of up to 13.7% at Week 72, it faces intense competition from established blockbusters like Eli Lilly's Zepbound. Furthermore, the trial noted higher discontinuation rates due to gastrointestinal adverse events, ranging between 19.3% and 20.8% across treatment arms, though company representatives express confidence that flexible dosing adjustments in future practice will mitigate this issue.

Boehringer is pursuing a dual-path regulatory strategy, aiming to seek approval for both obesity and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), with the initial weight loss application targeted for submission next year.

Faeth’s oral cancer combo clears phase 2, challenging big drug injection therapies

Fierce Biotech
Source published 2026-11-11 22:47 SGT
Media report

Faeth Therapeutics reported that its oral cancer therapy combination, called Piktor, hit key efficacy goals in a phase 2 trial for endometrial cancer. The drug achieved a 33% confirmed overall response rate, outperforming expectations and offering a pill-based alternative to injectable treatments like ADCs and chemotherapies.

Expand summary

Faeth Therapeutics tested its combination therapy, named Piktor—consisting of serabelisib and sapanisertib—alongside intravenous paclitaxel in 42 patients with advanced or recurrent endometrial cancer. The trial yielded a 33% confirmed overall response rate and a preliminary median progression-free survival of 7.4 months. These results surpass the 30% response rate benchmark set by market analysts, rivaling standard injectable options like immunotherapies and antibody-drug conjugates.

While major pharmaceutical companies heavily focus on injectable antibody-drug conjugates that often rely on topoisomerase I inhibitors, patient resistance remains a challenge. Faeth's oral approach targets the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway at multiple points, offering a distinct mechanism of action. The company now plans to discuss a randomized phase 3 trial with the FDA and expects to release data from an ongoing breast cancer study next year.

No Priors
Source published 2026-11-12 19:00 SGT
Interview
AI

In a podcast conversation with Elad Gil, Isomorphic Labs president Max Jaderberg discusses the company's vision to shift drug discovery into a process of precision engineering through predictive AI models. Jaderberg highlights how AlphaFold's success served as a proof of concept for deep learning in biology, and predicts that compounding learnings at scale will improve clinical trial success rates and speed, potentially reducing animal testing and extending human healthspan.

Expand summary

Isomorphic Labs president Max Jaderberg spoke with Elad Gil about the company's approach to transforming drug discovery from empirical trial and error into a computational discipline. Jaderberg noted that traditional drug development is slow and costly, and argued that success with models like AlphaFold demonstrates how deep learning can generalize in biology. He explained that Isomorphic focuses on building disease-agnostic drug design engines capable of handling various target classes and modalities.

According to Jaderberg, scaling AI models allows them to compound learnings and achieve generalizability, which could increase the probability of success in real-world clinical trials. He shared his perspective that accurate molecular engineering may eventually reduce the reliance on animal testing through in-silico models and could enable sophisticated multidrug cocktails targeting complex biological pathways. Jaderberg also discussed the long-term outlook for treating diseases and improving human healthspan as computational optimization advances.

Cardiovascular Drug Development Faces Major Setbacks and Upcoming Milestones

BioSpace Drug Development
Source published 2026-11-13 03:01 SGT
Media report

BioSpace reviews recent cardiovascular drug trials, noting that several late-stage failures have questioned traditional targets like IL-6 and lipoprotein(a). Despite these setbacks, successes from companies like Eli Lilly and Merck, along with upcoming late-stage data, offer hope for future treatments.

Expand summary

BioSpace reports on a turbulent period for cardiovascular drug development, highlighted by a series of late-stage clinical trial failures. According to Oppenheimer analysts, the sector experienced six clinical failures within nine weeks. Notable setbacks include Novo Nordisk's IL-6 inhibitor ziltivekimab, which failed to reduce cardiovascular risks, and Novartis's antisense therapy pelacarsen, which lowered Lp(a) levels without generating clinical benefit. Additionally, AstraZeneca and Ionis Pharmaceuticals reported a disappointing Phase 3 result for their TTR silencer Wainua.

Despite these challenges, the sector has seen positive developments. The FDA approved Eli Lilly's Mounjaro to lower cardiovascular risks in type 2 diabetes patients, and Merck secured approval for an oral PCSK9 inhibitor. Furthermore, early-stage trials showed promising results for Verve's base editor VERVE-102 in lowering LDL cholesterol.

Looking ahead, the fourth quarter brings critical readouts. NewAmsterdam Pharma anticipates Phase 3 data for its CETP inhibitor obicetrapib, while Johnson & Johnson and Bristol Myers Squibb await results from a Phase 3 trial of the oral Factor XIa inhibitor milvexian for secondary stroke prevention.
