Robotics enters its post-Jensen Huang era

The Robot Report
Source published 2026-11-20 11:06 SGT
Media report

NVIDIA's board accepted Jensen Huang's resignation, leaving the robotics industry without one of its most prominent advocates and financial backers. While Huang helped elevate robotics through keynote spotlights, development infrastructure, and strategic investments, his departure and potential shifts in NVIDIA's priorities test the young industry's momentum.

Expand summary

Jensen Huang's departure following his resignation from NVIDIA marks a turning point for the robotics industry, where he served as a central champion for physical AI. Over the years, Huang used his keynote platforms to showcase humanoid robots, fund struggling startups like Serve Robotics, and build a comprehensive development stack spanning GPUs, simulation tools like Omniverse, and foundation models like GR00T.

Industry leaders acknowledge that Huang's steady attention and willingness to invest in early-stage markets made it significantly easier for robotics founders to secure funding and credibility. However, with interim CEO Colette Kress at the helm and potential pressures from data center competition and regulatory scrutiny, the future of NVIDIA's direct support, keynote spotlights, and strategic investments in robotics remains uncertain.

Despite these concerns, many experts believe the robotics flywheel is already spinning on its own, supported by alternative hardware competitors and widespread developer momentum. Yet, as humanoid developers face mounting pressure to prove actual commercial use cases and secure future funding over the next year, the loss of Huang's unique charisma and institutional backing leaves a notable void in the ecosystem.

What industrial drone deployments reveal about real-world autonomy challenges

The Robot Report
Source published 2026-11-20 01:01 SGT
Media report

Deploying autonomous drones in industrial facilities exposes real-world challenges where subsystems like navigation, obstacle detection, and flight control interact unpredictably. ZenaDrone contributor Jonathan Metzelaar explains that metal structures, signal blockages, and wind gusts create simultaneous disturbances that lab tests rarely capture. Reliable industrial autonomy requires understanding how uncertainties propagate across the entire system rather than relying on isolated component performance.

Expand summary

Industrial facilities present complex environments that severely test autonomous drone navigation. GNSS signals can be blocked or distorted by metallic structures, while repetitive pipes and reflective surfaces confuse visual and lidar sensors. Furthermore, wind gusts near buildings introduce aerodynamic disturbances that challenge flight controllers.

ZenaDrone contributor Jonathan Metzelaar notes that autonomy performance depends heavily on the interactions between subsystems whose assumptions are rarely independent. Localization errors can corrupt obstacle coordinates, and processing latency directly translates into physical braking distance, meaning an obstacle avoidance system must be judged on end-to-end timing rather than detection range alone. Additionally, successfully flying a route does not guarantee that collected inspection data, such as thermal imagery or photogrammetry, will be accurate or repeatable.

To ensure safe and reliable deployments, engineering teams must move beyond isolated subsystem tests and evaluate their systems under combined failure scenarios. This includes defining measurable acceptance criteria for localization, latency, and failure recovery, while maintaining clear operational boundaries and supervisory human oversight.

Soft robotic suit helps older adults stand up and walk more easily

Robohub
Source published 2026-11-19 18:09 SGT
Media report
AI

Researchers at the Technical University of Munich have developed a soft, lightweight exosuit that uses machine learning and artificial tendons to help older adults stand up and walk. In a study of ten older participants, the suit helped them complete sit-and-stand cycles faster and reduced walking energy costs, while users still felt in full control of their movements.

Expand summary

How the exosuit works

Developed by researchers at the Technical University of Munich, the exosuit functions like a wearable system with thigh straps, a waist belt, and artificial tendons connected to a backpack-like motor unit. Sensors on the thighs track leg movements in real time, and a machine learning algorithm triggers the motor to pull on the tendons, providing hip support exactly when the user stands up or walks.

Study results and user feedback

In tests involving ten older adults aged 69 to 85, the suit improved mobility significantly. Participants completed up to four additional sit-and-stand cycles in one minute, and their average walking metabolic cost dropped by 14%. According to researcher Dr. Xiaohui Zhang, users also reported a high sense of personal agency, feeling that the suit acted like a regular piece of clothing rather than an intrusive machine.
