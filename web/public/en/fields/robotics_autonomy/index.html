Media report

At its recent tech day, XPeng announced that its humanoid robot IRON delivered an unscripted speech, and its second-generation VLA technology is expanding into L4 Robotaxi operations and subscription-based driving assistance. These developments mark a significant shift as the company transitions its core autonomous driving capabilities into broader commercial applications.

Expand summary

XPeng's latest technology showcase highlighted significant advancements across autonomous driving and robotics. The company reported that its humanoid robot IRON walked on stage and completed an unscripted bilingual speech without manual remote control, with hardware mass production slated for January 2027 and initial deployment as store guides expected in the first quarter. According to the company, it took just eleven months from business launch to securing L4 demonstration operation qualifications, with public testing already underway and a custom terminal model, YOYO-01, planned for 2027. Additionally, XPeng plans to introduce subscription services for its Turing AI driving system starting in the second quarter of 2027, while pushing out OTA updates that enhance battery charging efficiency and active safety features.

Why China's Embodied AI Data Gold Rush Struggles to Take Off

TMTPost 钛媒体
Source published 2026-11-26 09:34 SGT
Media report
AI

Training robots requires massive real-world data, sparking a global data collection gold rush. While the US has built a clear supply chain and independent financing model for data providers, China's data collection mostly relies on local government projects and internal tech incubation, lacking independent valuation and facing high contract compliance risks.

Expand summary

As embodied AI models rapidly evolve, training robots requires millions of hours of real-world video and physical interaction data. In the United States, startups like Maxinsights and Mecka have secured major funding rounds and achieved massive revenue growth because tech giants and VCs view data collection as a distinct, critical supply chain layer for physical AI. Meanwhile, Chinese data collection companies face three major hurdles: the inability to independently validate data value, heavy compliance and payment collection risks, and a lack of independent capital exit pathways. Although China's national data agency initiated national standards for embodied AI data to unify metrics, the domestic industry remains split between internal tech incubation and a fragmented market.

Whole-Body Mobile Manipulation with ROS 2: Integrating MoveIt2, PINK, and Controller Chaining

Clearpath Robotics
Source published 2026-11-26 23:21 SGT
Official release

According to Clearpath Robotics: At ROSCon 2026, engineers presented a practical approach to achieve whole-body control for mobile manipulators using ROS 2. By combining MoveIt2 and the Python Inverse Kinematics solver PINK, robots can coordinate their mobile base and robotic arm simultaneously, bridging the gap between trajectory planning and velocity execution.

Expand summary

According to Clearpath Robotics:

Background and Motivation

Traditionally, mobile manipulators control their mobile base and robotic arm independently using separate frameworks like Nav2 and MoveIt2. At ROSCon 2026, Stephanie Eng and Luis Camero demonstrated a whole-body planning approach that treats the base and arm as a single system. This allows robots to reach targets more efficiently and execute coordinated motions across all available degrees of freedom.

Technical Implementation

To overcome the limitation of MoveIt2's default KDL solver, which cannot handle multi-degree-of-freedom planar joints, the team integrated PINK (Python Inverse Kinematics) via a ROS service interface. For execution, they leveraged ROS 2 controller chaining to translate joint trajectories from MoveIt into velocity commands for the mobile base, ensuring smooth operation across coordinate frames.

Requirements and Availability

While this method enables collision-free whole-body motion, it requires accurate 3D collision models of the environment rather than standard 2D LiDAR mapping. Full details and simulation examples are available in the open-source clearpath-community-demos repository.
