Robotics enters its post-Jensen Huang era

The Robot Report
Source published 2026-11-20 11:06 SGT
Media report

NVIDIA's board accepted Jensen Huang's resignation, leaving the robotics industry without one of its most prominent advocates and financial backers. While Huang helped elevate robotics through keynote spotlights, development infrastructure, and strategic investments, his departure and potential shifts in NVIDIA's priorities test the young industry's momentum.

Expand summary

Jensen Huang's departure following his resignation from NVIDIA marks a turning point for the robotics industry, where he served as a central champion for physical AI. Over the years, Huang used his keynote platforms to showcase humanoid robots, fund struggling startups like Serve Robotics, and build a comprehensive development stack spanning GPUs, simulation tools like Omniverse, and foundation models like GR00T.

Industry leaders acknowledge that Huang's steady attention and willingness to invest in early-stage markets made it significantly easier for robotics founders to secure funding and credibility. However, with interim CEO Colette Kress at the helm and potential pressures from data center competition and regulatory scrutiny, the future of NVIDIA's direct support, keynote spotlights, and strategic investments in robotics remains uncertain.

Despite these concerns, many experts believe the robotics flywheel is already spinning on its own, supported by alternative hardware competitors and widespread developer momentum. Yet, as humanoid developers face mounting pressure to prove actual commercial use cases and secure future funding over the next year, the loss of Huang's unique charisma and institutional backing leaves a notable void in the ecosystem.

GPT-6.2 Astra doubles robotics task success rate

QbitAI 量子位
Source published 2026-11-21 14:35 SGT
Media report
AI

According to XDOF, OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Astra achieved a 41.2% success rate across 16 robot operating tasks, more than double its predecessor. The model notably cleared previously unpassed tasks like tying knots and unfolding T-shirts, showcasing major progress in handling soft objects and complex physical reasoning.

Expand summary

In recent tests conducted by robotics infrastructure firm XDOF, OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Astra demonstrated significant improvements in embodied AI tasks. Using six YAM workstations for parallel testing with strict controls to filter out hardware bias, XDOF found that GPT-6.2 Astra's average success rate across 16 standardized Ember-0 benchmark tasks rose from 19.6% to 41.2%.

Key Breakthroughs

The most notable achievement is the model's ability to successfully complete soft-object manipulation tasks such as tying knots and unfolding T-shirts, which previously had a 0% success rate across 143 trials. Sub-tasks requiring fine force control, spatial reasoning, and tool use, like picking up bread with a clip, also saw dramatic accuracy improvements from 10% to 90%.

Speed and Industry Impact

Despite improvements in lowering the average prediction delay from 15 seconds to 10.1 seconds, latency remains a hurdle for real-time robotic response. Nevertheless, researchers including NUS Assistant Professor Jiafei Duan noted the model's exceptional performance in real-world benchmarks, underscoring how frontier multimodal models are rapidly accelerating physical world capabilities.

The manufacturing data gap holding back physical AI

The Robot Report
Source published 2026-11-22 06:25 SGT
Media report
AI

Although robotics and physical AI companies have raised billions, deploying autonomous machines in factories remains tough because of a critical data gap. Traditional manufacturing processes rely heavily on the unspoken, hands-on experience of technicians rather than structured digital records. Without connecting process decisions to real-world production outcomes, even advanced robots struggle to learn, adapt, and improve industrial efficiency.

Expand summary

According to VulcanForms CTO Michael Kenworthy, physical AI and robotics companies have raised nearly $48 billion in 2026, but moving autonomous systems out of controlled labs and onto factory floors faces a major roadblock. Factories generate plenty of operational data, but it is often trapped in isolated systems, making it difficult to link machine parameters with final inspection results. Data from the Manufacturing Leadership Council shows that 37% of manufacturers cited data interoperability as a major smart factory hurdle in 2026, up from 22% in 2025.

To bridge this gap and prepare for physical AI, manufacturers need to focus on three key steps. First, they must capture real-world production conditions that reflect complex variables like temperature and material characteristics. Second, they need to create a unified digital thread, linking isolated machines and quality management systems together. Finally, companies must build clear feedback loops and safety boundaries around quality and repeatability, ensuring autonomous adjustments are properly validated, especially in highly regulated sectors like medical device manufacturing.

Medtronic’s Hugo surgical robot gets FDA clearance for general surgery

The Robot Report
Source published 2026-11-21 08:55 SGT
Media report

Medtronic announced that the FDA has cleared its Hugo robotic-assisted surgery system for upper and lower abdominal general surgery procedures. The company leveraged both a U.S. clinical study and clinical data from Europe to secure the broader approval, opening up millions of annual procedures in the U.S. market.

Expand summary

Medtronic reported that the U.S. Food and Drug Administration has cleared its Hugo robotic-assisted surgery system for general surgery, encompassing procedures such as cholecystectomy, gastric bypass, hernia repair, and appendectomy. According to Medtronic, these procedures represent about 3.5 million surgeries annually in the U.S.

To secure the expanded indication, Medtronic took a dual approach, conducting a U.S. clinical trial under an investigational device exemption while also leveraging CE mark data collected in Europe since 2022. Globally, Hugo is approaching 50,000 cumulative procedures.

Looking ahead, Medtronic is exploring the integration of artificial intelligence across preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative stages. The company recently gained FDA approval for its Touch Surgery Aide platform and continues to invest in surgical robotics partnerships and ongoing clinical trials for gynecological applications.
