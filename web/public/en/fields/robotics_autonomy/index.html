AI100 2026 report highlights embodied intelligence as a grand challenge

Robohub
Source published 2026-11-11 21:48 SGT
Media report

The AI100 2026 report points out that while generative AI is booming, physical AI remains a major hurdle. Despite rapid progress in robotics, bridging the gap between lab demos and real-world deployment requires solving complex physical world challenges.

Expand summary

Background

The AI100 is a long-term study that checks in every five years on how artificial intelligence is changing society. The 2026 edition looks at everything from generative AI scaling to cybersecurity, international governance, and scientific discovery.

Key Details

Experts highlighted six grand challenges, putting embodied intelligence at the top for robotics. Over the past five years, robotics has seen massive leaps by combining natural language processing with vision-language-action models. Notably, the report highlights that China leads in industrial robots and humanoid robot production.

Real-World Impact

Despite cool demos, a huge gap exists between testing capabilities and actual deployment outside controlled environments like warehouses. Overcoming the real-world complexity requires breakthroughs in long-horizon planning, dexterous manipulation, and potentially new learning paradigms.

Plurai raises $13 million to make AI agents reliable using autonomous driving tech

TechCrunch AI
Source published 2026-11-11 22:00 SGT
Media report
AI

AI agents are great at demos, but they frequently fail in real-world corporate environments because they can't handle unexpected scenarios. A startup called Plurai has raised $13 million to fix this problem by applying autonomous driving simulation techniques to business software. By creating a digital twin of a company's systems, Plurai helps train smaller, open-weight models that perform better and cost significantly less than big frontier models.

Expand summary

The Problem with AI Agents

While AI agents are generating massive hype, benchmarks show they still struggle to function without heavy supervision. Much like early autonomous driving software, agents operate probabilistically in unpredictable environments, leading to costly mistakes like ignoring corporate policies or breaking down when handling multiple datasets.

The Autonomous Driving Solution

Plurai's founders, veterans of autonomous vehicle tech, realized that AI agents face the exact same reliability hurdles that self-driving cars once did. To solve this, Plurai builds a digital twin of a company's electronic systems, mapping out potential agent workflows and generating synthetic data to prepare models for tricky edge cases.

Future Impact

Beyond simply testing agents, Plurai uses its collected data to post-train small, open-weight models tailored to specific businesses. Tests show these customized models can outperform mid-tier frontier models at a fraction of the cost, giving IT leaders a sustainable way to deploy AI without breaking the bank on massive general-purpose models.

How AI and Robotics Help Recyclers Turn Mixed Waste Into Reusable Materials

NVIDIA Generative AI
Source published 2026-11-11 11:00 SGT
Official release
AI

According to NVIDIA Generative AI: Global waste is piling up, but only a tiny fraction gets recycled due to manual labor shortages and messy materials. Startups are now using computer vision and AI to sort plastics, textiles, and household trash with incredible speed and accuracy. This breakthrough helps factories cut operating costs while keeping valuable resources in circulation.

Expand summary

According to NVIDIA Generative AI:

The Recycling Challenge

Globally, less than 7% of materials come from recycled sources, and municipal solid waste could nearly double by 2050. Traditional recycling relies heavily on hard-to-find manual labor, making it difficult to sort mixed scrap accurately and cost-effectively.

AI-Powered Solutions

Innovative startups are deploying edge AI and robotics to revolutionize recycling plants. Taiwan-based AIRECO uses vision AI to sort plastics for closed-loop recycling, aiming to triple processing capacity. EverestLabs AI operates robots on fast-moving conveyor belts across North America and Australia, identifying billions of objects. U.K.-based Greyparrot monitors waste streams in real time, replacing outdated manual sampling with instant analytics. Meanwhile, Germany's reverse.fashion automates the tedious task of sorting used textiles by analyzing fabric quality and defects at high speeds.

Industry Impact

By leveraging advanced computing, these companies are drastically improving sorting efficiency and boosting the yield of reusable materials. Major brands and recycling facilities are using these tools to reduce carbon emissions and build a more sustainable circular economy.
