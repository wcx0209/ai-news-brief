California DMV bans Waymo from state freeways

The Robot Report
Source published 2026-11-18 07:55 SGT
Media report

The California DMV has barred Waymo autonomous vehicles from operating on state freeways after vehicles repeatedly entered active construction zones and bypassed emergency responders. Waymo stated it is temporarily restricting freeway operations across all markets while working with officials to address safety deficiencies, though its city street robotaxi service continues normally.

Expand summary

The California Department of Motor Vehicles issued an order on November 11 barring Waymo autonomous vehicles from operating on state freeways. According to the DMV, the action stems from incidents where Waymo vehicles entered active construction zones on U.S. Route 101 and attempted to drive past California Highway Patrol officers during a traffic stop on Highway 280. The agency determined these occurrences pose an imminent hazard to public safety. This follows a prior safety recall in June after seven Waymo vehicles drove into closed lanes in San Francisco. Waymo stated it will restrict freeway operations across all operating areas as part of its safety-first approach and is working with officials to resume services, while its commercial ride-hailing operations on surface streets remain unaffected.

Ant Lingbot Embodied AI Challenge Concludes: How Developers Teach Robots Real-World Tasks

GeekPark 极客公园
Source published 2026-11-17 19:18 SGT
Media report
AI

The first Ant Lingbot Embodied AI Challenge finals recently concluded in Shanghai, gathering fifteen teams to tackle real-world robotic tasks using a unified open-source model. The competition showed that instead of mass data collection, smarter approaches involving foundation models and targeted fine-tuning allowed teams to achieve high success rates with minimal adjustments.

Expand summary

During the 48-hour final, fifteen teams utilized the same hardware and the open-source LingBot-VLA 2.0 model to complete tasks like sorting items and cleaning. Different backgrounds brought diverse solutions: the champion team TARS optimized training speed and handled heavy workloads, while the third-place team SST cleverly used DeepSeek and visual segmentation combined with just 13 additional data points. Meanwhile, other teams from autonomous driving and search-recommendation backgrounds discovered that protecting pre-existing capabilities during fine-tuning is crucial to prevent performance drops in dynamic environments. Ant Lingbot CEO Zhu Xing noted that embodied AI is still in its early stages, and open challenges help developers explore how foundation models can bridge the gap between simulation and the physical world.

Robot swarms trained in computer simulations successfully transfer skills to the real world

Robohub
Source published 2026-11-17 19:00 SGT
Media report

Researchers at ULB have solved the frustrating 'reality gap' in robotics by limiting what AI can control. Instead of letting reinforcement learning tinker with every tiny motor movement, they restricted it to choosing among a few proven behaviors, allowing robot swarms trained in simulation to work reliably in the real world and outperforming traditional methods that usually fall apart outside the computer.

Expand summary

Computer simulations are the safest place to teach robots new tricks, but skills learned virtually usually fall apart when machines face real-world friction, sensor noise, and mechanical imperfections, a persistent hurdle known as the reality gap. According to a study published in Science Robotics by Ilyes Gharbi, Mauro Birattari, and their team at IRIDIA, the artificial intelligence laboratory of ULB, this failure happens because overly flexible reinforcement learning algorithms overfit the simulator, learning quirks that do not exist in reality.

To fix this, the researchers restricted the learning algorithm. Rather than letting it control each wheel's speed directly, they forced it to choose moment by moment among a handful of pre-tested behaviors, such as moving toward a light, aggregating with peers, or exploring. This leaves the algorithm to focus only on what matters: deciding when to switch between these behaviors so the group coordinates effectively. In tests involving a swarm of 20 e-puck robots across 250 physical experiments and five collective missions, swarms transferred to reality reliably and significantly outperformed state-of-the-art methods that tried to control motors directly.

Can Robots Bring Stroke Surgery to Remote Hospitals?

IEEE Spectrum Robotics
Source published 2026-11-16 22:00 SGT
Media report

Remedy Robotics is developing the N1 system, a surgical robot designed to let specialists perform emergency stroke procedures remotely. Currently, only about 1 in 10 eligible patients receive timely thrombectomies due to a lack of local experts. While early human tests in Toronto show promise, making telesurgery practical requires overcoming network lag and hospital infrastructure gaps.

Expand summary

Remedy Robotics was founded by clinician David Bell and engineer Jake Sganga to tackle a stark medical reality: only about 1 in 10 eligible patients receive mechanical thrombectomies for acute ischemic strokes, largely because life-saving procedures are concentrated in major urban centers. Traditional stroke surgery requires physicians to manually thread a wire and catheter through narrow blood vessels while relying solely on a flat, two-dimensional X-ray, making it a blind and physically demanding task.

To bridge this gap, Remedy developed the N1 System, which features a steerable catheter driven by a robotic arm and guided by computer-vision software. The system translates basic directional commands from a controller into precise movements while overlaying 3D CT scans onto X-ray feeds. After successful preliminary tests on silicone models and a small nonemergency human trial in Toronto, the company recently secured $62 million in funding to advance upcoming clinical trials within the University of California system.

Despite the technological progress, widespread adoption of telesurgery faces steep hurdles. Experts point out that successful remote operations depend heavily on reliable network infrastructure, as rural areas often suffer from high latency that can disrupt surgical timing. Additionally, smaller hospitals frequently lack the necessary on-site medical staff, specialized intensive care units, and supporting equipment needed to handle complex stroke emergencies safely.
