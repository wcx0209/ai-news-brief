ESA Awards OHB €102M Contract for ClearSpace-1 Debris Removal Mission

European Spaceflight
Source published 2026-11-19 14:41 SGT
Media report

The European Space Agency has awarded German satellite maker OHB a €102 million contract to build the ClearSpace-1 spacecraft, which will capture and remove the retired Proba-1 satellite. The mission has faced previous delays and redesigns after its original target was struck by debris, and is now scheduled to launch in 2030.

Expand summary

Background and Restructuring

The European Space Agency signed the contract on 17 November at Space Tech Expo Europe in Bremen, marking the start of the implementation phase for ClearSpace-1. Originally awarded to a consortium led by Swiss startup ClearSpace in December 2020, the mission underwent major changes after its initial target was struck by space debris in August 2023. This forced a redesign, selecting Proba-1 as the new target and making OHB the prime contractor.

Launch Details

The mission's launch plans have also shifted over time. While it was once slated for a Vega C rocket in 2026, technical papers and presentations point toward a dedicated microlauncher flight. An OHB presentation featured an illustration of the RFA ONE rocket, which is notable since OHB holds a majority stake in its developer, Rocket Factory Augsburg. However, neither ESA nor OHB has officially confirmed this launch vehicle, with the overall mission timeline now targeted for 2030.
