ICEYE Launches €2.05B Takeover Bid for Italian Rocket Builder Avio

European Spaceflight
Source published 2026-11-17 16:08 SGT
Media report

Finnish satellite company ICEYE has launched a €2.05 billion all-cash takeover bid for Italian rocket builder Avio to create a unified European space and defense player. ICEYE also promised to invest €1 billion in Italy if the deal succeeds, though the transaction still faces multiple regulatory hurdles across Europe and the US.

Expand summary

Acquisition Details

Finnish satellite builder and operator ICEYE has made a €2.05 billion all-cash offer to acquire Italian rocket builder Avio through its Italian subsidiary, Iris 2. The offer of €41 per share represents a 21.3% premium over Avio's closing share price, and ICEYE intends to delist Avio from the Milan stock exchange following the acquisition.

Strategic Rationale

ICEYE CEO Rafal Modrzewski stated that the merger aims to build a European space and defense champion capable of manufacturing satellites, launching them, and providing intelligence services. The combination addresses reciprocal needs, as ICEYE requires secure launch capacity for its constellation while Avio benefits from long-term launch demand visibility.

Investment and Hurdles

Beyond the buyout, ICEYE plans to invest an additional €1 billion in Italy over five years to boost manufacturing, R&D, and local suppliers, potentially creating up to 400 jobs. The transaction will be financed through existing cash and debt, following ICEYE's recent €450 million funding round, but it still requires regulatory clearances from Italy, France, the UK, the US, and the EU.

SpaceNukes Opens New Facility to Build Next-Gen Space Nuclear Reactors

SpaceNews
Source published 2026-11-17 04:00 SGT
Media report
Manufacturing × Materials

Space Nuclear Power Corp. has opened a new manufacturing and materials facility in Los Alamos to develop its second-generation space reactor. According to CEO Andrew Phelps, the main hurdle for higher-temperature space reactors is not nuclear physics, but rather advanced materials and manufacturing techniques like 3D printing refractory alloys. The company aims to fly a nuclear reactor by 2028.

Expand summary

Facility Purpose

SpaceNukes opened its Manufacture and Materials Development Facility in Los Alamos on Nov. 13, 2026. The site focuses on manufacturing and materials development for the company's Generation 2 space reactor, including additive manufacturing of refractory alloys. No radioactive material is handled at this location.

Technical Challenges

The next-generation reactor is designed to operate at significantly higher temperatures, which allows for a much smaller radiator to shed heat. However, traditional nickel-based superalloys have reached their limits, forcing the company to transition to refractory metals such as tungsten, molybdenum, and niobium. These metals are notoriously difficult to form and machine using conventional methods.

Timeline and Context

Led by manufacturing director Omar Mireles, the facility complements the company's testing site in Ohio. This opening follows an agreement with DARPA in October 2026 to design and ground-test a Kilopower reactor, as SpaceNukes pushes toward its goal of flying a nuclear reactor by 2028.
