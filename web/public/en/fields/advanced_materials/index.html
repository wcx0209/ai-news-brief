Engineered Copper Surfaces Turn Graphene Into High-Performance Nanoscrolls

AZoM Materials News
Source published 2026-11-27 23:52 SGT
Media report

A study in Nature Communications reports that researchers used engineered copper substrates to guide graphene rolling through thermal stress. This method produces aligned nanoscrolls across four-inch wafers without chemical solvents, improving electrical and optical performance.

Expand summary

Background and Method

Researchers tackling the challenge of nanoscale fabrication in electronics focused on transforming atomic layers into complex structures. Traditional methods relying on liquid solvents often introduce contamination and lack precise orientation control. A research team developed a technique using vicinal copper substrates with uniform surface steps. By growing single-crystal graphene via chemical vapor deposition and controlling the cooling rate, they harnessed interfacial thermal stress to direct the rolling process uniformly across a four-inch wafer.

Performance and Implications

According to the study, this solvent-free approach successfully produced stacked nanoscrolls with a stable layer arrangement without causing new structural defects. Devices incorporating these nanoscrolls showed significantly enhanced photocurrent generation and higher carrier mobility at room temperature compared to standard monolayer graphene. While the findings point toward improved integrated device designs combining planar and rolled structures, manufacturing yield and reliability at scale still need further evaluation.

High-Entropy Alloys Could Make Nanoporous Metals Less Brittle

AZoM Materials News
Source published 2026-11-26 23:33 SGT
Media report

A recent study in npj Computational Materials explores how high-entropy alloys could help fix the brittle nature of nanoporous metals. By using computer simulations, researchers found that these special alloys can improve strain hardening and strength, which might lead to better lightweight and shock-absorbing materials.

Expand summary

Background and Simulation

Nanoporous metals are extremely light and have a huge surface area, making them great for absorbing energy. However, when you pull on them, they tend to snap easily because a crack in one tiny part quickly spreads to the rest. To solve this, researchers used computer models to test two types of high-entropy alloys, Al 0.1 CoCrFeNi and NbMoTaW, checking how they handle stretching and squeezing at different temperatures.

Mechanisms and Performance

The simulations showed that these alloys get their high strength from two main things: defects disappearing at the surface, and sluggish dislocation movement that helps with strain hardening. The two alloys handled stress differently because of their crystal structures. Overall, the tested high-entropy alloys showed specific yield strengths up to five times higher than typical nanoporous materials, though the researchers noted that real-world tests and lower simulation speeds are still needed to confirm these results.
