Google Research uses wearables and AI to forecast personalized health habits

Google ResearchArtificial IntelligenceResearchSource published2026-11-12 03:54 SGT
Official releaseStage not established

According to Google Research: Background Heart disease, kidney conditions, and metabolic disorders like type 2 diabetes form an interconnected web known as cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic (CKM) syndrome. These issues often develop silently years before symptoms appear, and while smartwatches easily track past activity, translating that data into meaningful, actionable guidance remains a major challenge. Framework Details Google's new framework introduces a coachable health age model, which avoids simply guessing a user's birth year. Instead, it relies on published epidemiological studies linking behaviors like resting heart rate, steps, and sleep to disease risk. It is paired with Sensor World Model, an action-conditioned model pre-trained on millions of user-weeks of data to forecast how 20 physiological markers will evolve over 12 weeks under different daily routines. Impact Evaluated across multiple cohorts and through blind tests with medical professionals, this multimodal approach combines continuous wearable data with routine blood biomarkers to map multi-organ aging. While currently an investigational research prototype, it lays the groundwork for future prospective trials aimed at helping people build lasting, healthy habits.

Google launches Gemini Decisions API for fast, low-cost multimodal choices

Google AIArtificial IntelligenceModelsSource published2026-11-12 02:00 SGT
Official releaseStage not established

According to Google AI: How it works Instead of generating answer text token by token like traditional language models, Gemini Decision 1 scores candidate options in a single forward pass. It supports text, images, audio, video, and PDFs up to 256K tokens, allowing developers to ask up to 1,000 independent questions simultaneously. Every answer includes a confidence score, letting developers route ambiguous cases to fallback systems or human reviewers. Pricing and Performance The model delivers a median latency of 150-250 ms end-to-end and is priced at $0.10 per 1M input tokens with zero output tokens billed. Google reports that it is nearly 5x cheaper than standard generative alternatives across shared benchmark suites. Real-World Applications Internal teams at Google are already using the API as a low-latency control plane. Examples include dynamic model routing in Gemini Notebook to reduce query latency by up to 40%, rapid design iteration in Stitch, instant emoji selection in Google Labs, and safety layers for Model Context Protocol tools in Google Search.

Is Claude Fable 5.2 worth it for office workers? Comparing it with Opus and Sonnet

ITmedia AI+Artificial IntelligenceModelsSource published2026-11-12 07:00 SGT
Source type unconfirmedStage not established

The article evaluates Anthropic's new flagship AI model, Claude Fable 5.2, against Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 across two typical office tasks: summarizing a 268-page government white paper into a 15-minute presentation, and conducting background research for a media interview. In the presentation-making task, Fable 5.2 followed the time constraint best, structuring the slides neatly with well-thought-out actionable takeaways and speaker notes. However, it took about 1.6 times longer and cost roughly twice as much as Opus 5.5, and 3.6 times as much as Sonnet 5.5. In the research task, all three models performed similarly well in grasping key contexts, though Fable 5.2 provided deeper localized insights at a higher cost. Third-party benchmarks indicate that Fable 5.2 leads in general intelligence but slightly trails in specific knowledge-work tasks like briefcase handling. Ultimately, while Fable 5.2 offers a more human-like output and better intent-matching for creative presentation structuring, its high cost means office workers should primarily rely on Opus 5.5 or Sonnet 5.5 for daily routines, reserving Fable 5.2 for high-stakes, heavy-duty information processing.
