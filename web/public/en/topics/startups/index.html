Plurai raises $13 million to make AI agents reliable using autonomous driving tech

TechCrunch AIArtificial IntelligenceStartupsSource published2026-11-11 22:00 SGT
Media reportStage not established
Robotics & Autonomous Systems

The Problem with AI Agents While AI agents are generating massive hype, benchmarks show they still struggle to function without heavy supervision. Much like early autonomous driving software, agents operate probabilistically in unpredictable environments, leading to costly mistakes like ignoring corporate policies or breaking down when handling multiple datasets. The Autonomous Driving Solution Plurai's founders, veterans of autonomous vehicle tech, realized that AI agents face the exact same reliability hurdles that self-driving cars once did. To solve this, Plurai builds a digital twin of a company's electronic systems, mapping out potential agent workflows and generating synthetic data to prepare models for tricky edge cases. Future Impact Beyond simply testing agents, Plurai uses its collected data to post-train small, open-weight models tailored to specific businesses. Tests show these customized models can outperform mid-tier frontier models at a fraction of the cost, giving IT leaders a sustainable way to deploy AI without breaking the bank on massive general-purpose models.

Zhengyuan Quantum Secures Funding to Tackle Thousand-Qubit Superconducting Challenges

QbitAI 量子位Quantum TechnologyStartupsSource published2026-11-10 16:33 SGT
Media reportStage not established

Background and Challenges Building quantum computers requires keeping fragile quantum states stable near absolute zero while precisely controlling them. As researchers successfully demonstrate quantum error correction where error rates drop with scale, a new bottleneck emerges: the physical limits of wiring and packaging. More qubits mean more control signals, but chip edges cannot expand indefinitely. Technical Solutions Zhengyuan Quantum is tackling this by moving connections into the third dimension, using Through-Silicon Vias (TSV) and 3D integration packaging to route signals vertically. This approach helps ease the wiring congestion at the chip's perimeter. The new funding will accelerate these hardware integration steps, along with developing cryogenic harnesses and quantum EDA design tools. Future Impact Ultimately, these engineering efforts aim to deliver reliable thousand-qubit systems that can handle complex simulations in chemistry and materials science, helping researchers model molecules and design new drugs or catalysts before expensive physical experiments.

What Is Eval-Driven Development? How to Improve AI Agents

LangChain BlogArtificial IntelligenceStartupsSource published2026-11-10 03:00 SGT
Official releaseStage not established

According to LangChain Blog: When building AI agents, developers often struggle to know if a recent change actually improved the system. Eval-driven development borrows from traditional test-driven development to solve this unpredictability. The process has three main steps: defining success clearly, writing evaluations before building, and comparing experiments iteratively. First, you create a short specification of what the agent should and shouldn't do. Next, you build a dataset of test cases, often using real user runs or synthetic data, and create evaluators using code or LLMs. Finally, you run a baseline, make your changes, and compare the side-by-side results to catch regressions and prevent overfitting.

Jonathan Kanter is using AI to take on Big Tech

The Verge AIArtificial IntelligenceStartupsSource published2026-11-09 22:30 SGT
Media reportStage not established

After stepping down as the head of the DOJ's Antitrust Division in 2024, Jonathan Kanter has pivoted to the private sector to continue his fight against illegal monopolies using an unexpected tool: artificial intelligence. Alongside former acting Antitrust Division chief Doha Mekki, Kanter launched FluxCap, a startup composed of former DOJ staffers and Palantir engineers. The company's platform uses proprietary technology and large language models to help attorneys conduct legal research, spot logical flaws, and sift through massive piles of discovery materials to uncover anticompetitive behavior. Antitrust litigation is notoriously resource-intensive. While government agencies and state attorneys general operate on tight budgets and limited headcounts, tech giants and massive corporations can deploy formidable legal teams with deep pockets. Kanter views FluxCap as a "force multiplier" that gives small legal teams the virtual capacity and manpower of dozens of lawyers working around the clock. Although Kanter stresses that technology cannot fully replace the need for government funding and increased headcount, he believes AI can serve as a great equalizer to help underdogs go toe-to-toe with powerful opponents.
