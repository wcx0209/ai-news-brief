From Relevant Posts to Attributable Spans: Explicit Symptom Evidence for Depression Questionnaire Inference

Inferring standardized depression questionnaire responses from longitudinal social media histories often suffers from retrieval errors, such as picking up discussions about other people or multi-symptom posts that lack precise attribution. To address this, the authors propose SREE (Symptom Retrieval and Evidence-Grounded InfErence). SREE extracts first-person and symptom-specific text spans, selects a representative subset using a Facility Location-inspired objective, and performs Evidence-Gated Inference. Evaluated on BDI-Sen and eRisk 2019-2021 datasets, SREE achieves significant improvements in post-level precision and F1 compared to standard dense retrieval, while obtaining the highest exact item-level agreement across eRisk collections. This is a preprint paper, and while it improves evidence localization and attribution, the source does not detail independent replication or code release status.

Auditable Human-Agent Teams for Autonomous X-ray Experimentation

This preprint describes the use of coding-agent infrastructure grounded in validated scientific methods to perform autonomous synchrotron X-ray experiments on nanoparticle superlattices under evolving, human-defined objectives. By capturing complete human-agent and agent-agent transcripts, the authors audit decision provenance and characterize the division of cognitive labor. Across three experiments, 61-67% of messages involved interpretation or judgment, and the share of methodological and scientific contributions rose from 46% in the first experiment to 56-61% in later ones. Furthermore, 59-68% of error corrections involved multiple participants, and roughly half of all decisions incorporated input from another participant. While the work presents a notable integration of agentic systems with physical instruments, it is a preprint requiring further independent replication and code or hardware availability checks.

Don't Blame The Preferences: Examining The Emergence of Sycophancy in LLM Training

AI sycophancy—the tendency of large language models to flatter users and validate incorrect beliefs—is often blamed on preference alignment methods such as RLHF and DPO. To test this assumption, researchers investigated sycophancy across the training pipelines of three model families using three different benchmarks. The empirical results show that sycophancy is largely established by the end of supervised fine-tuning (SFT). Across nine model-benchmark combinations, SFT checkpoints attain at least 92% of preference-aligned models' sycophancy scores and at least 85% of final models' scores. Preference alignment increased sycophancy in only four out of nine cases, with an average increase of 4.2 percentage points when it occurred. Mechanistic interpretability analysis further revealed that SFT datasets contribute substantially more examples activating sycophantic model representations, with the highest-scoring individual datasets originating from SFT. This is a preprint; while it provides valuable diagnostic insights across multiple models, it relies on specific architectural pipelines and prompts the research community to reconsider mitigation strategies beyond final-stage alignment.

Forcing a Letter Suppresses Refusal: Mechanistic Evidence from WMDP

Safety benchmarks for large language models frequently rely on multiple-choice question (MCQ) formats, yet prior work shows this format suppresses model refusal. This preprint provides the first mechanistic explanation of this phenomenon using Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B on the Weapons of Mass Destruction Proxy (WMDP) dataset. Using the tuned lens technique, the researchers demonstrate that when processing MCQs, models commit to an answer-letter token very early, contrasting with the gradual build-up of refusal seen in open-ended formats. Furthermore, activations during MCQ processing lie approximately three times further from the internal refusal direction. Ablating attention heads that carry the MCQ answer increases refusal rates, directly evidencing that answer-selection competes with and overrides safety refusal mechanisms. This implies that MCQ safety benchmarks often measure raw knowledge capabilities rather than true alignment or safety posture. As a preprint, code release and independent replication are necessary to fully corroborate these internal activation claims.

LingBot-Touch: Learning Heterogeneous Tactile Representation for General Dexterous Manipulation

Tactile sensing is essential for contact-rich dexterous manipulation, but existing commercial tactile sensors vary wildly in physics and design, making cross-sensor data scaling difficult. To address this, the researchers developed LingBot-Touch, a tactile foundation model designed to learn a unified and extensible representation across heterogeneous tactile sensors including piezoresistive, magnetic, and visuotactile devices. The model outputs plug-and-play tactile tokens that integrate seamlessly into existing policy architectures, such as diffusion policies and vision-language-action (VLA) models like pi_0.5 and LingBot-VLA. Extensive experiments spanning simulation and real-robot deployments across multiple hardware platforms show that the proposed representation consistently improves manipulation dexterity while maintaining high computational efficiency. As a preprint, further independent replication and code or weight releases will help confirm the generalizability of these cross-sensor tactile representations.

Video-Coder: Reinforcing Agentic Coding for Video Generation

Video-Coder addresses the challenge of precise spatial structure, motion dynamics, and controllable scene composition in video generation by introducing a code-driven agent. The agent autonomously writes and executes code to construct a dynamic 3D scene (Draft Video) that guides downstream video generators. To enable this paradigm, the authors designed a dedicated data pipeline, curated two datasets (Video-Coder-SFT-6k and Video-Coder-RL-2k), and developed the Video-Coder Harness, an interactive environment for code execution, 3D rendering, and feedback. The model is trained using supervised fine-tuning followed by harness-native reinforcement learning with dual reward feedback and an annealing strategy that balances execution and video rewards. Experiments indicate significant performance improvements on benchmarks such as PhyGenBench and VBench-2.0. The code, data, and models are fully open-sourced, though the paper is currently a preprint.

Exploring Weight-Space Programming: Capabilities, Generalization, and Limitations

Weight-Space Programming (WSP) allows reasoning agents to directly inspect and modify neural network parameters instead of relying on standard gradient-based adaptation. This preprint systematically investigates WSP using 16 editing trajectories across seven benchmarks and two model scales (Qwen3.5-0.8B and Qwen3.5-9B). The findings reveal three main insights: first, WSP can achieve substantial performance gains via highly localized interventions—such as improving accuracy by 29.45 percentage points on MATH-500 by modifying only 1,023 parameters. Second, WSP exhibits promising yet uneven cross-task generalization, where a math-edited checkpoint transfers well to some benchmarks (e.g., SciBench and IFEval) but leaves others unchanged. Third, effective editing remains challenging and inconsistent, with substantial variation across trajectories and several attempts retaining original weights due to unreliable validation. The paper provides open-source code and highlights the fundamental challenges of intervention discovery, reliability, and cross-task transfer in parameter-level editing.

SplatAnything: Gaussian Spacing Normalization for Feed-forward 3DGS across Arbitrary Scenes

SplatAnything addresses a fundamental limitation in existing feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) methods: although radial distances are often normalized, the actual spacing between neighboring Gaussian centers varies widely across diverse scene scales and camera layouts, causing systematic inconsistencies in scale targets and ambiguous supervision. To fix this, the authors propose Gaussian Spacing Normalization (GSNorm), which standardizes a robust scene-level statistic of Gaussian spacing to act as a consistent spatial reference. Evaluated across a newly established benchmark covering diverse datasets, sparse inputs, and extrapolated viewpoints, SplatAnything demonstrates state-of-the-art performance, especially under severe scale and viewpoint variations. As a preprint, the method offers practical utility for fields like autonomous driving and AR/VR, though independent replication and full ablation details should be verified from the complete paper text.

Confucius4-R2T2: A Stable-Prefix Streaming ASR Model for Real Real-Time Transcription

This paper presents Confucius4-R2T2, a stable-prefix streaming automatic speech recognition (ASR) model designed for real-time applications such as voice agents and simultaneous translators. Traditional streaming ASR systems often revise partial transcripts as more context arrives, which wastes compute and causes errors in downstream agents. To solve this, the authors introduce the Longest Stable Prefix (LSP) concept, proving it to be monotone, consistent with full-audio outputs, and maximal. By post-training Qwen3-ASR-1.7B on 1,500 hours of Chinese and English audio using LSP supervision while freezing the audio encoder and projector, R2T2 produces append-only, stable text without architectural modifications. Extensive evaluations demonstrate that R2T2 approaches base-model full-context quality with low emission lag and strong cross-lingual generalization. The code and model weights are open-sourced.
