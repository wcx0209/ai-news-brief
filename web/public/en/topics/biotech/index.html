Faeth’s oral cancer combo clears phase 2, challenging big drug injection therapies

Faeth Therapeutics tested its combination therapy, named Piktor—consisting of serabelisib and sapanisertib—alongside intravenous paclitaxel in 42 patients with advanced or recurrent endometrial cancer. The trial yielded a 33% confirmed overall response rate and a preliminary median progression-free survival of 7.4 months. These results surpass the 30% response rate benchmark set by market analysts, rivaling standard injectable options like immunotherapies and antibody-drug conjugates. While major pharmaceutical companies heavily focus on injectable antibody-drug conjugates that often rely on topoisomerase I inhibitors, patient resistance remains a challenge. Faeth's oral approach targets the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway at multiple points, offering a distinct mechanism of action. The company now plans to discuss a randomized phase 3 trial with the FDA and expects to release data from an ongoing breast cancer study next year.

Kyorin pays $140M for Japan rights to Edesa’s respiratory drug

Edesa Biotech has signed a licensing agreement with Japan's Kyorin Pharmaceutical for its anti-TLR4 antibody, paridiprubart, aimed at treating acute respiratory distress syndrome. Ipsen's moves, such as acquiring Memo Therapeutics for kidney-related conditions and Kartos Therapeutics for oncology, highlight how complete data analysis can guide companies into new fields without starting from scratch. The Future of Medicine The future of healthcare will not rely on repeatedly asking the same dead-end questions. True innovation demands the discipline to follow evidence and the courage to pivot, turning unexpected scientific outcomes into meaningful therapies for patients.

FDA's new rare disease framework faces its first big test with Capricor's DMD drug

Capricor Therapeutics is seeking FDA approval for deramiocel, a cell therapy designed to preserve upper limb function in non-ambulatory Duchenne muscular dystrophy patients. In its Phase 3 trial, the drug slowed upper limb decline by 54% compared to placebo, though the statistical analysis plan was revised, sparking debate and scrutiny from FDA reviewers during an advisory committee meeting. The debate underscores the core dilemma of the FDA's new Rare Disease Framework Initiative. In small patient populations with progressive diseases, running large trials is nearly impossible, and missing data is common. Experts argue that the FDA needs to explicitly define how it handles analysis plan changes, evaluates the totality of evidence, and values slowing functional loss as a meaningful clinical outcome. The upcoming decision will reveal whether the FDA's new framework leads to a genuinely flexible approach or simply changes public relations rhetoric. For patients, delaying approval for another multi-year trial means losing irreversible physical independence.

MIT leads $53 million project to develop AI-driven weed killers

Project Background Modern agriculture relies heavily on herbicides like glyphosate, but years of use have led to resistance mutations in weeds. At the same time, concerns are growing over the potential health and environmental risks associated with these chemicals. To address this, the U.S. Advanced Research Projects Agency for Health (ARPA-H) has awarded up to $53 million to an MIT-led team for the PLOW project. AI and Research Strategy The team will develop generative AI models to predict new chemical compounds or small interfering RNA (siRNA) that disrupt the same plant enzymes as traditional herbicides without triggering resistance. These models will optimize for low cost, safety, and effectiveness. Testing and Implementation The generated candidates will undergo high-throughput screening by partner institutions. A third team will then conduct greenhouse studies, with the first tests expected within a year. Additionally, the project will develop engineered microbes to safely degrade agrochemicals before they reach the environment.

New statistical method matches cellular states to known biological perturbations

Single-cell perturbation dictionaries are vital tools for interpreting large-scale transcriptomic data by cataloguing how cells respond to various biological and chemical stimuli. However, matching new experimental cellular states to these reference dictionaries has historically been difficult due to technical noise and biological variability. To solve this, Grabski and colleagues introduced RNA fingerprinting, a two-stage statistical approach. First, it extracts denoised perturbation signatures from a reference dictionary. Next, it applies a Bayesian probabilistic framework—adapted from genome-wide association studies—to compare query cells against these fingerprints. According to ground-truth dataset evaluations, the method reliably identifies known perturbations across cell types and scales effectively to genome-wide datasets and combinatorial perturbations. This framework offers a robust solution for researchers analyzing complex single-cell transcriptomic responses.

Background Traditional blood thinners are often avoided after a stroke because they carry a high risk of dangerous bleeding. Impact This approval introduces an entirely new class of medications to cardiovascular medicine, offering doctors a safer tool to protect stroke survivors and potentially changing how long-term stroke prevention is managed.
