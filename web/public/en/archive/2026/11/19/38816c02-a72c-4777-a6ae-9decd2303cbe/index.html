US Community Solar Faces Heavy Turbulence Amid Policy and Funding Changes

The United States community solar sector is facing major hurdles following the termination of the Solar for All program in 2025, which wiped out $7 billion in funding. Despite these policy upheavals and tax credit rollbacks, overall demand for solar power remains resilient as individual states step up with local clean energy initiatives.

pv magazine Global ↗Source published 2026-11-19 01:00 SGTMedia reportEnergy & Storage

Over the past year, the community solar landscape in the United States has undergone significant transformation due to a wave of federal policy shifts. According to pv magazine, these challenges include the termination of the Solar for All program, the introduction of strict domestic content requirements, the removal of tax credits, and the passage of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA). The cancellation of the Solar for All initiative alone resulted in the sudden evaporation of $7 billion in critical solar development funds, dealing a severe blow to community solar projects that serve households, businesses, and non-profits. Despite these regulatory roadblocks and funding losses, consumer and business demand for clean energy remains strong, prompting individual states to bypass federal shortfalls and implement their own community-level initiatives.
