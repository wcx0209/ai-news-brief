NASA Tests RS-25 Engine for Future Artemis Missions

According to NASA News: NASA successfully conducted a hot fire test of the RS-25 engine at the Stennis Space Center to support future Artemis moon missions. According to NASA, test teams fired the developmental engine for three minutes and 20 seconds to collect critical data for improving engine performance and reliability.

NASA News ↗Source published 2026-11-19 22:44 SGTOfficial releaseSpace Economy

According to NASA News:

NASA reports that the test took place on November 18, 2026, on the Fred Haise Test Stand at the Stennis Space Center in Mississippi. The RS-25 engine, designated as developmental engine No. 10001, was fired for its planned duration of 200 seconds.

Although this specific developmental engine will not fly in space, the test provides NASA and its prime contractor, L3Harris Technologies, with essential telemetry and performance metrics. These insights are intended to refine the propulsion systems for upcoming Artemis missions.

Looking ahead, the Space Launch System rocket is scheduled to launch astronauts for the Artemis IV lunar landing mission in 2028 from the Kennedy Space Center in Florida. This flight forms part of NASA's broader roadmap to explore the lunar South Pole and establish a sustainable human presence on the Moon.
