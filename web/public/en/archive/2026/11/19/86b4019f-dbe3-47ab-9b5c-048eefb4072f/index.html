Vulcan rocket failure points to fuel supply issue rather than engine problem

United Launch Alliance says a preliminary review of flight data shows that Sunday's Vulcan rocket failure was likely caused by an inadequate fuel supply to its engines rather than the engines themselves. The mishap resulted in the loss of 40 Amazon Leo satellites and has grounded the rocket, impacting both national security launches and Amazon's internet constellation plans.

Spaceflight Now ↗Source published 2026-11-19 05:11 SGTMedia reportSpace Economy

According to United Launch Alliance, the preliminary investigation into Sunday's failed Vulcan launch points to an inadequate supply of fuel from the vehicle to its two BE-4 engines, rather than a failure of the engines themselves. The rocket failed to reach orbit, resulting in the loss of 40 Amazon Leo internet satellites and marking ULA's first outright mission failure.

The finding clears the BE-4 engines manufactured by Blue Origin, providing some relief as the company works to return its New Glenn rocket to flight. It also clears the Centaur 5 upper stage and the solid rocket boosters, which performed normally. However, Vulcan is grounded indefinitely, leaving the U.S. Space Force and Amazon waiting for a return-to-flight date while the Federal Aviation Administration oversees the ongoing investigation.
