Securities Regulators Warn That Slow Progress on Quantum Security is a Mistake

Global securities regulators warn that the financial industry is moving too slowly to protect trades and payments from future quantum computer threats. According to The International Organization of Securities Commissions, most financial firms are still only in the early planning stages.

The Quantum Insider ↗Source published 2026-11-19 20:35 SGTMedia reportQuantum Technology

The Threat to Financial Markets

A global group of securities regulators warns that financial markets are lagging in preparation for quantum computers that could eventually break current encryption methods. According to The International Organization of Securities Commissions, most firms are merely training staff or identifying vulnerable systems, with very few actively testing or installing upgrades.

Interception and Supply Chain Risks

The report highlights that attackers do not need to wait for a functional quantum machine to cause harm. Through a tactic known as harvest now, decrypt later, malicious actors can intercept and store encrypted communications today for future decryption. Furthermore, a company that upgrades its systems can still remain exposed if its trading partners or shared payment networks fail to make the transition.

Mitigation and Current Experiments

To counter these risks, regulators recommend adopting post-quantum cryptography and crypto-agility. However, upgrading complex financial systems requires extensive coordination, as demonstrated by tests involving central banks. Meanwhile, actual financial applications of quantum technology remain largely experimental, prompting warnings about companies that overstate their quantum capabilities to investors.
