Cerebras CEO Pushes Back Against Short Seller Report on Chip Lifespan

According to Cerebras Blog: Cerebras CEO Andrew Feldman published a detailed response to a short seller report claiming that their massive wafer-scale AI chips wear out in two years. Feldman explained that they solved the extreme thermal expansion challenges years ago through steady temperature controls and flexible electrical connections.

Cerebras Blog ↗Source published 2026-11-05 08:00 SGTOfficial releaseArtificial Intelligence

According to Cerebras Blog:

Overcoming Thermal Challenges

Building wafer-scale computer processors has historically been considered nearly impossible because silicon and circuit boards expand at different rates when heated, threatening to crack the hardware. Cerebras spent a decade solving this mechanical hurdle. Their patented assembly avoids abrupt temperature changes by smoothing current swings, circulating chilled water evenly, and using flexible electrical connections that move naturally as temperatures fluctuate.

Debunking the Lifespan Claims

The short seller report relied on the Arrhenius equation and GPU economic lifespan comparisons to argue a two-year hardware failure rate. However, Feldman pointed out that the report used incorrect inputs for the equation. TSMC qualifies Cerebras wafers for ten years of continuous operation at high temperatures, while real-world operational temperatures average below 65 degrees Celsius. This indicates a physical lifespan measured in decades rather than years, mirroring standard silicon behavior where chips become economically obsolete before they physically wear out.
