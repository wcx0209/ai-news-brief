Are solid-state batteries in cars yet? Breaking down liquid, semi-solid, and all-solid timelines

According to reports by China Daily and China Economic Net, semi-solid batteries are hitting the road in 2026, while all-solid-state batteries are expected for limited use in 2027 and mass production around 2030.

The Solid-State Transition Spectrum

Solid-state batteries are not a simple binary of yes or no, but a gradual transition spectrum ranging from liquid, semi-solid, quasi-solid, to all-solid. According to China Daily, China's first solid-state battery standard takes effect in July 2026, defining products with 5% to 10% liquid electrolyte as mixed solid-liquid batteries. This semi-solid approach allows companies to utilize about 30% to 40% of existing liquid battery production line investments, explaining why they reach the market earlier.

Three Layers of Information

Industry observers emphasize separating facts into three tiers: already in vehicles, pilot lines, and planning. China Economic Net reports that SAIC MG has launched vehicles with semi-solid batteries, and EVE Energy's Chengdu base has rolled off solid-state batteries. Meanwhile, GGII data shows over 20 companies building pilot lines between 2025 and 2026. Corporate timelines for 2027 and beyond remain future plans that require ongoing verification.

Timelines and Roadblocks

Industry consensus points to limited all-solid-state deployment by 2027 and cost parity with liquid batteries around 2030. Challenges go beyond materials; manufacturing equipment requires complete redesign, and high initial costs mean liquid batteries will continue to occupy a major market share for a long time.
