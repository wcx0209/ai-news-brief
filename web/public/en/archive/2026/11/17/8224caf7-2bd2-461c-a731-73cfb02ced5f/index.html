3D Printing Transforms the Entire Crop Life Cycle for Sustainable Agriculture

According to CAAS Institute of Plant Protection 中国农科院植保所: Researchers from the Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences published a comprehensive review in *Progress in Materials Science* detailing how 3D printing can be applied across every stage of crop production. This framework connects manufacturing cycles with ecological loops, offering new ideas for sustainable agriculture.

According to CAAS Institute of Plant Protection 中国农科院植保所:

The review article, authored by researchers including Deng Muchen, Shangguan Wenjie, Huang Qiliang, and Cao Lidong, breaks away from the limitations of focusing on a single printing technique or material. Instead, it uses the entire crop growth cycle as a framework, examining applications from sowing and cultivation to growth management, harvesting, and crop residue recycling.

According to the authors, 3D printing's value lies in structural customization and material recycling, tailoring agricultural inputs to dynamic crop needs. For instance, it can create customizable seed-metering parts and intelligent pots during sowing, or precise irrigation nozzles and pheromone release carriers during growth management. Furthermore, agricultural waste can be turned into printing raw materials, forming a closed-loop system.

However, the study notes that most research remains at the laboratory or proof-of-concept stage. Field durability, cost-effectiveness, and the ecological safety of degradation products still lack systematic evaluation. Future developments should focus on smart materials responding to environmental changes and standard evaluation systems to transition these technologies from labs to fields.
