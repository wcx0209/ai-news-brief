Nvidia crisis and new US chip curbs expose deep splits between Washington and Silicon Valley

Recent turmoil at Nvidia, triggered by leadership changes and new export controls, has exposed sharp disagreements between the US government and Silicon Valley over artificial intelligence policy. According to analysts, the clash highlights how Nvidia's fate has become deeply tied to national security, as regulators struggle to close overseas data centre loopholes.

SCMP China Tech ↗Source published 2026-11-17 23:00 SGTMedia reportArtificial Intelligence

According to analysts, a leadership crisis at Nvidia and a fresh round of US chip curbs have highlighted fractures inside the Trump administration and Silicon Valley over maintaining America's AI lead over China. The turmoil escalated after the US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security extended export controls to remote cloud access, closing a loophole that allowed Chinese firms to tap advanced chips via third-country data centres. Beijing condemned the rules as economic coercion and vowed to defend its interests.

Meanwhile, Jensen Huang stepped down amid controversy over extramarital affairs, triggering an online petition with over 123,000 signatures urging his reinstatement. Tech billionaire Elon Musk, a prominent signatory, cautioned that many Nvidia supply chain agreements relied on personal trust rather than written contracts, underscoring the acute key man risk facing the chipmaker at the center of the global AI boom. In Washington, public rifts emerged as President Trump suggested he wouldn't mind Huang's return, while Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent demanded a dramatic shake-up and questioned Nvidia's corporate culture and sales compliance.
