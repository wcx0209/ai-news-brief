US companies race to upgrade solar panel recycling and recover valuable silver

As millions of old solar panels approach retirement, American companies are developing advanced recycling methods to capture valuable materials like silver and pure glass.

pv magazine Global ↗Source published 2026-11-17 01:00 SGTMedia reportEnergy & Storage

The looming waste wave According to the International Energy Agency, the annual volume of retired crystalline silicon solar modules in the US is projected to reach 87,000 metric tons by 2030 and skyrocket to 820,000 metric tons by 2050. Analysts at Rystad Energy estimate that the global value of recycled solar materials will hit $2.7 billion in 2030, surging to $80 billion by 2050. While glass and aluminum make up the vast majority of a panel's weight, silver used in the cells represents a major economic and strategic asset, having been added to the US Critical Minerals List in late 2025. Moving beyond shredding Traditional recycling systems rely on mechanical shredding, which often exports valuable metals trapped inside encapsulants and produces impure glass unsuitable for high-end manufacturing. To capture these values locally, several US companies are introducing new technologies. We Recycle Solar plans to open a critical materials recovery facility using regenerative solvents by 2027. Similarly, OnePlanet Solar is deploying its PRISM process to yield high-purity glass and silicon, while Comstock Metals is operating a facility in Nevada to decompose adhesives before extracting valuable metals without conventional smelting. Supply chains and regulation Success in this emerging sector relies heavily on partnerships with manufacturers and developers, such as agreements made by Solarcycle with major players like Qcells and Sunrun. Meanwhile, the regulatory landscape remains fragmented. Only four states currently ban solar panels from landfills, prompting companies to differentiate themselves through industry certifications such as the R2v3 standard and SEIA program participation to prove their traceability and environmental compliance.
