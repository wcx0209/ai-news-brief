Carbon Capture Lobbyist Registrations Hit Record High at COP31

According to the Center for International Environmental Law, 700 carbon capture representatives registered for the COP31 climate summit in Turkey, marking a third consecutive annual increase. This figure even surpasses the combined delegates of the 50 smallest national delegations, reflecting growing industry influence at global climate talks.

Environmental Protection ↗Source published 2026-11-17 22:28 SGTMedia reportClimate, Water & Environmental Engineering

According to the Center for International Environmental Law (CIEL), a total of 700 carbon capture lobbyists registered for the United Nations COP31 climate conference in Antalya, Turkey. This sets a new record since the organization began tracking these numbers at COP28 and marks a continuous upward trend, following 531 registrants at COP30 in Brazil and 480 at COP29 in Azerbaijan.

Key Details and Access

The analysis of the provisional participant list shows that the current tally exceeds the combined delegates of the 50 smallest national delegations, which total 699. Among the registered carbon capture representatives, 45 are included directly in national delegations, while 355 hold national delegation overflow passes, granting them more limited access to the venue.

Industry Context

CIEL categorizes these individuals as lobbyists based on their organizations' active involvement in carbon capture projects or public promotion of the technology. Carbon capture and storage (CCS) systems isolate carbon dioxide from industrial emissions for underground storage, whereas carbon capture, utilization and storage (CCUS) processes repurpose the captured carbon dioxide into manufacturing and industrial goods. Registration figures reflect provisional counts rather than final physical badge collection.
