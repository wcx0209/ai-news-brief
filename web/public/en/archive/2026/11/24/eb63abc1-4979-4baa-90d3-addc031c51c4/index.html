FDA Approves BridgeBio's Welgemda as First Targeted Therapy for Rare Muscle Disease

The U.S. FDA has approved BridgeBio's Welgemda as the first targeted treatment for limb-girdle muscular dystrophy type 2I/R9. Acting FDA Commissioner Kyle Diamantas called the decision a historic milestone for patients suffering from this progressive genetic disease.

BioSpace Drug Development ↗Source published 2026-11-24 05:18 SGTMedia reportBiotech & Programmable Life

Background and Details

Limb-girdle muscular dystrophy type 2I/R9 is a progressive genetic muscle disease caused by mutations in the FKRP gene, which impairs a cellular process called glycosylation and damages muscles. Welgemda, also known as BBP-418, is an oral glycosylation substrate therapy designed to increase FKRP and raise levels of glycosylated alpha-dystroglycan.

Clinical Trial Results

The approval is supported by data from the Phase 3 FORTIFY study, which met its primary interim endpoint at 12 months. The trial showed a statistically significant increase in the disease biomarker among treated patients, alongside improvements in walking speed and lung function compared to the placebo group.

Market Impact

As the first targeted treatment for any subtype of limb-girdle muscular dystrophy, analysts project significant market potential, with peak sales estimated at over $1 billion annually, pending broader genetic testing and diagnosis expansion.
