Finding faster quantum algorithms with agents and formal verification

According to Google DeepMind: Google researchers report that teams of Gemini 4 AI agents, guided by formal theorem proving in Lean, successfully optimized quantum algorithms for fields like nuclear fusion and drug discovery. The method reduced resource costs significantly across several benchmark problems, and the team has open-sourced their Lean library and verified solutions on GitHub.

Artificial Intelligence × Energy & Storage

According to Google DeepMind: Researchers from Google DeepMind and Google Quantum AI report using teams of Gemini 4 agents to improve quantum algorithms, creating highly efficient quantum circuits for complex scientific challenges like fusion energy and drug design. Because early resource-intensive quantum circuits cannot be tested on classical computers or current hardware, the team used Lean, a formal theorem prover, to mathematically verify every agent-generated result. Independent agent teams competed and traded ideas in virtual conferences every four hours, gradually reducing computational costs. According to the paper, the agents reduced computing costs for inertial confinement fusion by 11.8 times and lowered memory requirements. For drug metabolism and photochemical reactions, the agents achieved an 8 times cost reduction for light-sensitive molecules and a 2.5 times improvement for Cytochrome P450. Furthermore, they found a 2.57 times speedup for Grover's algorithm and a 290 times speedup for an arcsin arithmetic routine. The researchers are open-sourcing their Lean library, problem specifications, and verified solutions on GitHub to help the broader community build upon this work.
