WISP: Composable Representations for Wrist-Worn Activity Recognition

The authors report in this preprint: Researchers introduce WristHARBench, a standardised benchmark across nine wrist-acceleration and two multichannel inertial datasets, and propose WISP, a composable representation space combining complementary motion patterns like rhythm and shape for human activity recognition.

arXiv Machine Learning ↗Source published 2026-11-24 13:00 SGTPreprintArtificial Intelligence

The authors report in this preprint: Smartwatches and fitness bands are ubiquitous, yet existing human activity recognition (HAR) benchmarks often mix sensors from various body parts, failing to isolate performance from the wrist alone. To address this, the authors introduce WristHARBench, a benchmark standardising participant-disjoint evaluation across nine wrist-acceleration datasets and two multichannel inertial datasets. They hypothesise that distinct human activities leave complementary patterns in wrist motion—including local shape, rhythm, temporal order, and cross-axis relationships—and propose WISP (Wrist-Informed Space of Predictive Representations). WISP is a composable representation space that combines these distinct motion pattern representations with compatible classifiers. Evaluated across the nine primary datasets, WISP using random search achieves a 70.20% mean Macro-F1 score, compared to 59.62% for the strongest of 18 external baselines tested. Controlled ablations indicate that performance gains stem primarily from combining representations that capture non-overlapping motion characteristics rather than redundant encodings. Code, saved results, and benchmark data are publicly released on Hugging Face.
