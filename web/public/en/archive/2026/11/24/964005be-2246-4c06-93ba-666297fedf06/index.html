IBM Celebrates One Year of the Genesis Mission to Advance Quantum Computing

According to IBM Quantum: One year after the launch of the U.S. Genesis Mission, IBM and its partners are pushing forward with quantum-centric supercomputing by combining quantum hardware, AI, and classical systems. Recent efforts focus on error correction, fault-tolerant computing, and scientific applications in materials and energy research.

IBM Quantum ↗Source published 2026-11-24 13:00 SGTOfficial releaseQuantum Technology

According to IBM Quantum: Marking the first anniversary of the Department of Energy's Genesis Mission, IBM outlines its ongoing contributions to integrating quantum computing, artificial intelligence, and high-performance computing. Over the past month, IBM has collaborated with institutions like Oak Ridge National Laboratory, AMD, NVIDIA, and the University of Chicago on several initiatives. These include co-design platforms for quantum error correction, integrating quantum resource management with NVIDIA's Slurm workload manager, and deploying new quantum processors for research in drug discovery, materials, and high-energy physics. IBM continues to work toward its goal of deploying the fault-tolerant quantum computer, Quantum Starling, by 2029, combining hybrid quantum-classical algorithms and AI to tackle complex challenges in fields such as nuclear fusion research.
