A legendary philosopher dismisses Anthropic's AI consciousness concerns

Anthropic recently warned investors that its AI models might eventually develop consciousness, raising moral questions about treating them well. However, renowned philosopher Thomas Nagel sharply criticized this idea in a recent interview, calling such concerns idiotic.

MIT Technology Review AI ↗Source published 2026-11-24 01:36 SGTMedia reportArtificial Intelligence

Before going public, Anthropic highlighted AI consciousness as a potential risk factor, noting that it takes model sentience more seriously than other tech companies and might adopt stricter rules to protect AI welfare. While polls show a segment of the public and some philosophers believe future AI could become conscious, this stance has faced pushback.

Thomas Nagel, famous for his 1974 essay on what it is like to be a bat, strongly criticized researchers who worry about AI welfare. Nagel argues that we cannot ignore the physical differences between biological neurons and silicon circuits, asserting that reproducing what a brain does is not the same as reproducing what it experiences. Furthermore, he noted that a model's ability to produce language is insufficient to prove consciousness.

This debate underscores a deeper contradiction for Anthropic. If AI systems were indeed recognized as having moral status, Anthropic's business model of running and shutting down systems in data centers would become an ethical minefield, turning the company's historic IPO into a profound moral dilemma.
