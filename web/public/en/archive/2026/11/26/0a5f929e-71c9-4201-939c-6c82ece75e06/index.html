S&P considers Nvidia credit downgrade over governance and key man risks

S&P Global Ratings is reviewing Nvidia for a potential credit downgrade from AA to AA-. The rating agency points to governance concerns and heavy reliance on CEO Jensen Huang following his brief ouster and return. While Nvidia remains investment-grade, a lower rating could increase borrowing costs and affect its guarantees for AI infrastructure financing.

TechCrunch AI ↗Source published 2026-11-26 06:44 SGTMedia reportArtificial Intelligence

S&P Global Ratings announced it is considering downgrading Nvidia’s credit rating from AA to AA-, with a decision expected within 90 days. Although Nvidia demonstrates strong financial performance, the rating agency highlighted governance vulnerabilities, specifically citing the recent ouster and reinstatement of CEO Jensen Huang, weak executive oversight, and a lack of clear succession planning.

S&P emphasized the high degree of key man risk, reflecting the company's heavy reliance on Huang. While Nvidia is certain to retain its investment-grade status, a downgrade could raise its overall borrowing costs.

This potential change carries broader implications for the AI ecosystem. Nvidia frequently backstops neoclouds and infrastructure companies that lack strong standalone credit. In August, Nvidia announced a $500 billion plan to arrange AI infrastructure financing with major financial institutions, initially guaranteeing 25% of the loans. A lower credit rating would likely increase pressure from lenders, forcing Nvidia to provide larger guarantees or collateral for these massive financing arrangements.
