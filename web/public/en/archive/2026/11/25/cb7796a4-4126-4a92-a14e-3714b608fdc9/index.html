Plant-based and cultivated meat patent filings decline amid industry pressures

According to a patent analysis by Appleyard Lees, patent applications for plant-based and cultivated meat have dropped for consecutive years as companies face funding, regulatory, and commercialization hurdles. While filing volumes are down, the report notes that firms are shifting focus toward practical manufacturing challenges rather than stopping innovation altogether.

Green Queen ↗Source published 2026-11-25 20:00 SGTMedia reportAgriculture & Food Technology

A new report from British IP firm Appleyard Lees reveals that priority patent filings for plant-based meat dropped from a revised 239 in 2023 to 187 in 2024, marking a nearly 22% decline and the third consecutive year of slowing activity. Similarly, cultivated meat patent applications fell by nearly 13% from 119 to 104, seeing declines for the second year running.

The data shows shifting geographic trends and corporate strategies. Europe saw a sharp drop in plant-based filings from 85 to 50, though it remains the most prolific region, while South Korea overtook the US in cultivated meat patent applications with 25 filings against the US's 20. Major consumer goods giants like Unilever and Nestlé scaled back their filing activities significantly, whereas companies like Cargill and dsm-firmenich bucked the trend.

Experts note that the downward trend reflects a more commercially focused phase. Rather than chasing pure scientific breakthroughs, remaining innovators are prioritizing lower-cost production, cell-culture methods, and improving taste, texture, and manufacturing efficiency to overcome ongoing commercialization hurdles.
