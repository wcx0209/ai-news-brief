Fraunhofer IAPT uses ultrasound to remove 3D metal printing supports

German research institute Fraunhofer IAPT has developed an automated process that uses ultrasound to remove support structures from 3D printed metal parts. According to the institute, the method takes only a few minutes and works without manual labor, solving a major bottleneck in metal additive manufacturing.

Background and Process

Removing support structures is traditionally one of the most labor-intensive and hazardous steps in metal additive manufacturing, often exposing workers to fine metal powder. To tackle this, Fraunhofer IAPT has created an ultrasonic process that applies high-frequency mechanical vibrations to printed components. By pairing custom-designed support structures with tailored vibration settings, the method concentrates stress where the supports attach, causing them to break away cleanly within a few minutes.

Application and Industry Impact

Originating from the Sonodent research project, the technology is initially being applied in dental technology but is designed to scale across other industries. While Fraunhofer IAPT focuses on ultrasonic vibrations, other players are tackling the same bottleneck through robotics, such as Rivelin Robotics, or redesigned breakaway structures, like those from SnowX, highlighting a broader industry push toward automated post-processing.
