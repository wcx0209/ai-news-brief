Curiosity Spots Drifting Twilight Clouds on Mars

According to NASA News: NASA's Curiosity rover has captured images of wispy twilight clouds drifting across the Martian sky. According to NASA, these post-sunset clouds appear for a short time each fall over Gale Crater, which the rover has been exploring for over 14 years.

NASA News ↗Source published 2026-11-21 06:27 SGTOfficial releaseSpace Economy

According to NASA News: NASA reports that the Curiosity Mars rover captured images of drifting clouds on October 31, 2026, marking the 5,061st Martian day of its mission. Using its navigation cameras, the rover documented these post-sunset clouds, known as twilight clouds, which typically emerge for brief periods every fall within Gale Crater. Curiosity has been investigating this region for more than 14 years. The mission is managed for NASA by the Jet Propulsion Laboratory, built by JPL and overseen by Caltech in Pasadena, California, as part of the Mars Exploration Program.
