FDA Misses Decision Deadline for Savara's Rare Lung Disease Drug

The FDA announced it will miss its decision deadline for Savara's rare lung disease drug candidate, molgramostim, causing the company's shares to drop 18%. Despite the delay and lack of a new review date, market analysts remain confident in eventual approval, pointing to strong clinical trial data and potential agency bandwidth issues.

BioSpace Drug Development ↗Source published 2026-11-21 00:10 SGTMedia reportBiotech & Programmable Life

Savara recently reported that the FDA will fail to meet its already extended PDUFA decision date for molgramostim, an inhaled therapy intended to treat autoimmune pulmonary alveolar proteinosis, a rare and serious lung condition. Following the announcement, Savara's stock fell 18% to $4.26 per share at market open. This marks the second time the review process has faced delays, as the agency previously extended the timeline by three months due to a major application amendment.

Despite the uncertainty and the lack of a newly assigned decision deadline, financial analysts from Jefferies and Guggenheim expressed strong confidence in the drug's approval prospects. They noted that the delay is likely driven by general bandwidth constraints at the FDA rather than safety or efficacy concerns. The drug candidate previously demonstrated positive results in the Phase 3 IMPALA-2 trial, successfully meeting its primary endpoint and showing long-term durability, positioning it to potentially become the first approved treatment for the disease.
