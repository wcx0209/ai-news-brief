New approach enables physically consistent AI models of chaotic systems

According to MIT Advanced Manufacturing: Researchers at MIT have developed a new method to add physics-based hard constraints to machine learning models, ensuring they do not drift into impossible predictions over time.

MIT Advanced Manufacturing ↗Source published 2026-11-21 04:00 SGTOfficial releaseAdvanced Manufacturing & Industrial Infrastructure
Artificial Intelligence

According to MIT Advanced Manufacturing:

Background

Predicting complex and chaotic systems like weather patterns, ocean currents, and turbulent flows is notoriously difficult. While artificial intelligence models can learn from data, they often suffer from a fundamental flaw: small initial errors accumulate over time, causing simulations to drift into physically impossible states where values grow without limit. Details According to MIT researchers Sunbochen Tang, Navid Azizan, and Themistoklis Sapsis, previous attempts relied on soft penalties during training, which offered no guarantee at test time. The team’s new approach introduces a projection layer that enforces hard constraints based on real-world energy dissipation. The model learns an energy-like function and a confinement boundary, ensuring that predictions remain bounded over arbitrarily long forecast windows while still preserving complex chaotic behaviors. Impact Tested across various physical systems, the modular and model-agnostic approach closely matched true outcomes compared to conventional methods. The team has made their code and paper freely available online, paving the way for more trustworthy and reliable AI applications in meteorology, aviation, and industrial processes.
