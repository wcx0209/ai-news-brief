NASA's Astronomy Picture of the Day Features Voyager 2's Crescent Neptune

According to NASA News: NASA's Astronomy Picture of the Day features an inspired composite image of Neptune, its largest moon Triton, and its faint rings based on data from the Voyager 2 spacecraft's 1989 flyby. The image looks back toward the Sun, capturing the distant ice giant and its moon as thin sunlit crescents.

NASA News ↗Source published 2026-11-21 13:05 SGTOfficial releaseSpace Economy

According to NASA News:

According to NASA's Astronomy Picture of the Day, the featured image is a composite created by Rolf Olsen using historical data from NASA's Voyager 2 spacecraft and sky survey data. Voyager 2 made its closest approach to Neptune on August 25, 1989, remaining the only spacecraft to have visited the distant ice giant.

The composite scene adopts an interplanetary perspective looking back toward the Sun, showing Neptune and its largest moon, Triton, as thin sunlit crescents. Details visible in the scene include cirrus clouds, a dark band around the southern polar region, and a cloudy vortex above the pole itself. The background starfield corresponds to the outbound Voyager's view toward the constellation Camelopardalis. While Voyager 2 is now cruising through interstellar space, its historical flyby data continues to inspire visualizations of the Neptunian system.
