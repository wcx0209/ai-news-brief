SINTX Completes 3D Printing Facility for Patient-Specific Silicon Nitride-PEEK Implants

SINTX Technologies has completed its 3D printing facility and received medical-grade printers to produce personalized orthopedic implants made from a silicon nitride and PEEK composite. The company plans to source filament from Evonik, seek FDA clearance, and target initial custom devices by early 2027, though clinical benefits still need product-specific validation.

Facility and Manufacturing Plans

SINTX Technologies has finished building its additive manufacturing facility in Salt Lake City and received its EXT-220 MED medical-grade 3D printers. The company is setting up the systems to manufacture personalized orthopedic implants using a composite of silicon nitride and polyetheretherketone under its SiNERGY platform. SINTX expects to receive compounded filament from Evonik before the end of 2026 and is pursuing FDA 510(k) clearance for patient-specific extremity implants, aiming for limited custom device distribution in early 2027.

Material Science and Laboratory Testing

While PEEK is widely used in orthopedics, silicon nitride is a bioactive ceramic that has demonstrated antibacterial and bone-forming properties in laboratory studies. According to SINTX, published in vitro studies on 3D printed SiN/PEEK spinal cage designs showed significantly lower bacterial adhesion compared to conventional PEEK. However, the company notes that clinical relevance for specific human implants must still be confirmed through formal evaluation.

Digital Workflow and Industry Context

To support its production goals, SINTX has appointed James A. Smith as Director of 3D Additive-Manufacturing and is in talks with an engineering firm to integrate AI-assisted medical imaging. This workflow mirrors paths taken by other firms like 3D Systems, which secured FDA clearance for a PEEK cranial implant workflow, though SINTX is initially focusing on silicon nitride composites and extremity applications.
