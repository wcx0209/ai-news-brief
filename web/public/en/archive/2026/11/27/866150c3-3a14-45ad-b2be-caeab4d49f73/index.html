Japan's 30th solar auction allocates 27.7 MW of PV capacity

Japan recently completed its 30th utility-scale solar auction, allocating 27.7 MW of PV capacity across seven approved projects. According to OCCTO, the lowest bid reached JPY 5.00 ($0.031) per kWh, while the weighted average final price was JPY 9.13 per kWh.

Auction Results and Details

Japan's Organization for Cross-regional Coordination of Transmission Operators (OCCTO) released the outcomes of its 30th solar auction, which was designed for projects of at least 250 kW under the country's feed-in premium (FIP) scheme. Out of eight qualified projects totaling 41.4 MW, seven projects submitted bids and successfully secured support for a combined 27.7 MW.

Pricing Breakdown

Bids varied across the participating developers. Japanese solar installer Marushin Koei offered the lowest bid at JPY 5.00 ($0.031) per kWh for a 500 kW project, whereas the ceiling price of JPY 9.60 per kWh was reached by Machiokoshi Energy and Solar Field 13. Ultimately, the weighted average final price settled at JPY 9.13 per kWh.

Market Context

This procurement represents the third solar auction of the 2026 fiscal year in Japan. The results show a continuous trend of declining awarded capacity and rising average prices in recent exercises, as the Japanese government prepares to phase out large-scale solar auction support after fiscal 2026.
