UK Publishes Roadmap to Export Cultivated Meat to Non-EU Countries

The British Food Standards Agency has published a nine-step roadmap to help cultivated meat companies navigate exports to non-EU countries. The guidance comes from a government sandbox programme and aims to clarify cross-government export requirements, though the UK has not yet approved cultivated meat for human food.

Green Queen ↗Source published 2026-11-13 21:00 SGTMedia reportAgriculture & Food Technology

Background

The UK Food Standards Agency (FSA) and Food Standards Scotland have released a new export process map for cell-cultivated products destined for non-EU markets. This initiative stems from the FSA's Cell-Cultivated Products Sandbox Programme, a two-year government-funded effort running until February 2027 to develop safety standards and regulatory guidance for the sector.

Key Details

The newly published guidance breaks down the export process into nine practical steps. It addresses market eligibility, production standards, import and marketing requirements, export health certification, customs declarations with HMRC, and logistics such as maintaining cold chains and temperature controls. The FSA notes that cultivated biomass counts legally as a processed product of animal origin rather than standard meat.

Industry Impact

While this roadmap provides clarity for international trade, the FSA has yet to approve cultivated meat for human consumption in the UK, with several applications currently under review. Meanwhile, ongoing UK-EU trade negotiations regarding sanitary and phytosanitary agreements could soon impact local novel food regulations and approval timelines.
