MIT researchers develop a new training method for faster and more trustworthy climate AI models

MIT ClimateClimate, Water & Environmental EngineeringResearchSource published2026-11-12 13:00 SGT
Official releaseStage not established
Artificial Intelligence

According to MIT Climate: Traditional earth system models used for climate projection are extremely powerful, but they take months to run on supercomputers for just a single scenario. To solve this, scientists use climate emulators, which are fast AI models that mimic full-scale systems. However, training these emulators usually requires running complex models on six to eight realistic future emission scenarios, making the data creation process slow and costly. According to MIT researchers, emulators trained exclusively on realistic scenarios often fail to adapt when faced with unfamiliar or novel situations. To address this, the team developed an algorithm that generates a synthetic dataset of optimized, non-traditional climate scenarios tailored to boost predictive performance. By introducing rapid variations and high-frequency oscillations into the training data, the algorithm allows the emulator to extract much more information about the overall system while requiring far fewer full-scale model runs. The resulting emulators proved capable of accurately predicting outcomes in scenarios they had never seen during training, such as local pollutant control policies where some emissions rise while others fall. Published in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, this technique aims to reduce the months or years typically spent on emulator production, helping researchers and decision-makers get updated climate tools much faster.

The Definitive Fine-Tuning Guide for 3D Generation Models

Hugging FaceArtificial IntelligenceResearchSource published2026-11-12 08:00 SGT
Media reportStage not established

Background 3D generation has wide applications in robotics, simulation, and entertainment, but standardizing different file formats and representations remains challenging. While TRELLIS.2 improved 3D structure representation, general-purpose models often fail to produce accurate geometry for specific tasks, such as shoes where the base model frequently generates incorrect soles. Implementation The authors used a subset of the Google Scanned Objects dataset containing 188 footwear objects. To adapt TRELLIS.2 efficiently without catastrophic forgetting, they applied LoRA adapters exclusively to the sparse-structure flow DiT and the shape flow DiT, while freezing the texture generation stage. Training was executed using Hugging Face Jobs on A100 GPUs or locally via Docker. Results Quantitative evaluation on held-out objects showed major improvements. The fine-tuned model achieved a mean F-score of 0.803 compared to the base model's 0.276, reduced the Chamfer distance from 0.174 to 0.033, and increased normal consistency, proving that targeted fine-tuning successfully adapts general 3D models for specific object categories.

TMTPost 钛媒体Artificial IntelligenceSocietySource published2026-11-12 21:42 SGT
Media reportStage not established

Background and Survey Data Survey data indicate that generative AI users in China will exceed 700 million by mid-2026, with about a third using the tools for work. Research shows that more than a third of respondents pay for large-model access, and nearly three in ten buy dedicated AI office products. Outside a few tech giants, corporate reimbursement remains rare, leaving individuals, students, and early-career researchers to cover the shortfalls. Spending and Use Cases Workers use paid AI tools across four main clusters: drafting and slides, meeting notes and translation, data cleaning and light coding, and specialized work like contract review. Costs vary by user needs, ranging from a few hundred yuan a month for journalists to around 3,000 yuan for fund managers who rely on AI for research code. E-commerce platforms and telecom operators now offer token recharges alongside ordinary consumer goods, turning AI usage into a regular household bill. Cost Factors and Future Outlook Pricing design opacity and token-based metering drive up monthly expenses, as users often stack multiple subscriptions and burn credits on prompt tuning. Until employers treat AI access as standard infrastructure or vendors make pricing more transparent, workers will continue to subsidize the efficiency expected by their employers.

LlamaIndex BlogArtificial IntelligenceModelsSource published2026-11-12 08:00 SGT
Official releaseStage not established

ParseBench-2 addresses this by introducing severity-tuned scoring based on 330 expert-defined error types across six dimensions: text, formatting, tables, charts, layout, and forms.
