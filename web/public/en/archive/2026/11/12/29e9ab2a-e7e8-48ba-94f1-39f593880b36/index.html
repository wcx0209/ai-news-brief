Can AI Foundation Models Actually Explain How the Brain Works?

The TransmitterNeurotechnology & Human InterfacesResearchSource published2026-11-12 02:44 SGT
Media reportStage not established
Artificial Intelligence

The Brain Data Rush Researchers are applying the recipe behind large language models to neuroscience, training neural networks on vast brain recordings. Meta, Stanford, and startups have released or are building brain foundation models, hoping that scale will make them smarter. The Great Debate Optimists argue that models reproducing brain activity can act as stand-ins for living subjects and help us discover new biological insights. Skeptics counter that fitting brain data does not guarantee the model works like a real brain, nor does it grant mechanistic understanding. Scaling laws for brain data also remain murky compared to language models. The Path Forward Some suggest combining data-driven models with task-trained models, while others argue that true intelligence requires modeling the whole body and physical interactions, reminding the field that scaling data might not be a silver bullet.

Observation focus (editorial lens) Check participant numbers, study duration, safety and independent replication; a demonstration does not establish broad clinical benefit.
