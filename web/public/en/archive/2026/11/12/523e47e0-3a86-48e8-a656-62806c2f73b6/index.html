Chinese Workers Are Paying Their Own AI Bills to Stay Productive

A growing number of white-collar workers in China are paying out of pocket for AI memberships and API credits because companies rarely reimburse these costs. As free tiers run out, buying model access has become a recurring household expense for tasks like drafting, coding, and translation.

TMTPost 钛媒体 ↗Source published 2026-11-12 21:42 SGTMedia reportArtificial Intelligence

Background and Survey Data Survey data indicate that generative AI users in China will exceed 700 million by mid-2026, with about a third using the tools for work. Research shows that more than a third of respondents pay for large-model access, and nearly three in ten buy dedicated AI office products. Outside a few tech giants, corporate reimbursement remains rare, leaving individuals, students, and early-career researchers to cover the shortfalls. Spending and Use Cases Workers use paid AI tools across four main clusters: drafting and slides, meeting notes and translation, data cleaning and light coding, and specialized work like contract review. Costs vary by user needs, ranging from a few hundred yuan a month for journalists to around 3,000 yuan for fund managers who rely on AI for research code. E-commerce platforms and telecom operators now offer token recharges alongside ordinary consumer goods, turning AI usage into a regular household bill. Cost Factors and Future Outlook Pricing design opacity and token-based metering drive up monthly expenses, as users often stack multiple subscriptions and burn credits on prompt tuning. Until employers treat AI access as standard infrastructure or vendors make pricing more transparent, workers will continue to subsidize the efficiency expected by their employers.
