US tightens AI chip export controls to deter Chinese companies from accessing compute remotely

The Trump administration is introducing new export controls to stop companies in restricted countries like China from bypassing rules by remotely accessing AI data centers abroad.

TechCrunch AI ↗Source published 2026-11-16 23:02 SGTMedia reportArtificial Intelligence

Background

The U.S. Commerce Department is rolling out an interim rule designed to close loopholes in existing export restrictions. Previously, rules required licenses only for shipping chips directly to companies in embargoed countries like China, Russia, Iran, and North Korea. However, companies have increasingly turned to remote-access loopholes, using overseas data centers to train advanced models.

Key Details

The new rule applies to any data center or network exceeding a specific computing power metric of 12 million total processing performance, roughly equivalent to about 750 Nvidia H100 or H200 chips. Operators must obtain licenses within 90 days if they serve customers from restricted countries. Additionally, the rule introduces new licensing requirements for semiconductor transfers to Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand, and Brazil due to a higher risk of diversion, though chips destined for U.S.-based companies in these nations are exempt. The framework also rescinds the previous administration's AI Diffusion Rule.

Industry Impact

This regulatory shift forces global cloud and data center operators to strictly monitor customer ownership structures and network access. It comes in the wake of heightened scrutiny over Chinese firms scrambling for overseas compute capacity and aims to block actors from leveraging U.S. advanced semiconductors for intelligence-related or military activities.
