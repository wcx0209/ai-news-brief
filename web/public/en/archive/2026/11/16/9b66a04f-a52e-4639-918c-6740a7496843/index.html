Historical brief · 2026-11-16

Quantum error correction surpasses breakeven point using only drives and dissipation

Researchers in Nature Physics report a new autonomous quantum error correction protocol that extends logical qubit coherence beyond the physical components' limits. Unlike traditional methods requiring constant measurement and feedback, this approach uses engineered drives and dissipation to fix photon loss errors.

Nature Quantum Information ↗Source published 2026-11-16 08:00 SGTResearch paperQuantum Technology

Background

Quantum error correction is essential for scalable quantum computing, as it protects fragile quantum information from environmental noise. Most existing systems rely on measurement-based feedback, but read-out errors, hardware delays, and system complexity often limit their performance. Details & Results In this study published in Nature Physics, researchers led by Lida Sun and Luyan Sun implemented an autonomous quantum error correction protocol derived from quantum channel simulation. Instead of traditional error-checking loops, the system repeatedly restores a photon-loss-corrupted logical qubit by shifting error data to auxiliary qubits and resetting them quickly. By utilizing a binomial code within a long-lived bosonic mode, the team successfully extended the coherence time of the logical qubit to 1.04 times that of the best physical qubit in the experimental setup. Significance This demonstration proves that engineered drives and dissipation can successfully push error-corrected bosonic logical qubits beyond the breakeven point, offering a flexible, feedback-free pathway toward more efficient quantum computers.
