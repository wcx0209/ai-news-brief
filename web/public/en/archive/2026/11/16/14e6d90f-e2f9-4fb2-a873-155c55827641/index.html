University spin-out turns internal lab tool into JetForge 3D printer

Added, a spin-out from the University of Nottingham, has developed JetForge, a materials deposition platform featuring interchangeable printhead modules. Originally built because researchers could not buy a machine that let them easily swap different printheads and test custom inks, the company is now commercializing the hardware for other labs.

Background and Origins

Founded by Richard Hague, Chris Tuck, and Ricky Wildman, Added started as a contract research business to handle industrial projects that did not fit a traditional academic schedule. Over time, the team focused on material jetting and binder jetting, building custom equipment when commercial prototyping machines proved too restrictive for testing new inks.

The JetForge Platform

Commercial 3D printers often lock users into a single manufacturer's materials, while typical development systems can be difficult to operate and maintain. JetForge solves this by packing the driver board, recirculation pump, and printhead into a removable module called a Blade. Users can swap these modules or run different heads simultaneously, configuring the machine for specific experimental needs such as multi-material printing or droplet inspection.

Applications and Outlook

The founders see immediate opportunities in electronics, such as printing conductive circuits directly through the body of polymer components or onto wearables. Added also collaborates with industrial partners on projects like desalination filter spacers and pharmaceutical oral dosage forms, aiming to help researchers quickly test and customize new materials.
