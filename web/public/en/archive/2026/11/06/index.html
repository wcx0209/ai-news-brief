Google Research introduces AI system to detect hypertension using smartwatches

Background High blood pressure is a widespread health issue that often goes unnoticed because it rarely shows obvious symptoms. Traditional wearable blood pressure trackers usually require regular arm cuff calibration to measure relative changes, which limits their usefulness as independent screening tools. Technology Google Research scientists created a multimodal AI system named AI-PPG-ACC-HTN. It analyzes sensor data from consumer smartwatches over multiple days, using a deep convolutional neural network trained through self-supervised contrastive learning. By combining these passive physiological signatures with the user's height, the system calculates a continuous hypertension probability score. Impact Validated against 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure monitoring in a clinical study, the smartwatch system showed high specificity and predictive value. While not meant to replace standard medical devices, this technology could offer a scalable screening tool to catch the condition early and encourage people to seek professional medical care.

Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash brings 15-second videos and lower prices

Google is rolling out Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash, bringing significant upgrades to its AI video generation tool. The new version supports native 15-second video creation, up from 10 seconds, and allows users to specify durations in one-second increments. Pricing and Workflow Google has dropped the price by 30%, bringing it down to $0.07 per second for 720p video. The updated workflow lets users draft quickly at lower resolutions, fix specific details through follow-up edits, and upscale the final results to 1080p or 4K. Key Features and Availability The model introduces enhanced reference adherence, allowing characters, products, and styles to stay consistent across different camera angles. Users can also perform conversational editing turn by turn, altering backgrounds or lighting without starting from scratch. The tool is now generally available through the Gemini API, Google Cloud, and consumer apps like Google Vids and the Gemini app.

Video-Coder: Reinforcing Agentic Coding for Video Generation

Video-Coder addresses the challenge of precise spatial structure, motion dynamics, and controllable scene composition in video generation by introducing a code-driven agent. The agent autonomously writes and executes code to construct a dynamic 3D scene (Draft Video) that guides downstream video generators. To enable this paradigm, the authors designed a dedicated data pipeline, curated two datasets (Video-Coder-SFT-6k and Video-Coder-RL-2k), and developed the Video-Coder Harness, an interactive environment for code execution, 3D rendering, and feedback. The model is trained using supervised fine-tuning followed by harness-native reinforcement learning with dual reward feedback and an annealing strategy that balances execution and video rewards. Experiments indicate significant performance improvements on benchmarks such as PhyGenBench and VBench-2.0. The code, data, and models are fully open-sourced, though the paper is currently a preprint.

Exploring Weight-Space Programming: Capabilities, Generalization, and Limitations

Weight-Space Programming (WSP) allows reasoning agents to directly inspect and modify neural network parameters instead of relying on standard gradient-based adaptation. This preprint systematically investigates WSP using 16 editing trajectories across seven benchmarks and two model scales (Qwen3.5-0.8B and Qwen3.5-9B). The findings reveal three main insights: first, WSP can achieve substantial performance gains via highly localized interventions—such as improving accuracy by 29.45 percentage points on MATH-500 by modifying only 1,023 parameters. Second, WSP exhibits promising yet uneven cross-task generalization, where a math-edited checkpoint transfers well to some benchmarks (e.g., SciBench and IFEval) but leaves others unchanged. Third, effective editing remains challenging and inconsistent, with substantial variation across trajectories and several attempts retaining original weights due to unreliable validation. The paper provides open-source code and highlights the fundamental challenges of intervention discovery, reliability, and cross-task transfer in parameter-level editing.
