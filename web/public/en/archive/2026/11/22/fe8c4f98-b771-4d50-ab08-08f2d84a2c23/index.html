Will Australia's bipartisan AI consensus survive parliament's final weeks?

As Australia's federal parliament enters its final weeks of 2026, an upcoming joint select committee report on artificial intelligence will test whether cross-party cooperation can survive. The debate centers on contentious issues like datacentres, energy use, and copyright protections for creative industries as lawmakers rush to pass key reforms before the holiday break.

As the Australian parliament approaches its final four sitting days of 2026, the government faces a crowded agenda of unfinished business, ranging from domestic gas reserves to digital duty of care laws. Among these, artificial intelligence policy has emerged as a major flashpoint. A joint select committee report is set to reveal Labor's direction on critical infrastructure like datacentres and how creative works are protected from AI scraping, following fierce pushback from musicians and artists.

While some Liberal MPs previously argued that AI is too important to become a partisan weapon, recent proposals regarding potential copyright exemptions have sparked sharp rebukes from the opposition. Concurrently, Foreign Minister Penny Wong is scheduled to frame AI as a core national security and foreign policy issue, urging international prudence. How parliament handles these competing pressures this week will determine if a fragile bipartisan alliance on technology governance can hold together or fracture completely.
