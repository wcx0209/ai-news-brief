From Raye to Coldplay, How AI Scalpers Are Pricing You Out of Concert Tickets

Concert ticketing is facing a major wave of AI-driven scalping, where bots generate hundreds of thousands of fake registrations to hoard seats. Industry experts note that while traditional scripts still drive much of the abuse, generative AI accelerates fraudulent listings and complex bypass tactics, forcing platforms to rethink their defense strategies.

The Scaling Threat

Major artists like Raye and Coldplay have seen unprecedented demand overwhelmed by automated bot activity, with millions of sign-ups for just thousands of seats. According to industry insiders, while many scalpers still rely on traditional scripts and multiple accounts, generative AI has significantly lowered the barrier for creating convincing fake booking confirmations and dynamic pricing markups.

The Real Ticket Problem

Experts point out that the deeper issue is not just AI-generated fakes, but the resale of genuine tickets through informal channels. Secondary marketplaces are now adopting cryptographic proofs, such as zero-knowledge Transport Layer Security (zkTLS), to verify original purchases. However, gaps remain because platforms cannot easily track what happens to a ticket on the original ticketing site after it is sold.

The Future of AI Agents

As personal AI assistants emerge to help fans book tickets automatically, platforms face the challenge of distinguishing legitimate agents from malicious bulk-buying bots. Experts emphasize that future security systems must focus on verifying user identity and intent rather than simply blocking all automated traffic.
