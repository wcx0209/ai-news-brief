The manufacturing data gap holding back physical AI

Although robotics and physical AI companies have raised billions, deploying autonomous machines in factories remains tough because of a critical data gap. Traditional manufacturing processes rely heavily on the unspoken, hands-on experience of technicians rather than structured digital records.

The Robot Report ↗Source published 2026-11-22 06:25 SGTMedia reportRobotics & Autonomous Systems
Artificial Intelligence

According to VulcanForms CTO Michael Kenworthy, physical AI and robotics companies have raised nearly $48 billion in 2026, but moving autonomous systems out of controlled labs and onto factory floors faces a major roadblock. Factories generate plenty of operational data, but it is often trapped in isolated systems, making it difficult to link machine parameters with final inspection results. Data from the Manufacturing Leadership Council shows that 37% of manufacturers cited data interoperability as a major smart factory hurdle in 2026, up from 22% in 2025.

To bridge this gap and prepare for physical AI, manufacturers need to focus on three key steps. First, they must capture real-world production conditions that reflect complex variables like temperature and material characteristics. Second, they need to create a unified digital thread, linking isolated machines and quality management systems together. Finally, companies must build clear feedback loops and safety boundaries around quality and repeatability, ensuring autonomous adjustments are properly validated, especially in highly regulated sectors like medical device manufacturing.
