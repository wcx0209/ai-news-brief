Nature Study: Implantable Device Controls Diabetes in Rats Using Glucose-Powered Nerve Stimulation

Researchers from the National University of Singapore and Nanjing University of Posts and Telecommunications have developed an implantable device called NeuroAxis that controls blood sugar in diabetic rats without insulin injections.

QbitAI 量子位 ↗Source published 2026-11-22 14:21 SGTMedia reportNeurotechnology & Human Interfaces

According to a recent study published in Nature, the NeuroAxis system consists of a glucose fuel cell for power and sensing, flexible control circuits, and a self-healing neural electrode that wraps around the vagus nerve. Tested on type 2 diabetic rats, the device brought blood sugar down to near-normal levels within two weeks, alongside reductions in food intake and body weight. In type 1 diabetic models, eight weeks of treatment restored normal blood glucose control and showed signs of islet beta-cell proliferation. However, the authors note that challenges remain before human application, including biological differences in beta-cell regeneration, potential autoimmune recurrence in type 1 patients, and the long-term degradation of fuel cell power over time.
