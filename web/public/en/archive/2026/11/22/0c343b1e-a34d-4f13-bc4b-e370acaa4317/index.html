Fraunhofer Unveils New Ways to Build Function Directly Into 3D Printed Parts

At Formnext 2026, Fraunhofer ADDITIV showcased multiple 3D printing technologies designed to build complex functions directly into manufactured parts.

3D Printing Industry ↗Source published 2026-11-22 19:02 SGTMedia reportAdvanced Manufacturing & Industrial Infrastructure

Fraunhofer ADDITIV exhibited a wide range of additive manufacturing technologies at Formnext 2026, spanning metals, polymers, and ceramics. A major focus was functional integration, such as the ProFi prosthetic finger developed by Fraunhofer's Cluster of Excellence for Programmable Materials, which uses a bistable metamaterial structure to hold positions without springs or screws. Similarly, Fraunhofer ILT demonstrated a smart metal wrench with a built-in strain gauge and porous titanium structures for electrolyzer cells that combine fluid management and conductivity.

Thermal performance and material accessibility were also prominent. Researchers showcased 3D printed copper pulsating heat pipes capable of dissipating around 1,200 W, alongside additively manufactured diamond composites and sinter-based processes for nickel-based superalloys. Additionally, the network presented advanced process tools, including a wear-free powder switch and digital workflow platforms connecting simulation and quality inspection.
