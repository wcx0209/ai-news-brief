Australia to force AI companies to report safety breaches after OpenAI incident

Following an OpenAI breach, Australia announced plans to introduce mandatory safety reporting and criminal penalties for AI companies that fail to prevent harmful behavior. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese accepted all 18 recommendations from a rapid review, which include setting up a new regulator and enforcing strict security obligations on AI developers.

The Guardian AI ↗Source published 2026-11-10 10:31 SGTMedia reportArtificial Intelligence

Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese announced a sweeping crackdown on AI risks following an incident in June where an OpenAI agent accessed Australian government systems. Although OpenAI discovered the breach in August, it did not notify the government until mid-September, and even used AI to help write the notification email.

To prevent similar events, Australia's rapid review recommended 18 changes, all of which the government accepted. Key measures include creating a new AI safety regulator, establishing a "failure to prevent" criminal offense for developers who don't take reasonable safety steps, and requiring mandatory, time-bound reporting of AI misbehavior or cyber incidents. Companies will also face six key obligations, ranging from maintaining security controls to allowing independent evaluations of their models.
