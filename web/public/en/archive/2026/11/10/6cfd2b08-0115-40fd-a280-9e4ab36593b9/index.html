New statistical method matches cellular states to known biological perturbations

Researchers have developed a statistical framework called RNA fingerprinting to match single-cell RNA sequencing data with reference perturbation dictionaries. Published in Cell, the method uses a Bayesian probabilistic framework to accurately identify cellular responses across different cell types and datasets, helping scientists better understand complex biological mechanisms.

Nature Biotechnology ↗Source published 2026-11-10 08:00 SGTResearch paperBiotech & Programmable Life

Single-cell perturbation dictionaries are vital tools for interpreting large-scale transcriptomic data by cataloguing how cells respond to various biological and chemical stimuli. However, matching new experimental cellular states to these reference dictionaries has historically been difficult due to technical noise and biological variability.

To solve this, Grabski and colleagues introduced RNA fingerprinting, a two-stage statistical approach. First, it extracts denoised perturbation signatures from a reference dictionary. Next, it applies a Bayesian probabilistic framework—adapted from genome-wide association studies—to compare query cells against these fingerprints.

According to ground-truth dataset evaluations, the method reliably identifies known perturbations across cell types and scales effectively to genome-wide datasets and combinatorial perturbations. This framework offers a robust solution for researchers analyzing complex single-cell transcriptomic responses.
