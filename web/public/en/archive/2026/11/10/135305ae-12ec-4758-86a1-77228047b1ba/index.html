FDA approves Bayer's new stroke drug Sunharbo

The FDA has approved Bayer's new drug, Sunharbo, to help prevent recurrent strokes. As the first in a novel class of medications, it targets a specific protein to block dangerous blood clots without increasing the usual risk of bleeding.

STAT Biotech ↗Source published 2026-11-10 06:28 SGTMedia reportBiotech & Programmable Life

Background

Traditional blood thinners are often avoided after a stroke because they carry a high risk of dangerous bleeding. Researchers have long searched for a way to prevent secondary strokes more safely without this major downside.

Details

Bayer's newly approved drug, Sunharbo, works by inhibiting a specific protein called XIa. In a large Phase 3 clinical trial involving patients who had previously suffered a stroke, the drug successfully reduced the risk of secondary strokes by 26% compared to a placebo.

Impact

This approval introduces an entirely new class of medications to cardiovascular medicine, offering doctors a safer tool to protect stroke survivors and potentially changing how long-term stroke prevention is managed.
