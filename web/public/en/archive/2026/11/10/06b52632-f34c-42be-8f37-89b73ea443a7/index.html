Google launches Language Explorer to showcase global linguistic diversity

Google has a long history of expanding language technologies, growing from supporting just two languages in Google Translate in 2006 to nearly 250 languages today. Building products that reflect the world's linguistic diversity requires grassroots initiatives like Project Vaani in India and Project Waxal in Africa, as well as partnerships with local communities and organizations like the Gates Foundation. The newly released Language Explorer builds upon the open-source LinguaMeta dataset, bringing together data across thousands of languages to help researchers, educators, and language enthusiasts understand global speech diversity. This tool serves as a centerpiece in the Real Talk! Language. Power. World. exhibition at the Museum of Communication in Berlin, running through February 2028, and will also be featured in Frankfurt through September 2029, highlighting Google's ongoing efforts to make AI and language technologies accessible to everyone.
