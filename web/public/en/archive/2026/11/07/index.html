Tech Stocks Plunge as AI Bubble Fears and Regulations Hit Wall Street

The Market Shock On Thursday, November 5, the tech-heavy Nasdaq plunged by 5.5%, marking its worst session since April 2025 and erasing more than $2.5 trillion in market capitalization in just 24 hours. This sudden drop followed record highs in October, driven by stratospheric valuations and an unshakeable enthusiasm for artificial intelligence. However, the Democratic Party's sweeping victory in the midterm elections on November 3 acted as a major catalyst, heightening fears of stricter government regulations on AI development and data center expansion. Physical Constraints and Profitability The market correction highlights a growing disconnect between financial speculation and physical reality. The massive electricity and water consumption required by AI data centers has sparked local opposition, with voters backing initiatives to restrict such facilities. Furthermore, the industry faces mounting questions regarding investment profitability, as astronomical funding relied on assumptions of effortless future revenues while infrastructure obstacles multiplied. Economic Implications Beyond Wall Street, the downturn poses broader economic risks. AI has recently become a core engine of U.S. economic growth, supported by hundreds of billions in investments and a stock-driven wealth effect. A lasting loss of confidence could trigger a vicious cycle of delayed projects and reduced activity, potentially turning AI from an economic driver into a vulnerability.

NYT Cooking’s AI assistant saved my weeknight chicken, but almost ruined my cake

The NYT Cooking app recently introduced an AI assistant trained on user comments and its extensive recipe database, designed to help users customize recipes on the fly. During a weeknight test, the author asked the AI to adapt a chicken and rice recipe based on a random assortment of fridge ingredients. The AI successfully generated a modified version with alternative ingredients and adjusted cooking instructions, ensuring the dish remained tasty. However, baking proved to be a trickier challenge. When the author converted a Bundt cake recipe into muffins and halved the ingredients, a subsequent mix-up between the original and modified versions in the app led to a near-mistake with the butter quantity. Despite these minor hiccups, the tool offers a streamlined alternative to reading hundreds of reader comments for substitution tips, functioning as a practical assistant while still requiring human oversight.
