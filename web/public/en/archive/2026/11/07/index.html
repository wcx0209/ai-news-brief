Tech Stocks Plunge as AI Bubble Fears and Regulations Hit Wall Street

The Market Shock On Thursday, November 5, the tech-heavy Nasdaq plunged by 5.5%, marking its worst session since April 2025 and erasing more than $2.5 trillion in market capitalization in just 24 hours. This sudden drop followed record highs in October, driven by stratospheric valuations and an unshakeable enthusiasm for artificial intelligence. However, the Democratic Party's sweeping victory in the midterm elections on November 3 acted as a major catalyst, heightening fears of stricter government regulations on AI development and data center expansion. Physical Constraints and Profitability The market correction highlights a growing disconnect between financial speculation and physical reality. The massive electricity and water consumption required by AI data centers has sparked local opposition, with voters backing initiatives to restrict such facilities. Furthermore, the industry faces mounting questions regarding investment profitability, as astronomical funding relied on assumptions of effortless future revenues while infrastructure obstacles multiplied. Economic Implications Beyond Wall Street, the downturn poses broader economic risks. AI has recently become a core engine of U.S. economic growth, supported by hundreds of billions in investments and a stock-driven wealth effect. A lasting loss of confidence could trigger a vicious cycle of delayed projects and reduced activity, potentially turning AI from an economic driver into a vulnerability.

Amazon SageMaker now supports Multi-Instance GPU to cut AI inference costs

Deploying AI models to GPUs has traditionally forced a rigid choice: either pay for a whole accelerator that a smaller model cannot fully utilize, or struggle with complex self-managed Kubernetes stacks. With native GPU partitioning on SageMaker AI, users can now slice accelerators like the A100 or H100 into independent partitions with hardware-enforced isolation. AWS tests show that running a model on a partitioned newer GPU can actually be faster and cheaper than using a whole older GPU. For example, a partitioned H100 slice outperformed a whole A100 while significantly lowering the cost per million tokens. This capability makes it much easier to run a portfolio of models on the same hardware without performance interference.

How NVIDIA Open Source Tools Are Helping Researchers Build Next-Generation 6G Networks

Over the past year, NVIDIA's open source 5G and 6G tools, such as Aerial and Sionna, have surpassed one million downloads and are used by over 8,000 research and academic institutions globally. These tools connect simulation and AI training with real-world wireless experiments, helping researchers tackle key challenges in future networks. At King's College London, researchers used NVIDIA Sionna to develop goal-oriented semantic communications for robots. Instead of sending full images, robots transmit compact object descriptions, reducing task completion time by up to 52.6% in dynamic environments. Meanwhile, SoftBank and ETH Zurich demonstrated that AI-powered neural receivers can significantly improve signal reception and throughput under interference. Energy management and simulation speed are also major areas of improvement. Researchers at the University of Leeds used a portable 5G setup powered by NVIDIA to predict network demand, cutting base station power consumption by 45.6%. Additionally, the Centre Tecnològic de Telecomunicacions de Catalunya used GPU acceleration to reduce network simulation runtimes from hours to minutes, speeding up the path from innovation to real-world deployment.

v0.66.0 - Qualcomm AI Hub Models Updates with New Models and Breaking Changes

New Models Integration The latest release introduces several notable models, including agpose for pose estimation, netvlad for place recognition, and parakeet_tdt. Additionally, it integrates lfm3_1_7b and lfm3_vl_2b via GenieX llama.cpp, broadening the range of available architectures for developers. Breaking Changes and CLI Updates A significant breaking change removes the old deprecation warning when running python export commands. Now, executing that directly will trigger a ModuleNotFoundError, requiring users to switch to the qai-hub-models export command instead. Optimizations and Quantization Various improvements include Phi-4 mini onboarding, minilm_v2 shifting to w8a16 support, and Qwen3 models getting requantized with aimet-onnx 2.35.0. Furthermore, configuration files like perf.yaml can now be parsed using standard protobuf libraries.

The Verge AICompany23:50 SGTImportance 33

NYT Cooking’s AI assistant saved my weeknight chicken, but almost ruined my cake

The NYT Cooking app recently introduced an AI assistant trained on user comments and its extensive recipe database, designed to help users customize recipes on the fly. During a weeknight test, the author asked the AI to adapt a chicken and rice recipe based on a random assortment of fridge ingredients. The AI successfully generated a modified version with alternative ingredients and adjusted cooking instructions, ensuring the dish remained tasty. However, baking proved to be a trickier challenge. When the author converted a Bundt cake recipe into muffins and halved the ingredients, a subsequent mix-up between the original and modified versions in the app led to a near-mistake with the butter quantity. Despite these minor hiccups, the tool offers a streamlined alternative to reading hundreds of reader comments for substitution tips, functioning as a practical assistant while still requiring human oversight.
