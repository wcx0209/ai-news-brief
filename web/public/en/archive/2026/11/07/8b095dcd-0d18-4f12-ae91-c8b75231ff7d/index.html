Tech Stocks Plunge as AI Bubble Fears and Regulations Hit Wall Street

Le Monde AIArtificial IntelligenceBusinessSource published2026-11-07 19:45 SGT
Media reportStage not established

The Market Shock

On Thursday, November 5, the tech-heavy Nasdaq plunged by 5.5%, marking its worst session since April 2025 and erasing more than $2.5 trillion in market capitalization in just 24 hours. This sudden drop followed record highs in October, driven by stratospheric valuations and an unshakeable enthusiasm for artificial intelligence. However, the Democratic Party's sweeping victory in the midterm elections on November 3 acted as a major catalyst, heightening fears of stricter government regulations on AI development and data center expansion.

Physical Constraints and Profitability

The market correction highlights a growing disconnect between financial speculation and physical reality. The massive electricity and water consumption required by AI data centers has sparked local opposition, with voters backing initiatives to restrict such facilities. Furthermore, the industry faces mounting questions regarding investment profitability, as astronomical funding relied on assumptions of effortless future revenues while infrastructure obstacles multiplied.

Economic Implications

Beyond Wall Street, the downturn poses broader economic risks. AI has recently become a core engine of U.S. economic growth, supported by hundreds of billions in investments and a stock-driven wealth effect. A lasting loss of confidence could trigger a vicious cycle of delayed projects and reduced activity, potentially turning AI from an economic driver into a vulnerability.
