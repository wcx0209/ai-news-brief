MIT's new platform helps robots understand hidden meanings in human commands

According to MIT Robotics: MIT engineers have developed FLEX, a new AI platform that allows robots to interpret human natural language commands beyond literal meanings.

MIT Robotics ↗Source published 2026-11-09 13:00 SGTOfficial releaseRobotics & Autonomous Systems

According to MIT Robotics:

Background

Humans naturally grasp intuitive meanings and unstated intentions behind words, but robots typically require precise, step-by-step mathematical instructions. To bridge this gap, researchers at MIT and New York University developed FLEX, a flexible feature-editing pipeline designed to help robots interpret natural language more like humans do. Details FLEX operates through a three-step process: Add, Generalize, and Mask. First, the robot adds specific objects mentioned by the user to its avoidance list. Second, it uses a large language model, specifically GPT-4o, to extract broader patterns from internet training data, deducing general rules such as avoiding fragile items or electronics without requiring explicit commands for every single item. Finally, it uses computer vision to update the environment and remove irrelevant items. Impact This technology reduces the heavy communication burden on human users, making household and assistive robots significantly easier to operate. Instead of meticulously programming safety rules, users can rely on natural conversations, paving the way for robots that adapt seamlessly to diverse home environments.
