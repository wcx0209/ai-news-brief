AI Is Ruining My Computer Science Degree

QbitAI 量子位Artificial IntelligenceSocietySource published2026-11-09 16:57 SGT
Media reportStage not established

A computer science student named Katie sparked a massive discussion on Hacker News after publishing a blog post about the chaotic state of higher education in the age of generative AI. According to Katie, universities are constantly shifting their policies, swinging wildly between forcing students to use AI and threatening them with academic dishonesty penalties. Even worse, professors are relying on tools like Claude to generate convoluted syllabi and massive assignments without proper review, drastically increasing workloads while cutting human support like teaching assistants to maximize administrative profits.

The tech community remains deeply divided over this issue. Seasoned engineers view AI as a powerful learning catalyst that supercharges mentorship and efficiency, suggesting that top-tier young developers thrive by mastering fundamentals. However, recent graduates and students feel suffocated, facing a grim job market where entry-level roles are increasingly replaced by coding agents. Despite the industry's utilitarian shift, Katie's story highlights a reclaiming of the raw joy of programming through building simple, personal projects away from corporate productivity metrics.
