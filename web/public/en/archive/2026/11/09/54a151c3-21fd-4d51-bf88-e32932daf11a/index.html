Data Leaks Spark Fears of Highly Targeted Phishing Scams

Following a wave of recent cyberattacks on businesses, massive amounts of user personal information have leaked. Security organizations and firms like Check Point Technologies and Tobira Systems are warning that this data could drastically change the landscape of phishing attacks. Although a direct link to the recent leaks is not yet proven, phishing reports are trending upward. Experts explain that instead of traditional, generic blast emails, attackers can now combine various leaked data points to build detailed user profiles. This enables them to craft highly accurate, believable scams, such as fake payment failure notices sent to people who might actually be facing such issues. Additionally, malicious actors are beginning to use clever vectors like calendar invitation files to trick corporate employees. Security firms urge individuals and companies to use multi-factor authentication, avoid clicking links in emails directly, and stay extra vigilant against unexpected calendar invites or urgent IT support requests.
