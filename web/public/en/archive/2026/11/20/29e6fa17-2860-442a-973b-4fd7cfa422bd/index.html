Alsym Energy says US battery policy needs to focus more on upstream sodium

According to Alsym Energy COO Graeme Grant, current US battery tax credits are too focused on assembly rather than upstream materials, leaving the supply chain vulnerable. Grant states that sodium-ion technology gives the US a rare geographical advantage because Wyoming holds massive natural sodium carbonate deposits, offering a chance to bypass foreign dependencies.

Energy-Storage.news ↗Source published 2026-11-20 20:53 SGTMedia reportEnergy & Storage

At the RE+ event in Las Vegas, Alsym Energy COO Graeme Grant argued that current US battery policies have missed the mark by heavily subsidizing final cell assembly while ignoring upstream raw materials. According to Grant, battery manufacturing credits can reach about US$35 per kilowatt-hour, sometimes exceeding the actual sale price of a cell, whereas upstream input credits cover only around 10% of costs.

Grant noted that this policy imbalance has already limited the effectiveness of domestic lithium iron phosphate (LFP) efforts, as critical components like graphite and LFP materials still largely originate overseas. To avoid repeating past mistakes—such as when early US battery innovations were commercialized domestically only to be later acquired abroad—Grant believes the US should lean into sodium-ion technology. He highlighted that Wyoming holds 85% of the world's known natural sodium carbonate deposits in the form of trona, which is already mined industrially and offers a ready-made path to build a secure, independent supply chain.
