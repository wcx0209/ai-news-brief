Google's Quantum Chip Beats Classical Limits in Tests of Teamwork

Google Quantum AI researchers used their Willow processor to show that quantum contextuality can outperform classical strategies in cooperative games. The chip won a four-qubit game 98.3% of the time, beating the classical limit, and successfully scaled past classical limits with up to 71 qubits, offering a new method to detect hardware errors.

The Quantum Insider ↗Source published 2026-11-20 04:31 SGTMedia reportQuantum Technology

Cooperative Games and Quantum Contextuality Researchers at Google Quantum AI utilized the Willow processor to investigate quantum contextuality, a property where measurement outcomes depend on accompanying measurements. In a magic square game preventing player communication, four qubits achieved a 98.3% winning rate, surpassing the 88.9% classical limit. Scaling Up to 71 Qubits The team scaled the experiments using a Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger state. The winning rate stayed above the classical ceiling through 71 players, although the performance margin shrank as more qubits were introduced due to experimental noise. Mathematical Problem and Error Detection In a final test involving a two-dimensional hidden linear function, a 105-qubit experiment returned valid answers more than half the time. The researchers note that these tests can help spot subtle hardware errors that conventional checks might miss.
