Historical brief · 2026-11-20

Freiburg team secures about 10 million euros to explore commercial plastic volumetric 3D printing

According to reports by Nanjixiong, researchers Bastian Rapp and Jürgen Rühe from the University of Freiburg have received about 10 million euros in European Research Council synergy funding for the CHiC2PRINT project. Over six years, the team will explore volumetric 3D printing using standard industrial plastics while integrating material recycling into the process design.

Background and Funding

According to Nanjixiong, process technology professor Bastian Rapp and interface chemistry and physics professor Jürgen Rühe from the University of Freiburg have secured about 10 million euros in European Research Council (ERC) synergy funding. The funding will support the six-year CHiC2PRINT project, selected from over 950 applications.

Technical Approach

The project aims to bridge the gap between commercially mature plastics and light-driven additive manufacturing. Instead of traditional layer-by-layer stacking, the team plans to use directed light exposure to form three-dimensional structures inside an existing volume of plastic. By combining process technology and interface chemistry, they aim to make standard plastics responsive to light control.

Recycling and Outlook

CHiC2PRINT also incorporates product end-of-life handling into its design, aiming to separate formed objects through a process called hierarchical dissociation to recover and reuse raw plastics. The project intends to investigate whether commercial plastic systems can combine complex geometry manufacturing with efficient recycling, though specific recycling rates and production speeds are yet to be established.
