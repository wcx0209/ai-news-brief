Australia's PM faces a political storm over AI copyright and the creative sector

Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese is facing a tough political dilemma as he weighs whether to attract massive AI datacentres or protect local artists. Tech companies want Australia to relax copyright laws to build their data hubs, but creators are furious that AI models have been trained on stolen property.

The Guardian AI ↗Source published 2026-11-11 06:00 SGTMedia reportArtificial Intelligence

The Australian government is being courted by AI hyperscalers like Anthropic, which wants to base training models and renewable datacentres in the country, potentially creating a lucrative new green industry. However, this economic vision comes with a catch: tech companies are pushing the government to relax copyright laws that currently protect artistic work from being scraped without consent. Critics argue this would turn Australia into a global haven for data-washing.

Beyond intellectual property, the rise of AI is triggering broader societal concerns, ranging from the automated mimicry of human art to the erosion of educational cognitive skills and mounting pressures on energy and water resources. The creative sector, traditionally a loyal political ally of the Labor party, is fiercely pushing back against what they see as a sell-out, demanding proper control, compensation, and the active promotion of human work.
