AI Datacentres Plan Massive On-Site Gas Power Stations Despite Climate Risks

Background As the artificial intelligence boom drives up electricity demand, data centre developers face severe delays in connecting to national power grids. To bypass these bottlenecks and achieve faster power delivery, companies in the US, the UK, and mainland Europe are turning to fossil fuels. Key Details According to research by BloombergNEF, planned data centres across these regions propose building on-site gas-fired capacity totaling 181 gigawatts. About 90% of these projects are planned in the US, where they could emit roughly 345 million metric tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent annually, matching the emissions of about 72.8 million cars. By 2035, global AI data centre electricity demand is predicted to nearly quadruple to 2,354 terawatt-hours, accounting for 7% of global demand. Impact While developers claim these gas generators are temporary measures while waiting for grid access, experts warn that economic incentives once they are online could keep them running for years. In the UK, the government has even greenlit temporary on-site fossil fuel generation for fast-tracked data centres, sparking strong criticism from environmental campaigners over long-term climate commitments.
