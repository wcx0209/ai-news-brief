Nvidia board investigates CEO Jensen Huang over affair and China spy links

Nvidia’s stock dropped nearly 10% in two days after reports that CEO Jensen Huang had an extramarital affair with a woman suspected by U.S. intelligence of spying for China. The company's board has hired an outside law firm to investigate, a rare crisis that corporate governance experts say lacks clear precedent, while investors have already filed a class action lawsuit.

TechCrunch AI ↗Source published 2026-11-11 15:30 SGTMedia reportArtificial Intelligence

Nvidia is facing a severe corporate governance crisis after it was revealed that CEO Jensen Huang had an extramarital affair with Alice Huang, a woman U.S. intelligence believes was spying for China. Following the news, Nvidia's stock sank about 9.5% over two days. The Investigation and Legal Hurdles The board has hired the law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to conduct an internal investigation. Legal experts note that the inquiry will focus on whether laws were broken, internal policies were violated, and whether the board was misled. Meanwhile, Nvidia is already facing a proposed class action lawsuit alleging that executives concealed the relationship while describing business dealings with the woman's former employer as routine. Experts point out that while affairs are common, the intersection with potential national security compromises is uncharted territory. Governance and Succession Concerns The scandal has also drawn scrutiny to Nvidia's internal management, including its famously flat organizational structure and the employment of Huang's children within the company. Experts criticized the practice of hiring family members at a publicly traded tech giant, though they noted that Huang does not hold dual-class shares, meaning the board retains more leverage to act compared to companies with founder-controlled voting structures.
