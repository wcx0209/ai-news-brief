Brain-computer interface lets paralyzed patients control smart home devices using silent speech

Nature Brain-Machine InterfacesNeurotechnology & Human InterfacesResearchSource published2026-11-11 08:00 SGT
Research paperStage not established

Background

Brain-computer interfaces hold great promise for restoring independence to individuals with paralysis, enabling them to interact with their environment and operate smart devices. However, previous BCI systems for device control often relied on external visual displays or audio cues, which restricted the user's ability to issue commands autonomously and privately in everyday settings.

Details

In this recent study, a clinical trial participant living with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis used a chronically implanted electrocorticographic brain-computer interface. Over the course of 18 experimental sessions, the system detected silently mimed speech commands in real time. The technology successfully decoded these internal signals with a median accuracy of 97.1 percent, achieving nearly 12 correct decodes per minute without relying on any exogenous timing cues.

Impact

These findings represent a major step forward for assistive technology, demonstrating that silently attempted speech can be translated reliably into actionable commands. This capability significantly improves both the privacy and autonomy of people with severe motor impairments, bringing practical, hands-free smart home control one step closer to reality.
