Microsoft launches 'Autopilot' AI agent, pitches human control to win enterprise trust

Just days after a major global outage of its business applications, Microsoft is rolling out an autonomous AI worker called Autopilot to select early-access customers. To win over cautious enterprises, the tech giant is pitching human control, promising that managers will set strict limits on what the AI can do.

Mint AI ↗Source published 2026-11-18 07:30 SGTMedia reportArtificial Intelligence

Microsoft has officially released its new Autopilot AI agent to early-access frontier customers across select countries, including India. The launch comes closely on the heels of a significant global disruption to Microsoft 365 business applications on November 13-14, which affected customer support services worldwide for companies like IndiGo and Akasa Air.

To address enterprise fears about fully automated systems going rogue or failing, Microsoft is emphasizing a human-in-the-control-loop approach. The core selling point of Autopilot is that human managers retain the power to set the exact boundaries and operational limits for the AI agent, ensuring that artificial intelligence works under strict supervision rather than acting entirely independently.
