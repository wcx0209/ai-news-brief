Quantum simulators reveal hidden non-equilibrium phases and phase transitions in Nature study

According to AWS Quantum Technologies: Researchers from Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory and QuEra used Amazon Braket to study interacting quantum particles pushed far from equilibrium.

AWS Quantum Technologies ↗Source published 2026-11-18 02:13 SGTOfficial releaseQuantum Technology

According to AWS Quantum Technologies:

In a recent _Nature_ study, a collaboration between NERSC, QuEra Computing, Harvard, and Boston University used QuEra's Aquila quantum processor via Amazon Braket alongside NERSC's Perlmutter supercomputer to explore quantum particles pushed far from equilibrium. When the system is rapidly disturbed, it typically re-equilibrates to thermal physics. However, the team observed stable dynamical behaviors that escape ordinary thermalization.

By adjusting atom spacing, geometry, and control fields across arrays of up to 180 atoms, the researchers mapped out prethermal regimes in one and two dimensions. They found that strong interactions suppress simultaneous excitations, protecting the system in a long-lived prethermal state before it eventually thermalizes. Furthermore, specific energy resonances cause these states to 'melt' through identifiable pathways. In two-dimensional lattices, as system sizes scaled up, a sharp boundary emerged that satisfied a generalized Clausius-Clapeyron relation, providing quantitative evidence of a first-order dynamical phase transition occurring outside of equilibrium.
