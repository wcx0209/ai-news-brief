Pfizer Enters In Vivo CAR-T Space With $2.6B Deal For Starna's Autoimmune Programs

Pfizer has partnered with China-based Starna Therapeutics in a deal worth up to $2.6 billion to acquire ex-China rights for three preclinical in vivo CAR-T programs targeting autoimmune diseases. This move marks Pfizer's first step into the emerging in vivo CAR-T space, combining big pharma investment with advanced mRNA delivery technology.

Fierce Biotech ↗Source published 2026-11-18 21:30 SGTMedia reportBiotech & Programmable Life

Deal Structure and Scope According to Pfizer's announcement, the pharma giant has paid an upfront fee of $330 million to secure global rights outside Greater China for three preclinical in vivo CAR-T programs developed by Jiangsu-based Starna Therapeutics. The total agreement is worth up to $2.3 billion in milestone payments plus royalties, with Pfizer leading clinical development outside China and retaining an option to acquire Greater China rights later. Scientific Background and Industry Context The collaboration focuses on treating autoimmune diseases using in vivo CAR-T therapies, a hot trend in the pharmaceutical industry following similar moves by companies like Eli Lilly. Starna, founded in 2021, relies on its proprietary targeted LNP delivery platform and RNA technology. Pfizer plans to manufacture the finished drug products for early clinical trials at its U.S. facilities, leveraging its existing RNA manufacturing expertise.
