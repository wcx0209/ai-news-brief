Nvidia faces short-seller scrutiny over former executive's ties to China

Muddy Waters and other short sellers are scrutinizing Nvidia over its business in China, pointing to potential ties between a former top executive and a server maker accused of illegal chip shipments. Even so, Nvidia recently reported strong quarterly revenue and raised its future growth outlook, while some short sellers unwound their positions.

SCMP China Tech ↗Source published 2026-11-18 21:30 SGTMedia reportArtificial Intelligence

Short-seller firm Muddy Waters published a report questioning whether a former high-ranking Nvidia executive has links to Cenly Chen, a former officer at server maker Supermicro who was named in a shareholder complaint regarding unproven allegations of illicit GPU sales to China. Muddy Waters acknowledged that these civil allegations remain unproven and that Chen has not faced criminal charges. Supermicro pushed back against the claims, stating Chen did not manage its relationship with Nvidia or chip distribution.

This adds to mounting bearish pressure on Nvidia, following similar concerns raised by Michael Burry regarding geographic revenue reporting and a separate report by Culper Research concerning a Singapore-based customer, Megaspeed. US authorities recently confirmed a criminal probe into Megaspeed over potential export-control violations. Despite these headwinds and recent leadership turmoil involving co-founder Jensen Huang, Nvidia reported quarterly revenue of US$115.8 billion, a 103 per cent increase year-on-year, and raised its fiscal 2028 growth outlook.
