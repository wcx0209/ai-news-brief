Kymera’s oral eczema drug impresses analysts with Dupixent-like phase 2 efficacy

Kymera Therapeutics reports that its oral eczema drug KT-621 met the primary endpoint in a phase 2b clinical trial, showing efficacy comparable to or exceeding injected blockbusters like Dupixent. The mid-dose cohort achieved a 72% drop in skin severity scores, positioning the pill as a strong potential alternative to current biologic treatments.

Fierce Biotech ↗Source published 2026-11-23 22:50 SGTMedia reportBiotech & Programmable Life

Kymera Therapeutics evaluated 244 adults and adolescents with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis in a phase 2b trial, randomizing them to receive one of three doses of the STAT6 degrader KT-621 or a placebo once daily for 16 weeks. According to the company, the mid-dose group achieved a 72% mean drop in Eczema Area and Severity Index scores compared to 27% for the placebo.

Financial analysts noted that the placebo-adjusted EASI-75 response of 40% cleared expectations for investor enthusiasm. Furthermore, the drug demonstrated strong itch relief, with a 58% reduction on the Peak Pruritus Numerical Rating Scale. Kymera plans to initiate a phase 3 trial by mid-2027 and explore potential expansions into other indications.
