Historical brief · 2026-11-23

Microsoft and US officials warn that more major tech outages are coming

Following recent disruptions including an FAA ground stop and a multi-day Microsoft Outlook outage, US intelligence and Microsoft leadership warn that more system failures are likely ahead. Officials cite the massive task of upgrading global infrastructure and the double-edged sword of AI, which helps both hackers find vulnerabilities and developers patch code.

The Verge AI ↗Source published 2026-11-23 06:48 SGTMedia reportArtificial Intelligence

Background and Recent Incidents

Over the preceding weeks, a series of high-profile disruptions severely impacted critical services, including a nationwide FAA ground stop, a multi-day outage affecting Microsoft Outlook that brought entire companies to a standstill, and a major banking disruption at JPMorgan Chase.

Official Warnings

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton stated that the public should expect bumps in the road during major technology transitions, noting that substantial investments are flowing into system upgrades and cybersecurity. Concurrently, Microsoft President Brad Smith offered a more sobering assessment, explaining to media outlets that the world's information infrastructure is currently undergoing a massive global upgrade that cannot be resolved in just a couple of weeks.

The Role of AI and Vulnerabilities

According to Microsoft, artificial intelligence has lowered the barrier for discovering code flaws. Smith noted that this technological capability allows bad actors to exploit weaknesses just as easily as developers can use the knowledge to patch software. Coupled with recent cyberattacks targeting government and commercial entities like the USDA and JPMorgan, these factors point toward continued instability in the coming months.
