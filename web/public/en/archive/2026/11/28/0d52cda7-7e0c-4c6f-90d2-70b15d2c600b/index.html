Quantum Computing 101: Explaining Qubits, Superposition, and Why They Need Deep Cold

Quantum computers use qubits that exist in superposition, meaning they can represent both 0 and 1 at the same time until measured. These fragile states require temperatures colder than outer space inside dilution refrigerators to prevent disruption, and while they will not replace ordinary laptops, they could transform molecular simulation and break standard encryption.

The Quantum Insider ↗Source published 2026-11-28 00:51 SGTMedia reportQuantum Technology

The article breaks down the fundamentals of quantum computing for a general audience, outlining core concepts such as superposition and entanglement. According to the text, superconducting qubits developed at institutions like ETH Zurich must be cooled to roughly 10 thousandths of a degree above absolute zero to prevent decoherence caused by external heat. Furthermore, the report clarifies that quantum computers are not designed to accelerate everyday tasks like web browsing or spreadsheets; instead, their potential lies in simulating complex molecules for drug and materials discovery, as well as breaking public-key encryption, which is prompting organizations to adopt post-quantum cryptography.
