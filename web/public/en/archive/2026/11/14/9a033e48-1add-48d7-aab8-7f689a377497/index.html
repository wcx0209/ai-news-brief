Researchers Find a Better Way to Design Long-Lasting Sodium Battery Cathodes

Researchers from Nankai University and other institutions report a quantitative descriptor based on ionic field strength to design better O3-type cathodes for sodium-ion batteries.

AZoM Materials News ↗Source published 2026-11-14 18:47 SGTMedia reportAdvanced Materials & Nanotechnology
Energy & Storage

Research Background O3-type layered oxides are promising candidates for sodium-ion battery cathodes because they offer high capacity and initial sodium content. However, operating them at high voltages causes deep sodium extraction, which weakens electrostatic screening and triggers harmful phase transitions and lattice degradation. Traditional dopant selection has relied mostly on average ionic radii or valence states, which fail to capture local bonding heterogeneity. Method and Findings Published in JACS on October 20, 2026, by a team including Nankai University and the Chinese Academy of Sciences, the study introduces an ionic field strength descriptor to quantify local disparities among transition metal cations. By comparing low- and high-disparity materials, the researchers demonstrated that the low-disparity cathode follows a more reversible phase evolution path, avoids extra high-voltage phase changes, and exhibits smaller lattice parameter shifts during desodiation. Synchrotron X-ray and strain mapping further confirmed reduced local coordination disorder and suppressed strain accumulation. Practical Implications This approach transforms dopant selection from an empirical search into a quantitative design task. While the framework shows promise for accelerating cathode development in stationary storage and high-voltage sodium-ion batteries, the authors note that it still requires validation across broader compositions and practical full-cell conditions before commercial deployment.
